>[!note] Cascade of Multiple Systems
>In **signals and systems**, a **cascade** refers to the serial connection of two or more systems, where the output of one system serves as the input to the next system. For example, a cascade $H$ of two systems $F$ and $G$ can be graphically represented as
>```tikz
>\begin{document}
>\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=1.3]
>
>% Input
>\draw[->, thick] (-1,0) -- (0,0) node[at start, left]{$x(t)$};
>
>% First block
>\draw[draw, rectangle, minimum width=1.5cm, minimum height=1cm, thick] (0, -0.5) rectangle (1.5, 0.5);
>\node at (0.75, 0) {$F$};
>
>% Arrow between blocks
>\draw[thick] (1.5,0) -- (2,0);
>
>% Second block
>\draw[draw, rectangle, minimum width=1.5cm, minimum height=1cm, thick] (2, -0.5) rectangle (3.5, 0.5);
>\node at (2.75, 0) {$G$};
>
>% Output
>\draw[->, thick] (3.5,0) -- (4.5,0) node[at end, right]{$y(t)$};
>
>% H
>\draw[thick, dashed] (-0.5, -1) -- (4, -1) -- (4, 1) -- (-0.5, 1) -- cycle;
>\node[thick, scale=1.3] at (-0.3, -0.8) {H};
>\end{tikzpicture}
>\end{document}
>```
>For [[2.2a LTI System|LTI systems]], the impulse response of a cascade is given as
>$$h(t) = (f\textasteriskcentered g)(t),$$
>whereas the cascade's frequency response is given as
>$$H(\omega) = F(\omega)G(\omega).$$

>[!example] (Discrete-Time) Cascade
>Consider a system $F$, governed by the LCCDE
>$$y(n)-y(n-1) = x(n) - x(n-3).$$
>Suppose we cascade $F$ with a new system $G$ ($F$ comes before $G$ as in the figure above), governed by the LCCDE
>$$y(n)-y(n-3) = x(n) - x(n-1).$$
>By plugging in the eigenfunction input $x(n)=e^{j\omega n}$, we find that the frequency responses of $F$ and $G$ are
>$$F(\omega) = \frac{1 - e^{-j3\omega}}{1-e^{-j\omega}} \enspace \text{and} \enspace G(\omega) = \frac{1-e^{-j\omega}}{1 - e^{-j3\omega}}.$$
>We see here that $F(\omega)$ is undefined for $\omega = 2k\pi$, and $G(\omega)$ is undefined for $\omega = \frac{2k\pi}{3}$. We also know by definition that $H(\omega) = F(\omega)G(\omega)$. Since $F$ comes before $G$, any $\omega_{0}$ such that $F(\omega_{0})=0$ will lead to $H(\omega)=0$. Therefore, we can say that
>$$H(\omega) = 
>\begin{cases}
>\text{Undefined} &\text{for } \omega = 2k\pi \\
>0 &\text{for } \omega = \frac{2k\pi}{3} \cap k \pmod{3} \not\equiv 0 \\
>1 &\text{otherwise}
\end{cases},$$
>where $k \pmod{3} \not\equiv 0$ becomes a condition since $H(\omega)$ is still undefined for $2k\pi$.
