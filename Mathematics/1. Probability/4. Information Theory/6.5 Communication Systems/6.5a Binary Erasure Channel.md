>[!note] Binary Erasure Channel (BEC)
>The **Binary Erasure Channel (BEC)** is a discrete channel with an **input alphabet** $\mathcal{X}=\{ 0,1 \}$ and an **output alphabet** $\mathcal{Y}=\{ 0,1,e \}$, where $e$ represents an **erasure symbol**, indicating that the transmitted bit was lost with probability $p \in [0,1]$:
>```tikz
>\begin{document}
>\begin{tikzpicture}[every node/.style={font=\Large}]
>  % Draw Input nodes
>  \draw[->, thick] (0,3) -- (3,3) node[left, at start]{$0$} node[right]{$0$} node[above, midway]{$1-p$};
>  \draw[->, thick] (0,2.9) -- (3, 1.55) node[midway, below]{$p$};
>  \draw[->, thick] (0,0.1) -- (3, 1.45) node[midway, above]{$p$};
>  \draw[->, thick] (0, 0) -- (3, 0) node[left, at start]{$1$} node[right]{$1$} node[below, midway]{$1-p$};
>  \node at (3.3, 1.5) {$e$};
>\end{tikzpicture}
>\end{document}
>```
>Let $L$ be the length of a message, encoded to a message of length $n$. Then, the the number of bits of information about the source message per symbol that the receiver receives can be thought of as the **rate** $R=L/n$ of the channel. 
>
>Accompanying any code is an encoding function $f_n : \mathcal{X}^L \to \mathcal{X}^n$ and a decoding function $g_{n} : \mathcal{Y}^{n} →\mathcal{X}^L$. Then, the **maximum probability of erasure** is given as the worst-case probability–taken over all messages–that the decoder fails to recover the original message:
>$$P_{e}(n)=\max_{x \in \mathcal{X}^L}\mathbb{P}(g(Y^n) \neq x^n \mid X^n = f_{n}(x^n)).$$
>
