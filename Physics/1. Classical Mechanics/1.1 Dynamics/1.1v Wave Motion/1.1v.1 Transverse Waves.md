>[!note] Transverse Wave
>A **transverse wave** is a wave in which particles of the medium move perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation (e.g. light waves, water waves). A series of crests and troughs travel along a transverse wave:
>1. The **crests** of a transverse wave represent the ***positive*** peaks of its oscillation.
>2. The **troughs** of a transverse wave represent the ***negative*** peaks of its oscillation.
>
>A transverse wave's vertical displacement at distance $x$ and time $t$ can be modeled as a function, 
>$$D(x, t)=A\sin(kx \pm  \omega_{n} t + \phi),$$
>where $k=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}$ is the wave's wavenumber ($\lambda$ is the wave's wavelength), $\omega_{n}=\frac{2\pi}{T}$ is the wave's natural frequency ($T$ is its period), and $A$ is its peak $y$-axis displacement (amplitude). The $\pm$ sign is negative as the wave is moving towards the ***right*** of the x-axis.
>
>Furthermore, the speed of a transverse wave is given as
>$$v = \sqrt{\frac{F_{T}}{\mu}} = \lambda f,$$
>where $F_{T}$ is the tension on the cord, and $\mu = \frac{m}{\ell}$ is the mass per unit length of the cord, whereas $\lambda$ and $f$ are the sinusoidal wavelength and frequency respectively.
