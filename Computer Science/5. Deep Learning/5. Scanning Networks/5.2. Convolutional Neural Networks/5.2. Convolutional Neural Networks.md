>[!note] Convolutional Neural Networks
>This section covers networks built from convolutions, the standard architecture for images and other grid-shaped data. The [[5.2.1. Convolutional Layer|convolutional layer]] slides one small kernel over the input, which builds in locality and weight sharing. Its note covers padding, stride and dilation. Three companion notes in the same folder cover the [[5.2.1a. Receptive Field|receptive field]], [[5.2.1b. Equivariance and Invariance|equivariance and invariance]], and the [[5.2.1c. 1x1 and Depthwise Convolution|$1 \times 1$ and depthwise convolutions]]. [[5.2.2. Pooling]] shrinks feature maps and makes the output partly invariant to small shifts. [[5.2.3. Upsampling]] grows them back for networks whose output is an image, most flexibly with the transposed convolution.
>
>[[5.2.4. CNNs]] assembles these layers into classifiers such as AlexNet and VGG and into encoder-decoder networks, and explains the inductive bias that makes them work. [[5.2.5. Residual Networks]] adds skip connections, which made networks with hundreds of layers trainable. The [[6.4. U-Net|U-Net]], an encoder-decoder with skips between matching resolutions, lives in the Specialized Architectures section.

# Contents

- [[5.2.1. Convolutional Layer]]: the 1D and 2D operation, cross-correlation versus convolution, channels and parameter counts, padding, stride and dilation, the output-size formula, the banded weight matrix, and gradients of shared weights. **Not taught:** im2col.
    - [[5.2.1a. Receptive Field]]: the growth rule, downsampling and dilation, stacked small kernels, and two linear layers as one. **Not taught:** the effective receptive field.
    - [[5.2.1b. Equivariance and Invariance]]: the definitions, which parts of a CNN keep or break equivariance, composition, borders and strides. **Not taught:** max-blur-pooling, and the converse that shift-equivariant linear layers are convolutions.
    - [[5.2.1c. 1x1 and Depthwise Convolution]]: pointwise and depthwise layers, their weight counts, and bottleneck blocks. **Not taught:** grouped and depthwise separable convolutions.
- [[5.2.2. Pooling]]: subsampling, max pooling and average pooling, global average pooling, gradient routing, partial invariance, and the upward bias of max pooling under noise.
- [[5.2.3. Upsampling]]: duplication, max unpooling and the transposed convolution, with its output-size rule, matrix view and zero-insertion view. **Not taught:** bilinear upsampling layers and checkerboard artifacts.
- [[5.2.4. CNNs]]: design principles, the standard classification pipeline, AlexNet, VGG, encoder-decoder networks, and the inductive bias of convolution.
- [[5.2.5. Residual Networks]]: residual blocks, shattered gradients, the ensemble-of-paths view, variance growth and its fixes, projection shortcuts, bottleneck blocks, and ResNet-200.

# Recommended Path

Read [[5.2.1. Convolutional Layer]] first, since every later note uses its formulas. Its three companion notes are short and can be read in any order. [[5.2.4. CNNs]] then shows how the pieces fit together. [[5.2.5. Residual Networks]] explains how modern CNNs reach their depth.
