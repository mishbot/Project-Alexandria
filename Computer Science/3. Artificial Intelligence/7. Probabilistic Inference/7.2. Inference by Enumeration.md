>[!note] Inference by Enumeration
>**Inference by Enumeration** is the most direct method for computing posterior probabilities in any probabilistic model. It applies to **all models that define a full joint distribution** over variables, including **Bayes Nets**, **Markov Models**, and **Hidden Markov Models**,  but is computationally expensive for large or structured systems.
>
>The goal is to compute the posterior
>$$P(Q \mid e) = \alpha P(Q, e),$$
> where $Q$ is the query variable, $e$ is observed evidence, and $\alpha = \frac{1}{P(e)}$ is a normalization constant.
> 
>Using marginalization, the joint term expands as a sum over all **hidden variables** h:
>$$P(Q, e) = \sum_{h} P(Q, h, e).$$
> This operation (i.e., enumerating over all hidden variables and summing out their contributions) is what gives the method its name.
