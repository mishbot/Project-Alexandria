>[!note] Concentration Inequalities
>**Concentration inequalities** provide probabilistic bounds on how far a random variable is likely to be from some central value without requiring full knowledge of the distribution:
>- The **Markov inequality** provides a bound on the probability that a nonnegative random variable takes a value significantly larger than its mean. Let $X \ge 0$ and $\mathbb{E}[X] < \infty$. Then for any $c > 0$,
>$$P(X \ge c) \le \frac{\mathbb{E}[X]}{c}.$$
>
>- The **Chebyshev inequality** strengthens this by incorporating variance, yielding a bound on the probability that a random variable deviates from its mean from above or below by more than a specified amount. For any $X$ with $\mathbb{E}[X] < \infty$ and $\operatorname{Var}(X) < \infty$, it follows that
>$$P(|X - \mathbb{E}[X]| \ge c) \le \frac{\operatorname{Var}(X)}{c^2}.$$
>By applying Markov’s Inequality to the nonnegative random variable $(X-\mu)^2$, Chebyshev’s bound follows immediately.
>‎ 
>- Finally, the **Chernoff bound** provides an exponentially decreasing bound on the tail probabilities of a random variable using its [[Mathematics/2. Probability/2. Random Variables/2.3 Functions of Random Variables/2.5c Moment Generating Functions.md|moment generating function]]. For any $X$ with $M_X(t) = \mathbb{E}[e^{tX}] < \infty$, and for any $c \in \mathbb{R}$, it directly follows from the Markov inequality that
>$$\mathbb{P}(X \ge c) \le \inf_{t \ge 0} \left\{ \frac{M_{X}(t)}{e^{tc}} \right\}.$$

# Intuition
The proof for Markov's Inequality is derived directly from the definition of expectation:
$$\begin{align}
\mathbb{E}[X] &= \sum^{}_{x \in \mathcal{X}}x \cdot \mathbb{P}(X=x) \\
&\ge \sum^{}_{x \ge c}x \cdot \mathbb{P}(X=x) \\
&\ge \sum^{}_{x \ge c}c \cdot \mathbb{P}(X = x) \\
&= c \sum^{}_{x \ge c} \mathbb{P}(X = x) \\
&= c \cdot \mathbb{P}(X \ge c),
\end{align}$$
where the second line follows from the fact that $X$ is nonnegative.

As previously mentioned, Chebyshev's Inequality is a direct consequence of centering $X$ around its mean. Define $Y=(X-\mathbb{E}[X])^2$. Then,
$$\begin{align}

&\ge \frac{\mathbb{E}[(X-\mathbb{E}[X])^2]}{c^2} = \frac{\operatorname{Var}(X)}{c^2}.
\end{align}$$
