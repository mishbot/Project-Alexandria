>[!note] Capacitance
>This section builds a circuit element from the fields and potentials of the earlier sections. A [[3.5.1. Capacitance|capacitor]] is two conductors holding charges $\pm Q$. Its capacitance $C = Q/V$ depends only on the geometry and the material between the conductors. One recipe finds $C$ for any shape. Put $\pm Q$ on the conductors, find $\vec E$ with Gauss's law, integrate to get $V$, and divide. The recipe gives $\varepsilon_0 A/d$ for the [[3.5.1a Parallel-Plate Capacitor|parallel-plate capacitor]]. It gives $4\pi\varepsilon_0\,ab/(b-a)$ for the [[3.5.1b Spherical Capacitor|spherical capacitor]] and $2\pi\varepsilon_0 L/\ln(b/a)$ for the [[3.5.1c Cylindrical Capacitor|cylindrical capacitor]].
>
>Capacitors combine into an [[3.5.2. Equivalent Capacitance|equivalent capacitance]]. Capacitances add in parallel, and their reciprocals add in series. The work done to charge a capacitor is [[3.5.3. Energy Stored in an Electric Field|stored in its field]], $U = Q^2/(2C) = \tfrac12 CV^2$, with energy density $\tfrac12\varepsilon_0E^2$. A [[3.5.4. Dielectrics|dielectric]] between the plates weakens the field by its dielectric constant $K$ and multiplies $C$ by $K$. Every insulator has a largest field, beyond which [[3.5.4a Dielectric Breakdown|breakdown]] produces a spark. A dielectric slab near charged plates is [[3.5.4b Force on a Dielectric|pulled into the gap]], with a force found from the stored energy.

# Contents

- [[3.5.1. Capacitance]]: circuit elements and $V_{AB}$ notation, charging by a supply, $Q = CV$, the farad, uses, the recipe for $C$, and $C$ from a field measurement. **Not taught:** nothing.
    - [[3.5.1a Parallel-Plate Capacitor]]: $C = \varepsilon_0 A/d$ from $E = \sigma/\varepsilon_0$ and $V = Ed$, moving plates at fixed charge or fixed potential difference, and a metal sheet in the gap. **Not taught:** nothing.
    - [[3.5.1b Spherical Capacitor]]: $C = 4\pi\varepsilon_0\,ab/(b-a)$ with the signs worked out, a units check, and the field at the inner sphere. **Not taught:** the thin-gap and isolated-sphere limits.
    - [[3.5.1c Cylindrical Capacitor]]: $C = 2\pi\varepsilon_0 L/\ln(b/a)$ and the coaxial cable. **Not taught:** the whole note.
- [[3.5.2. Equivalent Capacitance]]: parallel and series rules, the zip-and-unzip method, voltage division in series, building a value from stock parts, and reconnecting charged capacitors. **Not taught:** nothing.
- [[3.5.3. Energy Stored in an Electric Field]]: $U = Q^2/(2C) = \tfrac12 CV^2 = \tfrac12 QV$, energy density, which form to use at fixed $Q$ or fixed $V$, and the discharge demonstration. **Not taught:** energy density for fields outside a capacitor, energy lost when capacitors share charge, and the single-sheet field.
- [[3.5.4. Dielectrics]]: induced dipoles, $E = E_0/K$, $C = K\varepsilon_0 A/d$, a table of $K$, fixed charge versus fixed potential difference, including the stored energy, and the electroscope and glycerin demonstrations. **Not taught:** the textbook values of $K$ for paper, mica, and water, the induced charge density, why water's $K$ is large, and a slab that fills part of the gap's thickness.
    - [[3.5.4a Dielectric Breakdown]]: dielectric strength, the electron avalanche, $V_{\max} = E_{\max}d$, the spark demonstrations, and blown capacitors. **Not taught:** numerical dielectric strengths, and breakdown near a charged sphere.
    - [[3.5.4b Force on a Dielectric]]: $C(x)$ for a partly filled capacitor, $F_x = -dU/dx$ at fixed charge, where the force is largest, and the oscillation. **Not taught:** why ignoring the edge field gives the right force, and the case with the supply connected.

# Recommended Path

Read [[3.5.1. Capacitance]] and [[3.5.1a Parallel-Plate Capacitor]] first, since every later note uses them. [[3.5.1b Spherical Capacitor]] is the main exercise in signs. [[3.5.1c Cylindrical Capacitor]] is optional. [[3.5.2. Equivalent Capacitance]] needs the most practice, mainly in spotting series and parallel pairs. Read [[3.5.3. Energy Stored in an Electric Field]] before the dielectric notes, since [[3.5.4b Force on a Dielectric]] differentiates the stored energy.
