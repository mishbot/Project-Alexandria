>[!note] Matrix Spaces
>Given a matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$, the **range** of $A$ (also referred to as the column space) is a [[3.4a Vector Spaces|vector subspace]] in $\mathbb{R}^m$ the represents the [[3.4b Span|span]] of its columns, defined as
>$$\mathcal{R}(A)=\operatorname{span}(\vec{a}_1, \vec{a}_2, …, \vec{a}_n)=\left\{\sum^{n}_{i=1}\alpha_i \vec{a}_i : \alpha_i \in \mathbb{R}\right\}\subseteq \mathbb{R}^m,$$
>where $\alpha_{i}$ is the scaling factor (or weight) of the $i$-th column vector of $A$. 
>
>Similarly, the **row space** of $A$, denoted $\operatorname{Row}(A) \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$, is the span of its rows. The row space of $A$ is related to the column space of $A^T$, such that $\operatorname{Row}(A)=\mathcal{R}(A^T)$ (and vice-versa).
>
>Conversely, the **null space** (or kernel) of $A \in \mathbb{R}$  is a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^n$ that includes all vectors that output the zero vector when multiplied by $A$, such that
>$$\mathcal{N}(A) = \{ x \in \mathbb{R}^n \mid A x = 0 \} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n.$$
>
>Per the **Fundamental Theorem of Linear Algebra**, every matrix $A$ naturally splits the domain ($\mathbb{R}^n$) and codomain into two complementary parts each. Namely,
>$$\begin{alignat}{0}\mathbb{R}^n = \mathcal{R}(A^T) \oplus \mathcal{N}(A), \quad \text{and} \quad \mathbb{R}^m = \mathcal{R}(A) \oplus \mathcal{N}(A^T).\end{alignat}$$

# Proof
We start by observing that every $x \in \mathcal{R}(A^T)$ in the row space of $A$ is orthogonal to every $z \in \mathcal{N}(A)$. This follows from the fact that for some $x=A^Ty$, it can be said that
$$x^Tz=y^TAz = y^T 0 = 0.$$
Thus, $\mathcal{R}(A^T) \perp \mathcal{N}(A)$, or equivalently, $\mathcal{N}(A)=\mathcal{R}(A^T)^\perp$. We know that a direct sum of a subspace and its orthogonal complement equals the whole subspace, and thus,
$$\mathcal{R}(A^T) \oplus \mathcal{R}(A^T)^\perp = \mathcal{R}(A^T) \oplus \mathcal{N}(A)=\mathbb{R}^n.$$
By the same reasoning, we can prove the fact for $\mathbb{R}^m$.
