>[!note] Work
>Work (SI: Joule / $\text{J} \equiv \text{kg} \cdot \text{m}^2 \cdot \text{s}^{-2}$) is the energy transferred to or from an object when a force causes it to move. It measures how much a force helps (or resists) movement in the direction of motion.
>
>When the force has constant magnitude and direction, the work done on an object is the component of the force in the direction of motion multiplied by the distance the object moves:
>$$W=\langle \vec{F}, \Delta \vec{r} \rangle = |\vec{F}| \cdot |\Delta \vec{r}| \cdot \cos\theta.$$
>
>Generally, work done by a variable force is the force’s component in the direction of motion multiplied by each infinitesimal step of displacement, summed over the entire path. Mathematically,
>$$W=\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\langle \vec{F}(t), \vec{v}(t) \rangle dt=\int^{}_{\mathcal{P}}\vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r}=\int^{}_{\mathcal{P}}|\vec{F}| \cdot |d\vec{r}| \cdot \cos\theta,$$
>where $\mathcal{P}$ denotes the path taken between $\vec{r}_{i}$ and $\vec{r}_{f}$, and $\theta$ is the angle between $\vec{F}$ and $d\vec{r}$:
>```tikz
>\begin{document}
>\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=1.2]
>  % Path and points
>  \draw[dashed, thick] (0,0) to[out=45,in=180] (2,1) to[out=0,in=225] (4,2);
>  \fill (0,0) circle (2pt) node[below left] {$\vec{r}_i$};
>  \fill (4,2) circle (2pt) node[above right] {$\vec{r}_f$};
>
>  % Particle and dr
>  \filldraw[] (2,1) circle (2pt);
>  \draw[->, red, thick] (2,1) -- ++(1,0) node[below] {$d\vec{r}$};
>
>  % Theta angle
>  \draw (2.3,1) arc[start angle=0, end angle=45, radius=0.3];
>  \node at (2.5,1.25) {$\theta$};
>
>  % Force vector and components
>  \draw[->, thick] (2,1) -- ++(1.2,1.2) node[above right=-1pt] {$\vec{F}(\vec{r})$};
>  \draw[->, dashed] (2,1) -- ++(0,1.3) node[above] {$\vec{F}_\perp$};
>  \draw[->, dashed] (2,1) -- ++(1.3,0) node[right] {$\vec{F}_\parallel$};
>
>  % Label the path
>  \node at (1.1,0.55) {$\mathcal{P}$};
>
>  % Equation
>  \node[align=center, anchor=west] at (-0.5,-1) 
>    {$dW \equiv F_\parallel\, dr = (F \cos\theta)\,dr = \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r}$};
>
>\end{tikzpicture}
>\end{document}
>```

>>[!example]- Determine if the work done by each force is positive, negative or zero.
>>**(a)** The force that pushes a crate is in the positive direction. Similarly, the work done by the force is in the positive direction, since the work is done in the same direction as the force. 
>>
>>**(b)** The force that holds a suitcase is upward, whereas gravity does work downward. However, since there is no vertical displacement, there is zero work done on the suitcase.
>>
>>**(c)** The force that catches an incoming basketball is positive, whereas the basketball moves in the negative direction. Since force and displacement are in the opposite directions, the work done is negative.
