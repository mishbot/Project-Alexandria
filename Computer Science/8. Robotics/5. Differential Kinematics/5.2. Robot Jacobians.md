>[!note] Robot Jacobians
>The **Jacobian** of a serial manipulator is the matrix that maps joint velocities $\dot{\Theta} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ to the end-effector's spatial or body velocity. It is the differential (linearization) of the [[2.1. Forward Kinematics|forward kinematic map]] $g_{st}(\Theta)$ and plays a central role in velocity control, force analysis, and [[5.4. Jacobian Inverse Kinematics|inverse kinematics]].
>
>For a serial chain with [[2.2. Twists and Screws|twists]] $\xi_1, \dots, \xi_n$ and [[2.1. Forward Kinematics|product-of-exponentials]] kinematics $g_{st}(\Theta) = e^{\hat{\xi}_1\theta_1}\cdots e^{\hat{\xi}_n\theta_n}g_{st}(0)$, the **spatial Jacobian** $J^s(\Theta) \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times n}$ satisfies
>$$V^s_{st} = J^s(\Theta)\,\dot{\Theta},$$
>where $V^s_{st}$ is the [[5.1. Rigid Body Velocity|spatial velocity]] of the tool frame. Its columns are
>$$J^s(\Theta) = \begin{bmatrix} \xi_1 & \xi'_2 & \cdots & \xi'_n \end{bmatrix},$$
>where the $i$-th column is the $i$-th joint twist **transformed by all preceding joints**:
>$$\xi'_i = \mathrm{Ad}_{e^{\hat{\xi}_1\theta_1}\cdots e^{\hat{\xi}_{i-1}\theta_{i-1}}}\,\xi_i.$$
>In particular, $\xi'_1 = \xi_1$ always (the first joint twist is already in the spatial frame), and subsequent columns depend on the current joint angles.
>
>The **body Jacobian** $J^b(\Theta) \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times n}$ satisfies
>$$V^b_{st} = J^b(\Theta)\,\dot{\Theta},$$
>where $V^b_{st}$ is the body velocity of the tool frame. Its last column is always $\xi_n^b$ (the $n$-th joint twist in body-frame coordinates at the home configuration), and earlier columns are transformed by joints closer to the tool:
>$$J^b_i = \mathrm{Ad}_{(e^{\hat{\xi}^b_i\theta_i}\cdots e^{\hat{\xi}^b_n\theta_n}g_{st}(0))^{-1}}\,\xi_i.$$
>
>The two Jacobians are related by the [[5.1. Rigid Body Velocity#The Adjoint Map|adjoint]] of the forward kinematics:
>$$J^s(\Theta) = \mathrm{Ad}_{g_{st}(\Theta)}\,J^b(\Theta).$$
>
>>[!warning] Configuration Dependence
>>Both Jacobians depend on the current joint configuration $\Theta$. A configuration where $\operatorname{rank}(J) < \min(6, n)$ is a **[[5.3. Singularities and Manipulability|singularity]]**—the manipulator loses the ability to move in certain task-space directions. Singularity analysis is essential for trajectory planning and control.

# Intuition
The Jacobian answers the question: "if I wiggle joint $i$ by $\delta\theta_i$, how does the end-effector move?" Each column of the Jacobian is the instantaneous twist that joint $i$ would produce if it alone were moving at unit speed. For the spatial Jacobian, this twist is expressed in the fixed spatial frame; for the body Jacobian, in the tool frame.

The key subtlety is that joint $i$'s axis in the spatial frame is not its home-configuration axis $\xi_i$—it has been moved by joints $1$ through $i-1$. The adjoint transformation accounts for this: $\xi'_i = \mathrm{Ad}_{g_{1\cdots(i-1)}}\xi_i$ is the twist of joint $i$ after the preceding joints have repositioned it.

For the body Jacobian, the reasoning is reversed: joint $n$ (closest to the tool) always contributes its home-configuration body-frame twist, while joints further from the tool must be "un-transformed" by the joints between them and the tool.

The relationship $J^s = \mathrm{Ad}_{g_{st}}J^b$ is exactly the change-of-frame formula for velocities: the same physical end-effector motion, reported in spatial vs. body coordinates.

# Properties

## Spatial Jacobian

#### *(Column construction)* Each spatial column is the joint's home twist transformed by all preceding joints.

$$J^s_i(\Theta) = \mathrm{Ad}_{e^{\hat{\xi}_1\theta_1}\cdots e^{\hat{\xi}_{i-1}\theta_{i-1}}}\,\xi_i.$$
Column 1 reduces to $\xi_1$ (no upstream joints); column 2 is $\mathrm{Ad}_{e^{\hat{\xi}_1\theta_1}}\xi_2$; the last column does not depend on $\theta_n$ because no joint is upstream of itself. Consequently, $J^s$ is a function of $\theta_1, \dots, \theta_{n-1}$ only.

#### *(Derivation)* The column structure follows from differentiating the PoE formula and right-multiplying by $g_{st}^{-1}$.

Starting from $g_{st} = e^{\hat{\xi}_1\theta_1}\cdots e^{\hat{\xi}_n\theta_n}g_{st}(0)$:
$$\dot{g}_{st} = \sum_{i=1}^n e^{\hat{\xi}_1\theta_1}\cdots e^{\hat{\xi}_{i-1}\theta_{i-1}}\hat{\xi}_i e^{\hat{\xi}_i\theta_i}\cdots e^{\hat{\xi}_n\theta_n}g_{st}(0)\,\dot{\theta}_i.$$
Right-multiplying by $g_{st}^{-1}$ and using $e^{\hat{\xi}\theta}\hat{\xi}e^{-\hat{\xi}\theta} = \widehat{\mathrm{Ad}_{e^{\hat{\xi}\theta}}\xi}$, each term collapses to $\hat{\xi}'_i\dot{\theta}_i$, yielding $\hat{V}^s = \sum_i \hat{\xi}'_i\dot{\theta}_i$.

#### *(Revolute column)* A revolute joint's spatial column has the same twist structure after transformation, with axis and pivot carried along by the upstream chain.

At home, $\xi_i = \begin{bmatrix} -\omega_i \times q_i \\ \omega_i \end{bmatrix}$. After the preceding joints rotate and translate the chain, the transformed column $\xi'_i$ has the axis rotated to $R\omega_i$ and the effective pivot point shifted to $R q_i + p$, where $(R, p)$ is the upstream PoE product.

## Body Jacobian

#### *(Column construction)* Each body column is the joint's home twist transformed by the rigid motion from joint $i$ to the tool.

$$J^b_i(\Theta) = \mathrm{Ad}_{(e^{\hat{\xi}_i\theta_i}\cdots e^{\hat{\xi}_n\theta_n}g_{st}(0))^{-1}}\,\xi_i.$$
The last column is $J^b_n = \mathrm{Ad}_{g_{st}(0)^{-1}}\xi_n = \xi^b_n$, which is configuration-*independent*. Symmetrically to the spatial case, $J^b$ depends on $\theta_2, \dots, \theta_n$ but not $\theta_1$.

#### *(Relation to spatial Jacobian)* $J^s$ and $J^b$ are equivalent under the adjoint of the forward kinematics.

$J^s = \mathrm{Ad}_{g_{st}}\,J^b$ and $J^b = \mathrm{Ad}_{g_{st}^{-1}}\,J^s$. Because $\mathrm{Ad}_{g_{st}}$ is always invertible, $\operatorname{rank}(J^s) = \operatorname{rank}(J^b)$ — singularity is an intrinsic geometric property, independent of the frame in which the Jacobian is expressed.

## Dimensions and Rank

#### *(Dimensions)* For $n$ joints, $J \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times n}$ with maximum possible rank $\min(6, n)$.

The $6$ comes from the dimension of $\mathfrak{se}(3)$; the $n$ from the number of joint-velocity inputs.

#### *(Rank and mobility)* Jacobian rank determines how much of the 6D task space the manipulator can access at a given configuration.

At a regular configuration:
- $n < 6$: the image of $J$ is an $n$-dimensional subspace of task-space twists — the manipulator is kinematically incapable of moving in certain directions.
- $n = 6$: $J$ is square and generically invertible, so arbitrary end-effector twists are reachable.
- $n > 6$: $J$ has a non-trivial null space of dimension $n - \operatorname{rank}(J)$. Joint velocities in this null space produce zero end-effector motion — these are **self-motions** of a [[2.2. Workspaces|redundant]] manipulator, which [[5.4. Jacobian Inverse Kinematics|null-space IK]] exploits.

## Force Duality

#### *(Transpose maps wrenches to torques)* The dual of the velocity relation $V = J\dot{\Theta}$ is the static relation $\tau = J^T F$.

The principle of virtual work requires $\tau^T\dot{\Theta} = F^T V$ for all $\dot{\Theta}$; substituting $V = J\dot{\Theta}$ gives $\tau^T = F^T J$, i.e., $\tau = J^T F$. This duality lets the same matrix used for velocity control be reused for force control and gravity compensation, with no extra kinematic modeling.

# Examples
>[!example] Spatial Jacobian of a 2R Planar Manipulator
>Consider a planar 2-revolute arm with link lengths $\ell_1, \ell_2$, both joints rotating about $z$. At home configuration, the joints are at the origin and at $(\ell_1, 0, 0)$.
>>[!example]- Column-by-column construction
>>**Joint twists at home:** $\xi_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$ (rotation about $z$ through origin), $\xi_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ \ell_1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$ (rotation about $z$ through $(\ell_1, 0, 0)$, since $-\omega \times q = -(0,0,1) \times (\ell_1, 0, 0) = (0, \ell_1, 0)$).
>>
>>**Column 1:** $J^s_1 = \xi_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$.
>>
>>**Column 2:** $J^s_2 = \mathrm{Ad}_{e^{\hat{\xi}_1\theta_1}}\xi_2$. Since joint 1 is a pure rotation about $z$ through the origin, $e^{\hat{\xi}_1\theta_1} = \begin{bmatrix} R_z(\theta_1) & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$ and $\mathrm{Ad} = \begin{bmatrix} R_z & 0 \\ 0 & R_z \end{bmatrix}$. The angular part is unchanged ($\omega = (0,0,1)$ is invariant under $R_z$). The linear part becomes $R_z(\theta_1)\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ \ell_1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -\ell_1 s_1 \\ \ell_1 c_1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$.
>>
>>$$J^s(\theta_1) = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -\ell_1 s_1 \\ 0 & \ell_1 c_1 \\ 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
>>
>>**Singularity:** $\det(J^s_{v_{xy}}) = 0$ when $\ell_1 c_1 \cdot 0 - (-\ell_1 s_1)\cdot 0 = 0$—but more usefully, the planar $3\times 2$ sub-Jacobian (taking $v_x, v_y, \omega_z$ rows) loses rank when the two joint axes produce linearly dependent twists. For this 2R arm, this happens when $\theta_2 = 0$ or $\theta_2 = \pi$ (arm fully extended or folded).

>[!example] Spatial vs. Body Jacobian Relationship
>For any configuration $\Theta$, verify that $J^s = \mathrm{Ad}_{g_{st}}J^b$ by checking that both give the same end-effector velocity:
>>[!example]- Verification
>>$V^s = J^s\dot{\Theta}$ and $V^b = J^b\dot{\Theta}$, so $V^s = \mathrm{Ad}_{g_{st}}V^b = \mathrm{Ad}_{g_{st}}J^b\dot{\Theta}$. Since this holds for all $\dot{\Theta}$, we get $J^s = \mathrm{Ad}_{g_{st}}J^b$. This confirms that singularities are frame-independent: $\operatorname{rank}(J^s) = \operatorname{rank}(\mathrm{Ad}_{g_{st}}J^b) = \operatorname{rank}(J^b)$, since $\mathrm{Ad}_{g_{st}}$ is always invertible.
