>[!note] Gaussian Distribution
>A continuous random variable $X \in \mathbb{R}$ follows the **Gaussian distribution** $X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$ with parameters $\mu \in \mathbb{R}$ and variance $\sigma^2 > 0$, defined by the [[2.5c.1 Probability Density Function|probability density function]]
>$$f_{X}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}e^{-(x-\mu)^2/2\sigma^2}.$$
>
>The Gaussian distribution is used to model real-valued data that tend to cluster around a central value (mean) $\mu$ with symmetric dispersion $\sigma$.
>
>Moreover, if $X \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$, then $X$ is a **standard normal random variable**. Its probability density function simplifies to $f_{X}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^2/2}$, which leads to its cumulative distribution function,
>$$\Phi(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{ 2 \pi }}\int^{x}_{-\infty}e^{-x^2/2}dx.$$
>
>A key characteristic of the normal distribution is the **empirical rule (68-95-99 rule)**, which states that around $68\%$, $95\%$, and $99\%$ of the data falls within $1\sigma$, $2\sigma$, and $3\sigma$ of the mean.
>>[!warning] Standardization
>>Any Gaussian random variable can be standardized into the Normal distribution using the transformation $Z=\frac{X-\mu}{\sigma}$.

# Properties
#### (*Mean*) $\mathbb{E}[X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)]=\mu$.

#### (*Variance*) $Var[X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)] = \sigma^2$.

#### (*Sum*) If $X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma_{X}^2)$ and $Y \sim \mathcal{N}(\eta, \sigma_{Y}^2)$, then $X+Y \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu+\eta, \sigma_{X}^2+\sigma_{Y}^2)$.
#### (Positive Gaussian) $\mathbb{E}[X \mid X>0] = \sigma \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}$ and $\mathbb{E}[X \mid X+Y > 0]$.
Since we know that $f_{X \mid A}(x)=\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(X \in A)}$, we proceed as follows:
$$\begin{align}
\mathbb{E}[X \mid X > 0] &= \int^{\infty}_{0} xf_{X \mid X >0}(x)dx \\
&= \frac{\int^{\infty}_{ 0 }xf_{X}(x)dx}{\mathbb{P}(X>0)} \\
&= \frac{\int^{\infty}_{ 0 }xf_{X}(x)dx}{0.5} \\
&= \frac{2}{\sigma\sqrt{ 2\pi }}\int^{\infty}_{ 0 }xe^{-x^2/2\sigma^2}dx \\
&= -\sigma^2\sqrt{ \frac{2}{\pi\sigma^2} } e^{-x^2/2\sigma^2} \bigg|^{\infty}_{x=0} \\
&= \sigma \sqrt{ \frac{2}{\pi} }
\end{align}$$
