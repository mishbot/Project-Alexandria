>[!note] Newton's Laws
>**Newton’s Laws** describe the relationship between force, motion, and interaction: how objects behave when forces act on them or when they interact with other objects:
>1. **Newton’s First Law** states that ***an object remains at rest or moves at constant velocity unless acted upon by a net external force***. This only holds in inertial reference frames, where motion is not observed to spontaneously accelerate or decelerate.
>‎ 
>2. **Newton’s Second Law** defines the precise relationship between force and motion: the net force **exerted** on an object is equal to the product of its mass and [[1.1.2. Acceleration|acceleration]], such that
>$$\sum \vec{F} = m\vec{a}.$$
>The First Law is a special case of the Second Law, where $\sum \vec{F} = 0$.
>‎ 
>3. **Newton’s Third Law** states that if object $A$ exerts a force on object $B$, then $B$ simultaneously exerts a force of ***equal magnitude*** and ***opposite direction*** on $A$. These action–reaction pairs act on different objects and therefore do not cancel.
>

>>[!example]- What is the relation between the inertial masses of these three objects?
>>Let $a=\dfrac{1}{M_{i}}F$. Then, we see that there is an inverse relation between mass and acceleration: given the same force, a lighter object will be accelerated more than a heavier object. As such,
>>$$M_{1} < M_{2} < M_{3}.$$


>[!example] Two Masses
>Consider two objects $A$ and $B$. When we apply a force $F$ on object $A$, it obtains an acceleration of $3 m/s^2$. The ratio of the two objects’ masses is $m_{A}$ : $m_{B} = 3 : 2$. The two objects are placed together, and $F$ is applied to them such that the two move together.
>>[!example]- What is the acceleration of the combined system?
>>Since $\dfrac{m_{A}}{m_{B}}=\dfrac{3}{2}$, then $m_{B}=\dfrac{2}{3}m_{A}$. If the same force is applied on both objects, then $3m_{A}=a(m_{A}+m_{B})$, so $a=\frac{3m_{A}}{m_{A}+m_{B}}=9/5 \; \text{m/s}^2$.
