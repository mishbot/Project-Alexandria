>[!note] Projectile Motion
>**Projectile motion** is the motion of an object thrown or projected into the air, subject only to the force of gravity and initial velocity. It follows a parabolic trajectory, with horizontal motion being uniform and vertical motion influenced by [[1.1.2. Acceleration|constant acceleration]] due to gravity:
>```tikz
>\begin{document}
>\begin{tikzpicture}
>
>    % Axes
>    \draw[->] (0,0) -- (7,0) node[right] {$x$};
>    \draw[->] (0,0) -- (0,3.5) node[above] {$y$};
>
>    % Parabolic trajectory
>    \draw[thick, dashed, domain=0:6.5] plot(\x, {1.5*\x - 0.25*\x*\x});
>
>    % Initial velocity and components
>    \draw[thick,->,green] (0,0) -- (1,2) node[pos=0.6,right] {$\vec{v}_0$};
>    \draw[->,green,dashed] (0,0) -- (1,0) node[pos=0.5,below] {$\vec{v}_{x_{0}}$};
>    \draw[->,green,dashed] (0,0) -- (0,2) node[pos=0.5,left] {$\vec{v}_{y_{0}}$};
>
>    % Angle theta
>    \draw[->] (0.8,0) arc[start angle=0,end angle=54,radius=0.8];
>    \node at (1,0.5) {$\theta_0$};
>
>    % Tangential velocity vectors at different points on the parabola
>    \draw[thick,->,green] (4.75,1.5) -- +(0.5,-0.5) node[pos=0.8,right] {$\vec{v}$};
>    \draw[thick,->,green] (6,0) -- +(1,-2) node[pos=0.8,right] {$\vec{v}$};
>
>    % Velocity components at points along the parabola
>    \draw[thick,->,green] (2.85,2.25) -- +(0.5,0) node[right] {$\vec{v}$};
>    \draw[thick] (2.85, 2.25) -- (3.5, 3) node[right] {$\vec{v}_y = 0$};
>
>    \draw[thick,->,green,dashed] (4.75,1.5) -- +(0.5,0) node[above] {$\vec{v}_x$};
>    \draw[thick,->,green,dashed] (4.75,1.5) -- +(0,-0.5) node[left] {$\vec{v}_y$};
>
>\end{tikzpicture}
>\end{document}
>```
>The horizontal length $R$ between the two roots of the parabola is the total distance traveled by the object, also called the **level horizontal range** of the object. It is defined as 
>$$R = v_{x_0}t.$$
>When $y_0 = y_f$ (i.e. the object lands at the same height that it was projected from), the level horizontal range is given as
>$$\begin{align}&&R=\frac{v_0^2 \sin 2 \theta}{g} && (\text{for } y_0 = y_f)\end{align},$$
>since $t=\dfrac{2v_{y_0}}{g}$ is the total time of flight for any projectile launched from and landing back at the same height.

>[!example] (Medium) Package Dropoff
>A plane flying horizontally at height $H$ and with speed $v_{0}$ drops a package. At the same time, it fires a flying capsule horizontally which immediately accelerates with a horizontal acceleration $a$. The capsule travels twice the horizontal distance from the point it was released as the package when it hits the ground:
>```tikz
>\usepackage{tikz}
>\usepackage{amsmath}
>\begin{document}
>
>\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.75]
>
>% Axes
>\draw[->] (0,0) -- (7,0) node[right] {$x$};
>\draw[->] (0,0) -- (0,5) node[above] {$y$};
>
>% Height marker
>\draw[dashed] (0,0) -- (0,4) node[midway,left] {$H$};
>
>% Plane as dot
>\filldraw[black] (0,4) circle (2pt) node[left] {Plane};
>
>% Elliptical arcs (projectile paths)
>\draw[dotted, thick] (0,4) arc[start angle=90, end angle=0, x radius=3cm, y radius=4cm];
>\draw[dotted, thick] (0,4) arc[start angle=90, end angle=0, x radius=6cm, y radius=4cm];
>
>\end{tikzpicture}
>
>\end{document}
>```
>>[!example]- What is the horizontal acceleration $a$ of the capsule?
>>Since the level horizontal range of the capsule is twice the one of the package, it holds that the total time of flight for both objects is
$$2v_{0}t=v_{0}t+\frac{1}{2}at^2 \iff t=\frac{2v_{0}}{a}.$$
Additionally, $H-\dfrac{1}{2}gt^2=0$ for both the capsule and the package. Substituting for $t$,
$$H-\dfrac{1}{2}g \cdot \dfrac{4v_{0}^2}{a^2}=0 \iff a = v_{0}\sqrt{ \frac{2g}{H} }.$$

>[!example] (Medium) Free Throws
>Breanna shoots a free throw with a horizontal distance $L$ away from the hoop. The hoop has a height $H_{1}$ and the height at which she releases the ball is $H_{2}$. Suppose the ball leaves her hand with an angle $\theta$ with respect to the horizontal.
>>[!example]- What should the initial speed of the ball be when it leaves Breanna’s hand in order to make the shot?
>>Consider the following graph:
>>```tikz
>>\usepackage{amsmath}
>>\usepackage{tikz}
>>\begin{document}
>>\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.9]
>>
>>% Axes
>>\draw[->] (0,0) -- (7,0) node[anchor=north] {\(x\)};
>>\draw[->] (0,0) -- (0,4.5) node[anchor=east] {\(y\)};
>>
>>% Trajectory
>>\draw[dotted, thick] (0,0) arc[start angle=155, end angle=45, x radius=3.75cm, y radius=4cm];
>>
>>% Initial velocity vector
>>\draw[->, thick] (0,0) -- (1,3) node[above right] {\(\vec{v}_0\)};
>>% Components
>>\draw[->, thick] (0,0) -- (1,0) node[midway, below] {\(v_0 \cos\theta\)};
>>\draw[->, thick] (1,0) -- (1,3) node[midway, right] {\(v_0 \sin\theta\)};
>>
>>% Angle theta
>>\draw (0.5,0) arc[start angle=15,end angle=45,radius=1cm];
>>\node at (0.6,0.35) {\(\theta\)};
>>
>>% Hoop
>>\draw[fill=black] (6,1.2) circle (0.06);
>>\draw[dashed] (6,0) -- (6,1.2);
>>\node at (6,-0.3) {\(L\)};
>>\node at (7,0.6) {\(H_2 - H_1\)};
>>
>>\end{tikzpicture}
>>\end{document}
>>```
>>Begin by expressing $v_{x_{0}}=v_{0}\cos\theta$ and $v_{y_{0}}=v_{0}\sin\theta$. Then, find the time at which the ball reaches a horizontal length of $L$, such that
>>$$L=v_{0}\cos \theta t \iff t = \frac{L}{v_{0}\cos\theta}.$$
>>
>>After finding the time, express the height of the hoop as $H_{1}-H_{2}=v_{0}\sin\theta t - \dfrac{1}{2}gt^2$, and solve, such that
>>$$H_{1}-H_{2}=L\tan\theta - \frac{1}{2}g \cdot \frac{L^2}{v_{0}^{2}\cos^2\theta} \iff v_{0}=\sqrt{ \frac{gL^2}{2\cos^2\theta(L\tan\theta-H_{1}+H_{2})}}.$$


>[!example] (Hard) Colliding Baseballs
>At time $t = 0$, a baseball is thrown from with an initial speed $v_{1}$ and an angle $\theta = 60\degree$ with respect to the horizontal. At a distance $L$ away a second baseball thrown straight up at initial speed $v_2$ tries to intercept the first baseball while it's still ascending.
>>[!example]- At what time $t_0$ should the second baseball be launched?
>>Let $t_1, t_2$ denote the relative times since launch at which the two balls collide. Then, $t_{1}=t_{2}+t_{0}$. Ball $1$ meets ball $2$ when they are at the same position, so $$L=v_{1}\cos\theta t_{1}=v_{1}\cos\theta(t_{2}+t_{0}) \iff t_{2}=\dfrac{L}{v_{1}\cos \theta}-t_{0}.$$
>>
>>We then equate the $y$-coordinate of both balls, such that
>>$$v_{1}\sin \theta t_{1}-\frac{1}{2}gt_{1}^2=v_{2}t_{2}-\frac{1}{2}gt_{2}^2.$$
>>Substitute $t_{1}=\dfrac{L}{v_{1}\cos \theta}$ and $t_{2}=\dfrac{L}{v_{1}\cos \theta}-t_{0}$ to solve and obtain
>>$$t_0 =\frac{L}{v_1\cos\theta} -\frac{v_2}{g} +\frac{1}{g}\,\sqrt{\,v_2^2 \;-\;2\,g\Bigl(L\tan\theta-\frac{gL^2}{2v_1^2\cos^2\theta}\Bigr)\!}\,.$$
