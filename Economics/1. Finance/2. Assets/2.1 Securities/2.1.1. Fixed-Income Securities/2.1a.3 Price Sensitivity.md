>[!note] Duration & Convexity
>The **duration** of a [[2.1a.1 Bonds|bond]] measures the sensitivity of its price to changes in yield (interest). There are two types of duration:
>- **Macaulay duration** is the weighted average time until all cashflows are received, where the weights are the present values of each period's cashflow. This is formally defined as
>$$D = \frac{1}{nP}\left[\sum^{nT}_{t=1}\frac{C_{t}}{(1+y/n)^t}t+\frac{F}{(1+y/n)^{nT}}nT\right].$$
>- **Modified duration** adjusts Macaulay duration to approximate the percentage change in the price of a bond for a 1% change in yield, such that
>$$D^* = -\frac{1}{P} \cdot \frac{dP}{dy} = \frac{D}{1+y/n},$$
>where the negative sign arises from the inverse relationship between bond price and yield.
>
>Generally, price-yield curves are *convex*. Higher price-yield convexity implies a higher price regardless of which way prices or directions move: 
>
>
>The convexity of a price-yield curve is represented as the second derivative of its price with respect to yield, such that
>$$\Gamma = \frac{1}{P} \cdot \frac{d^2P}{dy^2}=\frac{1}{n^2P(1+y/n)^{2}}\left[\sum^{nT}_{t=1}\frac{C_{t}}{(1+y/n)^{t}}(t^2+t)+\frac{F}{(1+y/n)^{nT}}(n^2T^2+nT)\right].$$
>
>Convexity can be used in tandem with modified duration to create a better approximation for price:
>$$\frac{dP}{P}=-D^* \cdot dR + \frac{1}{2}\Gamma \cdot dR^2.$$
