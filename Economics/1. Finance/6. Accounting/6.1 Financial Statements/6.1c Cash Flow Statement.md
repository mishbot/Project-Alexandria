>[!note] Statement of Cash Flows
>The **statement of cash flows** is a financial report that tracks the movement of cash in and out of a company over a specific period. It categorizes cash flows into three main activities, which it equates to net income, such that
>$$\text{Net Income} = \text{Operating Cash Flow} + \text{Investment Cash Flow} + \text{Financing Cash Flow}.$$
>The following example shows the trickle-down equation from the starting to the ending cash balance:

>    \node[anchor=west] at (0.2,-1.3) {\textbf{CASH DISBURSEMENTS}};

>The statement of cash flows serves as a bridge between the income statement and the balance sheet by tracking the movement of cash within a company. It adjusts net income from the income statement and explains changes in balance sheet accounts to show how much cash was generated or used.
>
>>[!warning] Accrual vs. Cash Accounting
>>Whereas the balance sheet and the income statement are accrual (they recognize revenue and expenses when incurred), the statement of cash flows reports transactions **when cash actually moves**.
