>[!note] Variance
>The **variance** of a random variable measures the spread of its values around its [[2.6a Expectation|expected value]] (mean). For any random variable $X$, where $g: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$,
>$$\operatorname{Var}(X)=\mathbb{E}[(X-\mathbb{E}[X])^2]=\mathbb{E}[X^2] - E[X]^2.$$
>The exact calculation of variance is given by
>$$\operatorname{Var}(X)=\begin{dcases}\sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}}(x-\mathbb{E}[X])^2p_{X}(x) & x \text{ discrete}, \\ \int^{\infty}_{-\infty}(x-\mathbb{E}[X])^2f_{X}(x)dx & x \text{ continuous}\end{dcases}$$
>
>Furthermore, the square root $\sigma_{X}(X)=\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(X)}$ is known as the standard deviation of $X$.
>
>>[!warning] Non-Negativity
>>Since $(X-\mathbb{E}[X])^2 \geq 0$ for all $X$, the variance of any random variable is always non-negative.
# Intuition
We will now prove the steps to go from the middle to the RHS of the above equation. Firstly, we take $Var(X)=\mathbb{E}[(X-\upmu)^2]$ and $\mathbb{E}[X]=\upmu$. We proceed as follows:
$$
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Var(X) &= \mathbb{E}[(X-\upmu)^2] \\
&= \mathbb{E}[X^2-2\upmu X+\upmu^2] \\
&= \mathbb{E}[X^2]-2\upmu\mathbb{E}[X]+\upmu^2 \\
&= \mathbb{E}[X^2]-2\upmu \times \upmu + \upmu^2 \\
&= \mathbb{E}[X^2]-\upmu^2
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
$$
We also need to prove the identity $Var(cX)=c^2Var(X)$. An important note here is that  [[3.1b Linearity of Expectation|linearity of expectation]] leads us to $\mathbb{E}[cX]=c\mathbb{E}[X]=c \upmu$ instead of just $\upmu$. Knowing this, we proceed as follows:
$$
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Var(cX) &=\mathbb{E}[(cX-c\upmu)^2] \\
&= \mathbb{E}[c^2(X-\upmu)^2] \\
&= c^2\mathbb{E}[(X-\upmu)^2] \\
&= c^2Var(X)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
$$
# Properties
#### (*Linearity*) $\operatorname{Var}(\alpha X + \beta Y + \gamma) = \alpha^2\operatorname{Var}(X)+\beta^2\operatorname{Var}(Y)$.
As we proved in the [[2.6b Variance|previous note]], $Var(cX)=c^2Var(X)$. Here, we will prove the same for [[2.3 Independent Random Variables|independent random variables]] using linearity of expectation:
$$
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Var(c_1X+c_2Y) &= \mathbb{E}[(c_1X+c_2Y)^2]-\mathbb{E}[c_1X+c_2Y]^2 \\
&= c_1^2\mathbb{E}[X^2]+2c_1c_2\mathbb{E}[XY]+c_2^2\mathbb{E}[Y^2]-c_1^2\mathbb{E}[X]^2-2c_1c_2\mathbb{E}[X]\mathbb{E}[Y]-c_2^2\mathbb{E}[Y]^2 \\
&= c_1^2(\mathbb{E}[X^2]-\mathbb{E}[X]^2) + 2c_1c_2(\mathbb{E}[XY]-\mathbb{E}[X]\mathbb{E}[Y])+c_2^2(\mathbb{E}[Y^2]-\mathbb{E}[Y]^2) \\
&= c_1^2Var(X)+c_2^2Var(Y)+2c_1c_2(\mathbb{E}[XY]-\mathbb{E}[X]\mathbb{E}[Y]) \\
&= c_1^2Var(X)+c_2^2Var(Y)+2c_1c_2(\mathbb{E}[X]\mathbb{E}[Y]-\mathbb{E}[X]\mathbb{E}[Y]) \\
&= c_1^2Var(X)+c_2^2Var(Y)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
$$
Note that the last term cancels out since $X$ and $Y$ are independent, so $\mathbb{E}[XY]=\mathbb{E}[X]\mathbb{E}[Y]$.

#### (Sum) $\text{Var}(\alpha X+ \beta Y)=\alpha^2\operatorname{Var}(X)+\beta^2\operatorname{Var}(Y)+2\alpha\beta\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)$.

#### (*R.V. Conditionality*) $\operatorname{Var}(X \mid Y = y)= \mathbb{E}\left[(X-\mathbb{E}[X \mid Y=y])^2 \mid Y\right]$

#### (*Total Variance*) $\operatorname{Var}(X)=\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{Var}(X \mid Y)] + \operatorname{Var}(\mathbb{E}[X \mid Y])$.
# Examples

>[!example] Coin Tossing (Binomial Variance)
>We now return to the example of coin tosses. Let $X_n=I_1+I_2+...+I_n$ denote the number of heads in $n$ tosses of a biased coin with probability of heads $p$ ($X \enspace \textasciitilde \enspace \text{Bin}(n,p)$). Once again we use the indicator random variable $I_i$, which is either $1$ or $0$ depending on whether or not the $i$th toss returned heads. We also already know that $\mathbb{E}[X_n]=\sum^{n}_{i=1}\mathbb{E}[I_i]=np$. Let's compute the variance of the distribution:
>$$
>\begin{equation}
>\begin{aligned}
>Var(X_n) &= \sum^{n}_{i=1}Var(I_i) \\
>&= \sum^{n}_{i=1}\mathbb{E}[I_i^2]-\mathbb{E}[I_i]^2 \\
>&= \sum^{n}_{i=1}(1^2 \times p + 0^2 \times p)-(1 \times p + 0 \times p)^2 \\
>&= \sum^{n}_{i=1}p-p^2 \\
>&= np(1-p)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
>$$
