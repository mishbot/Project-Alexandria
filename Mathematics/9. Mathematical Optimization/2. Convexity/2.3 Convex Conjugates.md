>[!note] Convex Conjugate
>The **convex (Fenchel) conjugate** $f^*: X^* \to \mathbb{R}$ of a function $f: X \to \mathbb{R}$ is used to represent $f$ as a set of supporting hyperplanes with slopes $x^*$, such that the parameters of each $x^*$ are encoded in $f^*$. Then, $f^*(x^*)$ is the intercept at which the line $\langle x^*, x \rangle$ becomes a tangent of $f$.  Formally,
>$$f^*(x^*)=\sup_{x \in X} \{ \langle x^*, x \rangle - f(x)\},$$
>such that $t(x) = \langle x^*, x \rangle - f^*(x^*)$.
>
>>[!warning] Preservation of Convexity
>>The convex conjugate of any function is always a convex function.

# Properties
#### (*Fenchel's Inequality*) Any conjugate pair $f, f^*$ satisfies $\langle x^*, x \rangle \leq f(x) + f^*(x^*)$.
#### (*Weak Duality of Biconjugation*) For any $f$, it follows that $f^{* *} \leq f$, such that $f^{**}=f$ if and only if $f$ is convex and l.s.c.
#### (*Affine Conjugate*) Any $a(x)=\langle x^*_{a}, x \rangle + b$ has $a^*(x^*)=\begin{dcases}-b & x^*=x_{a}^* \\ + \infty & x^* \neq x_{a}^*\end{dcases}$, and $a^{**}(x)=a(x)$.


# Examples
>[!example] Economic Interpretation
>Let $x$ be the quantity of some goods you are trying to sell (i.e., laptops, tablets, phones, etc.). Then $f: X \to \mathbb{R}$ can be defined as the total cost of producing the goods (i.e., the dot product between $x$ and the respective cost of producing 1 unit of each good). Take the Fenchel conjugate of $f(x)$:
>$$\underbrace{f^*(x^*)}_{\text{Profit}}=\sup_{x \in X}\{ \underbrace{\langle x^*, x \rangle}_{\text{Revenue}} - \underbrace{f(x)}_{\text{Cost}} \}.$$
>Then, $f^*: X^* \to \mathbb{R}$ represents the optimal profit given the goods are sold at some prices $x^*$ per 1 unit of goods.
