>[!note] Option Pricing
>This section prices options with a model of the stock price. [[2.3.3a One-Step Binomial Model]] lets the stock move to one of two prices and prices the option by hedging, by replication, or by arbitrage, which all give $f = e^{-rT}[p f_u + (1 - p) f_d]$. [[2.3.3b Risk-Neutral Valuation]] reads $p$ as the probability under which the stock earns the risk-free rate, so the price is a discounted expected payoff. [[2.3.3c Binomial Tree]] repeats the step over many periods, with $u = e^{\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}}$ and $d = 1/u$, and handles American options. [[2.3.3d Delta Hedging]] shows that the hedge must be rebalanced at every node and that the rebalancing is self-financing.
>
>In continuous time the stock follows geometric Brownian motion. [[2.3.3e Black-Scholes-Merton Equation]] derives the partial differential equation that every derivative price satisfies, and [[2.3.3f Black-Scholes-Merton Formula]] solves it for European calls and puts. The formula is the limit of the binomial tree as the number of steps grows. [[2.3.4 Greeks]] and [[2.3.5 Volatility]] build on the formula.

# Contents

- [[2.3.3a One-Step Binomial Model]]: hedged portfolio, replication, the arbitrage at a wrong price, and the one-step pricing formula.
- [[2.3.3b Risk-Neutral Valuation]]: the no-arbitrage condition $d < e^{r\Delta t} < u$, risk-neutral and real-world probabilities, and why expected returns do not enter the price. **Not taught:** state prices and the details of Girsanov's theorem.
- [[2.3.3c Binomial Tree]]: recombining trees, backward induction, the Cox-Ross-Rubinstein parameters, American options, and other tree designs. **Not taught:** the closed-form sum for European prices, derivations of the alternative trees, and how the error behaves as the number of steps grows.
- [[2.3.3d Delta Hedging]]: dynamic hedging on a tree, self-financing rebalancing, and why the cost of hedging equals the option price.
- [[2.3.3e Black-Scholes-Merton Equation]]: the model's assumptions, two derivations of the equation, the dividend-yield version, and its solution through the heat equation.
- [[2.3.3f Black-Scholes-Merton Formula]]: call and put prices with a dividend yield, limits, put-call parity, the risk-neutral density and derivation, the link to the binomial tree, and the choice of $\sigma$. **Not taught:** the sketch of the binomial limit and the at-the-money approximation $c \approx 0.4\,S\sigma\sqrt{T - t}$.
