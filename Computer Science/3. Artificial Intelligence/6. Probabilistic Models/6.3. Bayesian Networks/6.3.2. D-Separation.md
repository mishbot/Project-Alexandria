>[!note] D-Separation in Bayes Nets
>Conditional independence relationships in [[6.3. Bayesian Networks|Bayesian Networks]] can be directly read from the graph through **D-separation**, a graphical criterion that determines whether two nodes are independent given a set of observed nodes. Consider the three canonical three-node patterns:
>- **Causal Chain** ($X \to Y \to Z$): it follows that $X \not\perp Z$, but $X \perp Z \mid Y$.
>- **Common Cause** ($X  \leftarrow Y \to Z$): it follows that $X \not\perp Z$, but $X \perp Z \mid Y$.
>- **Common Effect (Collider)** ($X \rightarrow Y \leftarrow Z$): it follows that $X \perp Z$, but $X \not\perp Z \mid Y$, or any other descendant of $Y$.
>
>A path between two variables is said to be **active** if all its triples (segments of three nodes):
>- ...form a **chain** or **common cause**, and the **middle node** is **unobserved**.
>- ...form a **common effect**, and the **middle node** or one of **its descendants is observed**.
>
>If all paths between $X$ and $Y$ are inactive given a set of observed nodes $Z$, then $X \perp Y \mid Z$. Otherwise, $X$ and $Y$ are **D-connected** and potentially dependent.
>
>In practice, the D-separation algorithm proceeds as follows:
>1. Shade all observed nodes $Z$.
>2. Enumerate all undirected paths between $X$ and $Y$.
>3. For each path, decompose it into triples and determine which are active.
>4. If every path is inactive, $X \perp Y \mid Z$.
