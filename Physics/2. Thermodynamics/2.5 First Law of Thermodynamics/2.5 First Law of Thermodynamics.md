>[!note] First Law of Thermodynamics
>This section turns energy conservation into a working tool for gases. [[2.5.1 Thermodynamic Work|Work]] is the area under a path on the $P$-$V$ diagram, and it depends on the path. The [[2.5.2 First Law of Thermodynamics|first law]] states that the internal energy of a system changes by the heat that enters minus the work it does, $\Delta E_{\text{int}} = Q - W$. The change in internal energy depends only on the endpoints, and for an ideal gas it is $\tfrac{d}{2}(P_2 V_2 - P_1 V_1)$, where $d$ counts the [[2.3.4 Equipartition of Energy|accessible degrees of freedom]] and each holds $\tfrac{1}{2} kT$. The heat has no formula of its own and is always found last, from the first law.
>
>The four standard [[2.5.3 Thermodynamic Processes|processes]] are the paths on which the work has a closed form. Three of them fix one of $V$, $P$, and $T$. The fourth, the adiabatic process, fixes $Q = 0$ and follows $PV^{\gamma} = \text{const}$. The [[2.5.4 Molar Specific Heats|molar specific heats]] $C_V = \tfrac{d}{2} R$ and $C_P = (\tfrac{d}{2} + 1) R$ are the constant-volume and constant-pressure cases written per mole, and their ratio is $\gamma = 1 + 2/d$.

# Contents

- [[2.5.1 Thermodynamic Work]]: $W = \int P\,dV$, the $P$-$V$ diagram, and why work depends on the path. **Not taught:** stirring work, expansion into a vacuum, and $W = P_{\text{ext}}\,\Delta V$ for a sudden change.
- [[2.5.2 First Law of Thermodynamics]]: $\Delta E_{\text{int}} = Q - W$, the differential form $dQ = dE_{\text{int}} + P\,dV$, the sign conventions, and the recipe for every gas problem. **Not taught:** work done by an expanding solid.
- [[2.5.3 Thermodynamic Processes]]: the isovolumetric, isobaric, isothermal, and adiabatic paths, with a table of $W$, $\Delta E_{\text{int}}$, and $Q$.
- [[2.5.4 Molar Specific Heats]]: $C_V$, $C_P$, $C_P - C_V = R$, $\gamma$, and the heat capacity $nC_V$ of a whole sample. **Not taught:** the effective heat capacity of a $PV^x$ path.
