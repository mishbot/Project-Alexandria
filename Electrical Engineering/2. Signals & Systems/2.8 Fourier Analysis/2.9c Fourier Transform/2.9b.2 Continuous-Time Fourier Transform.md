>[!note] Continuous-Time Fourier Transform (CTFT)
>The continuous-time Fourier transform is a mathematical tool used in signal processing to analyze the frequency content of **non-periodic continuous-time signals** into **continuous functions of frequency**, providing a complete representation of the signal’s frequency spectrum, such that
>$$x(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi} \int^{\infty}_{-\infty}X(\omega)e^{j\omega t}d\omega \iff X(\omega)=\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}x(t)e^{-j\omega t}dt.$$
>As in discrete time, the frequency response of $x(t)$ is equivalent to its Fourier transform.

# Properties
#### (*Dirac Input*) $x(t)=\delta(t) \iff X(\omega) = 1$.
#### (Unit Input) $x(t)=1 \iff X(\omega)=2\pi\delta(\omega)$
#### (Sine Input) $x(t)=\frac{A}{\pi t}\sin(Bt) \iff X(\omega)=\begin{cases}A & \text{if } -B \leq \omega \leq B \\ 0 &\text{otherwise}\end{cases}$.
#### (*Time Shift*) $x(t-t_0) \iff X(\omega)e^{-j\omega t_0}$.
$$\begin{align}
X(\omega) &= \int^{\infty}_{-\infty}x(t-t_0)e^{-j\omega t}dt \\
&= \int^{\infty}_{-\infty}x(u)e^{-j\omega(u+t_0)}du \\
&= e^{-j\omega t_0} \int^{\infty}_{-\infty}x(u)e^{-j\omega u}du \\
&= e^{-j\omega t_0}X(\omega),
\end{align}$$
where $u=t+t_0$ is a change of variables.

#### (*Frequency Shift*) $X(\omega - \omega_0) \iff x(t)e^{-j\omega_0 t}$.

+the special case $x(t)=e^{j\omega_0 t} \iff X(\omega)=2\pi \delta(\omega-\omega_{0})$

#### (*Convolution*) $y(t) = (x * h)(t) \iff Y(\omega) = X(\omega) H(\omega)$.
#### (*Modulation*) $y(t) = x(t) \cdot c(t) \iff Y(\omega) = \frac{1}{2\pi}(X * C)(\omega)$.
$$\begin{align}
Y(\omega) &= \int^{\infty}_{-\infty}y(t)e^{-j\omega t}dt \\
&=\int^{\infty}_{-\infty} x(t)c(t)e^{-j\omega t}dt \\
&= \int^{\infty}_{-\infty}c(t) \left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty} X(\lambda)e^{j\lambda t}d\lambda\right) e^{-j\omega t}dt \\
&= \frac{1}{2\pi}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty} X(\lambda)\left(\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}c(t)e^{j(\omega - \lambda) t}dt\right)d\lambda \\
&= \frac{1}{2\pi}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty} X(\lambda)C(\omega - \lambda)d\lambda \\
&= \frac{1}{2\pi} (X * C)(\omega),
\end{align}$$
where the two integrals are swapped in order to isolate the coefficient $C(\omega - \lambda)$.

#### (*Differentiation*) $\frac{d^k}{dt^k}x(t) \iff (j\omega)^kX(\omega )$.
#### (*Integration*) $\int x(t)dt = \frac{1}{j\omega}X(\omega )$
