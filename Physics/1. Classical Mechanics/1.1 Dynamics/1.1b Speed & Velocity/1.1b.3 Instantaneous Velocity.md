>[!note] Instantaneous Velocity
>The **instantaneous velocity** at any moment is defined as the [[1.1b.1 Average Velocity|average velocity]] over an infinitesimally short time interval. We can write the definition of instantaneous velocity, $\vec{v}$, for $n$-dimensional motion as
>$$\vec{v} = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0}\frac{\Delta \vec{r}}{\Delta t}=\frac{d \vec{r}}{dt}.$$
>where $\Delta \vec{r}$ is the [[1.1a Displacement|displacement]] of the object, and $\Delta t$ is the (infinitesimal) time elapsed.
>
>Then, the instantaneous speed of the object is simply $s = |\vec{v}|$.

# Intuition
Instantaneous velocity captures an object's velocity before it has time to change its speed or direction.
