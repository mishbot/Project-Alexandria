>[!note] Yield Curves
>[[2.1a.1 Bonds|Bond yields]] can be plotted against their respective maturities to create a **yield curve**, which graphically represents a snapshot of the term structure of interest rates at a given point in time. The difference between yield curves is known as the **credit spread**, and quantifies the risk of a bond given a risk-free benchmark:
>
>```tikz
>\usepackage{tikz}
>\usepackage{amsmath}
>\usetikzlibrary{arrows.meta, decorations.pathreplacing}
>
>\begin{document}
>
>\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.9, >=stealth]
>
>  % Axes
>  \draw[->] (0,0) -- (6,0) node[right] {Maturity (Years)};
>  \draw[->] (0,0) -- (0,5) node[above] {Yield (bp)};
>  
>  \draw[thick] (0.5,0.8) arc (150:105:8cm and 8cm) node[right]{Corporate Bond};
>  \draw[thick] (0.5,0.4) arc (140:106:10cm and 6cm) node[right]{Treasury Bond};
>  
>  % Credit Spread
>  \draw[dashed] (3.4, 0) -- (3.4, 5) node[at start, below] {$t$};
>  \draw[thick, <->, color=purple] (3.4, 1.8) -- (3.4, 3.7) node[midway, right, yshift=0.25cm] {Credit Spread};
>  
>  % -- X-axis ticks & labels for maturities
>  \foreach \x in {1,2,3,4,5}
>    \draw (\x,0.1) -- (\x,-0.1) node[below] {\x};
>
>  % -- Y-axis ticks & labels (just an example in increments of 1)
>  %    Interpreting each 1 as 20 bp (or your chosen scale).
>  \foreach \y in {1,2,3,4}
>    \draw (0.1,\y) -- (-0.1,\y)
>          node[left] {\pgfmathparse{20*\y}\pgfmathresult};
>
>\end{tikzpicture}
>
>\end{document}
>```
>
>The yield curve of stripped (zero-coupon) bonds is known as the **spot rate** curve, and can be derived via the process of **bootstrapping** from the yield curve. Spot rates, which represent the zero-coupon yield at a certain snapshot of time, are crucial in determining **forward rates** (i.e., the rate which makes an investor indifferent between two or more bonds), such that the $\tau$-period forward rate starting at time $t$ is
>$$f_{t,t+\tau}=\left[\frac{(1+S_{t+\tau})^{t + \tau}}{(1+S_{t})^\tau}\right]^{1/\tau}-1,$$
>for some spot rates $S_{i}$.
