>[!note] Gauss's Law
>This section adds a law about closed surfaces. With enough symmetry it finds fields without slicing integrals. [[3.3.1. Electric Flux|Electric flux]] measures how much field crosses a surface, $\Phi_E = \int \vec E\cdot d\vec A$, with $d\vec A$ pointing outward on a closed surface. [[3.3.2. Gauss's Law|Gauss's law]] sets the flux through any closed surface equal to the enclosed charge divided by $\varepsilon_0$. [[3.3.2a Spherical Symmetry|Spherical symmetry]] covers point charges, uniform balls, and shells. [[3.3.2b Cylindrical Symmetry|Cylindrical symmetry]] gives the line-charge field $\lambda/(2\pi\varepsilon_0 r)$ again. [[3.3.2c Planar Symmetry|Planar symmetry]] gives the sheet field $\sigma/(2\varepsilon_0)$.
>
>The law also locates charge on metals. Inside a [[3.3.3. Conductors in Electrostatic Equilibrium|conductor in electrostatic equilibrium]] the field is zero. Any net charge sits on the surface, and the field right outside is $\sigma/\varepsilon_0$. A [[3.3.3a Conducting Shells|conducting shell]] around a charge $q$ carries $-q$ on its inner wall. Two [[3.3.3b Parallel Conducting Plates|parallel conducting plates]] with opposite charges hold a uniform field $\sigma/\varepsilon_0$ between them and none outside.

# Contents

- [[3.3.1. Electric Flux]]: energy flux from a lamp, the area vector, $\Phi_E = \vec E\cdot\vec A$, curved surfaces, open and closed surfaces, and flux as a line count. **Not taught:** the shadow (projected-area) view of flux, and the dome example.
- [[3.3.2. Gauss's Law]]: $\oint \vec E\cdot d\vec A = Q_{\text{enc}}/\varepsilon_0$, the point-charge derivation, zero flux versus zero field, the conditions for finding $E$, the field-line test, and the step-by-step procedure. **Not taught:** line counts proportional to charge, and the comparison with a $1/r^{2.1}$ force law.
    - [[3.3.2a Spherical Symmetry]]: point charge, uniform ball, thin shell, and a ball whose density depends on $r$. **Not taught:** densities that vary with $r$.
    - [[3.3.2b Cylindrical Symmetry]]: the line charge by a Gaussian can, charged tubes, and charged cylinders. **Not taught:** the fields of charged tubes and cylinders.
    - [[3.3.2c Planar Symmetry]]: the infinite sheet by a pillbox, two sheets by superposition, and a disk as a finite sheet. **Not taught:** the whole note.
- [[3.3.3. Conductors in Electrostatic Equilibrium]]: zero field inside, charge on the surface, the perpendicular surface field $\sigma/\varepsilon_0$, cavities of any shape, and the ice-pail demonstration. **Not taught:** the comparison with a lone charged sheet.
    - [[3.3.3a Conducting Shells]]: charge on the inner and outer walls, the field in each region, and an off-center charge. **Not taught:** a shell with its own net charge, and the even outer charge for an off-center charge.
    - [[3.3.3b Parallel Conducting Plates]]: charge on the inner faces, a uniform field between, zero field outside, and fringing. **Not taught:** the $4d/\sqrt A$ estimate of the fringing region, and plates with unequal charges.
