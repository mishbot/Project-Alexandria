# Dynamics

While [[3. Robot Kinematics|kinematics]] and [[5. Differential Kinematics|differential kinematics]] describe how a robot moves, **dynamics** describes *why*: it relates the forces and torques applied at the joints to the resulting motion of the configuration. For a manipulator, this is the bridge between a control signal sent to the motors and the trajectory the end-effector actually traces.

The classical approach using Newton's laws becomes unwieldy for multi-link mechanisms — it requires an inertial frame, explicit handling of constraint forces, and one $\vec{F}=m\vec{a}$ equation per rigid body in three dimensions. The energy-based **Lagrangian** formulation sidesteps all three problems and is the standard tool in robotics.

### Notes

- [[6.1. Lagrangian Dynamics]] — Generalized coordinates, action and the Lagrangian $L = T - V$, the Euler–Lagrange equations, procedure for deriving equations of motion
- [[6.2. Kinetic Energy of a Rigid Body]] — Decomposition into translational and rotational parts, the inertia tensor $\mathcal{I}$, the generalized inertia matrix $\mathcal{M}$, body-frame twist
