>[!note] Autocorrelation Function (ACF)
>The **autocorrelation function** of a stationary [[2.1. Time Series|time series]] $\{X_t\}$ is the normalized [[2.2.2 Autocovariance Function|autocovariance]], measuring the **linear dependence** between $X_t$ and $X_{t+h}$ on a unitless scale:
>$$\rho_X(h) = \frac{\gamma_X(h)}{\gamma_X(0)} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X_t, X_{t+h})}{\text{Var}(X_t)},$$
>where $\gamma_X(h)$ is the autocovariance at lag $h$ and $\gamma_X(0) = \text{Var}(X_t)$. This is the time series analogue of the [[2.5b Second-Order Moments|correlation coefficient]] $\rho_{X,Y}$, applied to a single series at two different times.
>
>For non-stationary processes, the general form uses two time indices:
>$$\rho_X(s, t) = \frac{\gamma_X(s, t)}{\sqrt{\gamma_X(s, s)\,\gamma_X(t, t)}}.$$
>
>The ACF is one of the most important diagnostic tools in time series analysis: its shape reveals the memory structure and guides model selection (e.g., AR vs. MA vs. ARMA).
>
>>[!warning] ACF Measures Linear Dependence Only
>>Like [[2.5b Second-Order Moments|correlation]], $\rho_X(h) = 0$ means $X_t$ and $X_{t+h}$ are **linearly uncorrelated**, but they may still be dependent in nonlinear ways. For Gaussian processes, uncorrelatedness implies independence; in general, it does not.

# Intuition
The ACF answers: **how strongly does the value at time $t$ predict the value $h$ steps into the future, on a scale from $-1$ to $1$?** A slowly decaying ACF signals **persistence**: the series drifts for long stretches above or below its mean. A sharply truncating ACF (dropping to zero after lag $q$) is the signature of a **moving average** MA$(q)$ process. An ACF that decays geometrically suggests an **autoregressive** AR process.

In practice, the **sample ACF** $\hat\rho(h) = \hat\gamma(h)/\hat\gamma(0)$ is plotted as a **correlogram**, with confidence bands at $\pm 1.96/\sqrt{T}$ (under the null of white noise). Lags where $\hat\rho(h)$ exceeds these bands indicate statistically significant serial dependence.

# Properties
#### (*Normalization*) $\rho_X(0) = 1$ always.
#### (*Boundedness*) $-1 \leq \rho_X(h) \leq 1$ for all $h$, by the [[2.2.2 Autocovariance Function|Cauchy–Schwarz bound]].
#### (*Symmetry*) $\rho_X(h) = \rho_X(-h)$.
#### (*Positive Semi-Definiteness*) The function $\rho_X(h)$ is positive semi-definite: for any $n$, times $t_1, \dots, t_n$, and constants $a_1, \dots, a_n$, $\sum_{i,j} a_i a_j \rho_X(t_i - t_j) \geq 0$.
#### (*White Noise*) If $\{W_t\} \sim \text{WN}(0, \sigma^2)$, then $\rho_W(h) = \begin{cases} 1 & h = 0, \\ 0 & h \neq 0. \end{cases}$
#### (*MA(q) Cutoff*) If $X_t = \sum_{j=0}^q \theta_j W_{t-j}$, then $\rho_X(h) = 0$ for all $|h| > q$. This sharp cutoff is the defining ACF signature of MA processes.
#### (*AR(1) Decay*) If $X_t = \phi X_{t-1} + W_t$ with $|\phi| < 1$, then $\rho_X(h) = \phi^{|h|}$, which decays geometrically.

# Examples
>[!example] ACF of MA(1)
>Let $X_t = W_t + \theta W_{t-1}$ with $\{W_t\} \sim \text{WN}(0, \sigma^2)$.
>>[!example]- Derive the ACF.
>>From the [[2.2.2 Autocovariance Function|autocovariance]] of MA(1):
>>$$\rho_X(h) = \begin{cases} 1 & h = 0, \\ \dfrac{\theta}{1 + \theta^2} & |h| = 1, \\ 0 & |h| \geq 2. \end{cases}$$
>>Note that $|\rho_X(1)| \leq 1/2$ for all $\theta$, since $\frac{\theta}{1+\theta^2}$ is maximized at $\theta = 1$ giving $1/2$. This means an MA(1) process can never have lag-1 correlation exceeding $0.5$ in absolute value.

>[!example] ACF of AR(1)
>Let $X_t = \phi X_{t-1} + W_t$ with $|\phi| < 1$ and $\{W_t\} \sim \text{WN}(0, \sigma^2)$.
>>[!example]- Derive the ACF and describe its behavior.
>>Since $\gamma_X(0) = \frac{\sigma^2}{1 - \phi^2}$ and $\gamma_X(h) = \phi^{|h|} \cdot \gamma_X(0)$ (by recursive substitution), the ACF is
>>$$\rho_X(h) = \phi^{|h|}.$$
>>If $\phi > 0$, the ACF decays monotonically toward zero, indicating positive persistence. If $\phi < 0$, the ACF alternates in sign while decaying in magnitude, indicating oscillatory mean-reversion. The closer $|\phi|$ is to $1$, the slower the decay and the longer the memory.
