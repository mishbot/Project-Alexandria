>[!note] Adaptive Learning Rates
>**Adaptive learning rate algorithms** are extensions of [[6.4. Gradient Descent|gradient descent]] that automatically adjust the learning rate $\eta$ for each parameter during training. Instead of using a single global learning rate, they maintain **individual learning rates** that change over time based on gradient history in order to reduce oscillations and improve convergence by **moving quickly along dimensions where gradients are small**, and **slowing down where they are large**.
>
>Formally, these methods still follow the same update rule,
>$$w_i^{(t)} = w_i^{(t-1)} - \eta_i^{(t)} \, \frac{\partial L}{\partial w_i},$$
>but the learning rate $\eta_i^{(t)}$ now depends on the parameter’s past gradients.
>
>1. **AdaGrad (Adaptive Gradient)** keeps track of the **cumulative sum of squared gradients**, and **divides** the base learning rate **by the square root** of this accumulated quantity:
>$$\eta_{i}^{(t)} \frac{\eta}{\sqrt{r_i^{(t)} + \epsilon}}, \quad \text{such that} \quad r_i^{(t)} = r_i^{(t-1)} + \left( \frac{\partial L}{\partial w_i} \right)^2.$$
>This approach quickly decays learning rate for frequently updated weights, but may shrink $\eta_i$ too much, halting learning over time.
>⠀
>2. **RMSProp** modifies AdaGrad to avoid the problem of vanishing learning rates by **focusing on recent** rather than cumulative gradient history. It replaces the sum of squared gradients with an **exponentially decaying moving average**, such that
>$$r_i^{(t)} = \beta r_i^{(t-1)} + (1 - \beta)\left( \frac{\partial L}{\partial w_i} \right)^2,$$
>Here,$\beta$ (typically around $0.9$) determines how much weight is given to the past. By reacting primarily to recent gradients, RMSProp maintains a stable step size even in non-stationary settings such as recurrent networks or online learning tasks.
>⠀
>3. **Adam (Adaptive Moment Estimation)** builds on RMSProp by incorporating momentum, effectively combining the first and second moments of the gradient. It keeps one moving average of the gradients themselves and another of their squares:
>$$s_i^{(t)} = \beta_1 s_i^{(t-1)} + (1 - \beta_1)\frac{\partial L}{\partial w_i},\qquad r_i^{(t)} = \beta_2 r_i^{(t-1)} + (1 - \beta_2)\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial w_i}\right)^2.$$
