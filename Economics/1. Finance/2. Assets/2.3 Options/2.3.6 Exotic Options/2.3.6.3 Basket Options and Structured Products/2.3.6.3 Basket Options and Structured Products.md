>[!note] Basket Options and Structured Products
>Basket options pay on several assets at once. A **structured product** is a note that combines a bond with options in one security. The investor pays for each feature by giving up interest or by selling options to the issuer inside the note.
>- [[2.3.6.3a Basket Options]] covers worst-of, best-of, rainbow, spread and exchange options. Correlation between the assets is a pricing input.
>- [[2.3.6.3b Autocallables]] covers notes that pay conditional coupons and end early once the index is high. Each coupon is a cash binary, and an unprotected principal holds a short down-and-in put.
>- [[2.3.6.3c Cliquets]] covers sums of capped and floored period returns, built from forward start options.
>- [[2.3.6.3d Principal-Protected Notes]] covers notes that spend the interest on a zero-coupon bond on calls or binaries.
>- [[2.3.6.3e Reverse Convertible Notes]] covers notes that sell a down-and-in put to raise the coupon. They mirror principal-protected notes.
>
>All five notes come from IEOR 221 Module 9 and its discussion section. Module 5 also names principal-protected notes.

# Contents

- [[2.3.6.3a Basket Options]]: worst-of, best-of, rainbow, spread and exchange payoffs, the role of correlation, and the rainbow's special cases. **Not taught:** the best-of plus worst-of identity and the sign of the correlation effect; Margrabe's formula; model prices for worst-of calls.
- [[2.3.6.3b Autocallables]]: the coupon and call rules, three sample paths, coupons as cash binaries, the short down-and-in put in the principal, and the replication of the final payment. The coupon hedge and the replication follow the discussion section.
- [[2.3.6.3c Cliquets]]: forward start options and forward volatility, cliquets as strips of forward start call spreads, reverse cliquets and Napoleons. **Not taught:** the forward start value and the forward volatility formula (Hull); the cliquet value under a constant volatility.
- [[2.3.6.3d Principal-Protected Notes]]: the bond-plus-options construction, the participation rate and its dependence on rates and volatility, the binary version, partial protection and caps. **Not taught:** everything beyond the name; the construction follows Hull.
- [[2.3.6.3e Reverse Convertible Notes]]: the bond plus short put, the par coupon, the mirror image of a principal-protected note, what sets the coupon, and barrier monitoring. **Not taught:** the par coupon; Hull's closed form for the down-and-in put; the discrete-monitoring shift.

# Recommended Path

Read the notes in order. 2.3.6.3a stands alone. The other four build on [[2.3.6.1b Binary Options]] and [[2.3.6.2a Barrier Options]]. Read 2.3.6.3d right before 2.3.6.3e, its mirror image. Readers short on time should read 2.3.6.3b and 2.3.6.3e.
