>[!note] Span
>The **span** of a set of vectors $\mathcal{P}=\left\{\vec{v}_1, \dots, \vec{v}_n\right\} \subseteq \mathcal{R}^n$ is the set of all vectors that can be built using the vectors in $\mathcal{P}$ by scaling and adding them together (i.e., the linear combinations of vectors in $\mathcal{P}$). Formally,
>$$\mathrm{span}(\mathcal{P})=\left\{\sum^{n}_{i=1}\alpha_i \vec{v}_i : \alpha_i \in \mathbb{R}\right\} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n,$$
>where $\alpha_{i}$ is the scaling factor (or weight) of each vector in $\mathcal{P}$.
>
>If $\mathcal{P}$ contains $k$ linearly independent vectors, then the dimension $d$ of its span is $n \geq d \geq k$. Note that the maximum amount of linearly independent vectors in $\mathbb{R}^n$ is $n$.
>>[!warning] Infinite Property
>>All nonzero vectors in an infinite vector space have an infinitely extending span.
