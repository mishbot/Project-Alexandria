> [!note] Particle Filtering (Approximate Filtering for HMMs)
> **Particle filtering** is an approximate inference algorithm for **Hidden Markov Models** that replaces exact belief tables with a finite set of samples ("particles") when the state space is too large or continuous for the forward algorithm to be tractable. While the [[6.4.3. Forward Algorithm|forward algorithm]] computes the exact filtering distribution $P(X_t \mid e_{1:t})$ by summing over all states, particle filtering approximates this same distribution using sampling.
> 
> Instead of storing a full probability distribution over states, particle filtering maintains a list of $n$ particles, where each particle represents a possible value of the hidden state at time $t$. The belief distribution is represented empirically: the probability of a state is proportional to the fraction of particles occupying that state. This approximation is effective when $n \ll d$, where $d$ is the size of the state space, yet still large enough to capture the dominant modes of the distribution.
> 
> Particle filtering proceeds iteratively and mirrors the structure of the forward algorithm, consisting of a **time elapse update** followed by an **observation update** at each timestep. During the **time elapse update**, each particle is propagated forward by sampling from the transition model $P(X_{t+1}\mid X_t)$. This step is analogous to prior sampling in Bayes nets: the transition dynamics are reflected in how particles spread across states.
> 
> During the **observation update**, particles are weighted according to how well they explain the newly observed evidence. For a particle in state $x_t$ and observation $e_t$, the particle is assigned a weight proportional to the sensor likelihood $P(e_t\mid x_t)$. These weights are then aggregated by state and normalized to form a probability distribution over states. If all particle weights are zero, the algorithm reinitializes the particles, reflecting a failure to explain the evidence under the current sample set.
> 
> Finally, the algorithm performs a **resampling step**, drawing a new set of particles from the normalized weight distribution. States with higher likelihood under the evidence are more likely to be resampled, concentrating computational effort on high-probability regions of the state space. This resampling step is what allows particle filtering to continually refine its approximation as new evidence arrives.
