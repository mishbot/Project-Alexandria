>[!note] The Normal Force
>**Normal force** is the contact force that acts **perpendicular** to the surface between two objects. It arises when two surfaces are pressed together and prevents them from passing through each other.
>
>When an object rests on a surface, the normal force balances the component of the object’s weight perpendicular to that surface. For an object of mass m resting on a horizontal surface:
>$$\vec{N} + \vec{F}_g = 0 \quad \implies \quad N = mg,$$
>as shown in the following figure:

##### Hidden

>**Gravity** is the **attractive force** exerted by a massive body on another object with mass. Near Earth’s surface, this force causes all objects to accelerate downward at approximately $9.8 \, \text{m/s}^2$, regardless of their mass.
>
>The gravitational force on an object is given by the equation
>$$\vec{F}_G = m \vec{g},$$
>where $m$ is the object’s **mass**, $\vec{g}$ is the **acceleration due to gravity** (typically $\vec{g} = -9.8 \, \text{m/s}^2$), and $\vec{F}_G$ is the **weight** of the object, directed downward toward the center of the Earth.
>
>When an object is in contact with a surface, the surface resists compression by exerting a **normal force**, per [[1.2.1. Newton's Laws|Newton's Third Law]]. Normal Force is denoted as $\vec{F}_N=-\vec{F}_G$, and **always acts perpendicular** to the surface, even on slopes:

>[!example] (Easy) Sliding Blocks
>Two blocks of mass $m_{1}$ and $m_{2}$ are placed on a frictionless ramp with an inclination angle $\theta$, such that $m_{2}$ is below $m_{1}$, and $m_{1} > m_{2}$. The two blocks are then released and slide down.
>>[!example]- Find the acceleration of block $m_{2}$.
>>To proceed, observe $m_{1}$ and $m_2$ as one system. Since $m_{1}>m_{2}$, then both blocks will move together, such that $(m_{1}+m_{2})a=(m_{1}+m_{2})g\sin\theta$. Therefore, $a=g\sin\theta$.
>
>>[!example]- Find the normal force between the two blocks.
>>Now observe only block 1. Per Newton's Second Law, $m_{1}a=m_{1}g\sin\theta-N_{21}$. Substitute for $a$ and obtain
>>$$m_{1}g\sin\theta=m_{1}g\sin\theta-N_{21} \iff N=0.$$
>>Consider why that is the case. As gravity pulls the blocks down the slope, block 1 is not pushing block 2 in a way that creates a normal force. Because both blocks accelerate at the same rate, nothing pushes back on block 1.
