>[!note] Least Squares Optimization
>**Least squares** optimization is a method for finding an approximate solution to a system of equations $A\vec{x}=\vec{y}$ for which $\nexists\vec{y} \in \mathcal{R}(A)$. Formally, given a real matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ and a vector $\vec{y} \in \mathbb{R}^m$, the least squares problem finds (at least one) vector $\vec{x}^* \in \mathbb{R}^n$ such that $A\vec{x}^*$ is the [[10. Projection Theorem|orthogonal projection]] of $\vec{y}$ onto $\mathcal{R}(A)$,
>$$\vec{x}^* \in \arg\min\limits_{\vec{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n}\|A\vec{x} - \vec{y}\|_{2}^2,$$
>where $\vec{x}^*$ is always an optimal solution to the system of normal equations $A^TA\vec{x}=A^T\vec{y}$.
>
>Particularly, if $A$ is in full column rank (i.e., $\operatorname{Rank}(A) = n$), and therefore $Ax=y$ is *overdetermined*, then the least squares solution is given as
>$$\vec{x}^* = (A^TA)^{-1}A^T\vec{y}.$$
>
>Moreover, if $A$ is in full row rank (i.e., $\operatorname{Rank}(A) = m \leq n$), and therefore $A\vec{x}=\vec{y}$ is *undetermined* with infinite solutions, then the least squares solution is defined as the solution of the system with the smallest Euclidean norm, such that
>$$\vec{x}^* = \arg\min\limits_{\vec{x}:A\vec{x}=\vec{y}}\|\vec{x}\|_{2} = A^T(AA^T)^{-1}\vec{y}.$$
>>[!warning] LS and the Pseudoinverse
>>Note that the set of optimal solutions to the least squares problem can be expressed as
>>$$\mathcal{X}_{\text{opt}}=A^\dagger \vec{y} + \mathcal{N}(A),$$
>>where $A^\dagger \vec{y}$ is the minimum-norm point in the optimal set. This means that if $\operatorname{Rank}(A) = \min(m, n)$ (i.e., $A$ is full rank), then the optimal solution $x^* = A^{\dagger}\vec{y}$.
# Proofs
### 1. General Solution
Per the Projection Theorem, we know that given any $y \in \mathbb{R}^m$, there exists some $\bar{y} \in \mathcal{R}(A)$ at minimal distance from $\vec{y}$. We also know that $(y-\bar{y}) \perp \mathcal{R}(A) \equiv (y-\bar{y}) \in \mathcal{N}(A^T)$. As such, for all $y$,
$$A^T(y-\bar{y}) = 0.$$
Furthermore, since $\bar{y} \in \mathcal{R}(A)$, there certainly exists some $x$, such that $Ax = \bar{y}$. Combining the two equations,
$$A^Ty-A^TAx=0 \iff A^TAx=A^Ty.$$
### 2. Full Col. Rank Solution
Since $A^TA$ is invertible for wide matrices of full column rank, we have $x^*=(A^TA)^{-1}A^Ty$.
### 3. Full Row Rank Solution
When a tall matrix $A$ has full row rank $m \leq n$, the rank-nullity theorem implies that the nullspace of $A$ is nontrivial, such that $\text{Nullity}(A) = n-m \geq 1$.

# Variants
#### (*Weighted LS*) For $W = \operatorname{diag}(w_{1}, \ldots, w_{m})$, $\exists\vec{x}^*_{w}=\arg\min\limits_{\vec{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n}\|W(A\vec{x}-\vec{y})\|_{2}^2$, such that $A^TW^TWA\vec{x}_{w}^* = A^TW^TW\vec{y}$.
The proof is simple. We know from the proof of general LS that $x^*$ is the optimal solution to $A^TAx=A^Ty$. We then take $A_{w}=WA$, $y_{w} = Wy$, and substitute for the solution, such that
$$A_{w}^TA_{w}\vec{x}=A_{w}^Ty_{w} \iff A^TW^TWAx=A^TW^TWy.$$
