>[!note] Euler's Formula
>Euler's formula states that for a phase angle $\theta$,
>$$e^{j \theta} = \cos\theta + j\sin\theta.$$
>This theorem is used to convert complex exponentials to rectangular form.

# Properties
#### (Sine Representation) $\sin\theta = \frac{e^{j\theta}-e^{-j\theta}}{2j}$.
We start by expressing $e^{j\theta}=\cos\theta+j\sin\theta$. Then, 
$$
\begin{align}
e^{j\theta}-e^{-j\theta} &= \cos(\theta)+j\sin(\theta)-\cos(-\theta)-j\sin(-\theta) \\ 
&= \cos(\theta)+j\sin(\theta)-\cos(\theta)+j\sin(\theta) \\ 
&=2j\sin\theta.
\end{align}
$$
Therefore, $\sin\theta = \frac{e^{j\theta}+e^{-j\theta}}{2i}$.

#### (Cosine Representation) $\cos\theta = \frac{e^{j\theta}+e^{-j\theta}}{2}$.
We start by expressing $e^{j\theta}=\cos\theta+j\sin\theta$. Then, 
$$
\begin{align}
e^{j\theta}+e^{-j\theta} &= \cos(\theta)+j\sin(\theta)+\cos(-\theta)+j\sin(-\theta) \\ 
&= \cos(\theta)+j\sin(\theta)+\cos(\theta)-j\sin(\theta) \\ 
&=2\cos\theta.
\end{align}
$$
Therefore, $\cos\theta = \frac{e^{j\theta}+e^{-j\theta}}{2}$.
