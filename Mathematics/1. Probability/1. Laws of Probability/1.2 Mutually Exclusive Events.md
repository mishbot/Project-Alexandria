>[!note] Mutually Exclusive Events
>If events $A_1,...,A_N \subseteq \Omega$ in the same [[1.1 Probability Space|probability space]] are disjoint, such that $A_i \cap A_j = \emptyset$ for all $i \neq j$ (i.e. the events do not share common sample points), then by the **Law of Finite Additivity**, the probability that one of the events occurs is equal to the sum of their probabilities,
>$$\mathbb{P}[A_{1} \cup \ldots \cup A_{N}]=P[A_{1}] + \ldots + P[A_{N}].$$
>Such events are called **mutually exclusive**, and cannot occur simultaneously.
# Examples
>[!example] Dice Roll
>A 6-sided dice may have many events. Two of these events are $A = \{ 2, 4, 6 \}$ (i.e. rolling even), and $B = \{ 1, 3, 5 \}$ (i.e. rolling odd). Their intersection is $A \cap B = \emptyset$. Therefore, these events are mutually exclusive; you cannot roll odd and even at the same time.
>
>However, consider the event $C = \{ 3, 6 \}$ (rolling a multiple of 3). Then, $A \cap C = \{ 6 \}$, and $B \cap C = \{ 3 \}$. Therefore, neither $A$ nor $B$ are mutually exclusive to $C$, since you can both roll a multiple of 3 and an even number, or roll a multiple of 3 and an odd number.
