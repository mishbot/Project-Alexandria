>[!note] Stochastic Calculus
>Stochastic calculus is calculus for quantities that move randomly in continuous time, such as stock prices and interest rates. Their paths are too rough for ordinary derivatives, so the usual integral and chain rule need corrections. The section builds the tools in order. [[7.1 Generalized Wiener Process]] builds the basic random motion $W_t$ from small normal steps with standard deviation $\sqrt{\Delta t}$, then adds a constant trend and scale. [[7.2 Quadratic Variation]] shows that the squared steps of $W_t$ add up to the elapsed time, written $(dW_t)^2 = dt$. Every correction that follows comes from this fact. An [[7.3 Ito Process|Itô process]] lets the trend and the scale depend on the current value and on time. The [[7.4 Ito Integral|Itô integral]] adds up gains from holdings chosen before each move, and [[7.5 Ito's Lemma|Itô's lemma]] is the chain rule, with the extra term $\tfrac{1}{2}b^2\,\partial^2 G/\partial x^2\,dt$.
>
>Two standard models follow. [[7.6 Geometric Brownian Motion]] is the stock model behind the Black-Scholes-Merton formula. The [[7.7 Ornstein-Uhlenbeck Process]] pulls a quantity back toward a long-run level, as in interest-rate models. The [[7.8 Feynman-Kac Formula]] connects the two ways of pricing a derivative: as the solution of a partial differential equation, and as a discounted expected payoff.
>
>The notes stay at a heuristic level: increments $\Delta W = \varepsilon\sqrt{\Delta t}$, Taylor expansions, and limits of sums. They do not use measure theory. Notes written for IEOR 221 list the modules that covered them in their `modules` property. A warning titled Not Taught in Lecture flags each part that goes beyond the modules.

# Prerequisites

- [[4.6 Brownian Motion]], for the formal properties of $W_t$, written $B_t$ there.
- [[4.4 Random Walks]], for the discrete version of the same motion.
- [[4.5 Martingales]], for fair-game processes. Itô integrals are martingales, and so are discounted prices under risk-neutral probabilities.
- [[3.3 Central Limit Theorem]] and [[3.2 Laws of Large Numbers]], for the limits of sums.
- [[2.3. Partial Derivatives]], for the Taylor expansion behind Itô's lemma.
- [[2.2c.11 Lognormal Distribution]], for the distribution of a stock price under geometric Brownian motion.

# Contents

- [[7.1 Generalized Wiener Process]]: the Wiener process built from normal steps, why the steps scale with $\sqrt{\Delta t}$, and constant drift and diffusion, so that the trend grows like $t$ and the noise like $\sqrt{t}$. **Not taught:** the argument for the power $1/2$.
- [[7.2 Quadratic Variation]]: squared steps add up to the elapsed time, smooth paths versus Wiener paths, the multiplication rules for $dt$ and $dW_t$, why the drift drops out, and realized variance. **Not taught:** the name, the variance of the sum, smooth paths, total variation, the multiplication rules, the quadratic variation of an Itô process, and realized variance.
- [[7.3 Ito Process]]: drift and diffusion that depend on the current value, the stock model, the Euler simulation rule, and the Markov property. **Not taught:** the coefficients read as conditional moments, the Euler rule, and mean reversion.
- [[7.4 Ito Integral]]: the left-endpoint definition, $\int_0^t W\,dW = \tfrac{1}{2}W_t^2 - \tfrac{1}{2}t$, and the contrast with the Stratonovich integral. **Not taught:** the mean and variance of an Itô integral, its martingale property, the proof and the normal distribution of $\int_0^t s\,dW_s$, and the general integral form of an Itô process.
- [[7.5 Ito's Lemma]]: the chain rule for Itô processes, its heuristic derivation, and worked uses. **Not taught:** the weighted-sum argument and the shortcut $(dx)^2 = b^2\,dt$.
- [[7.6 Geometric Brownian Motion]]: the solution $S_t = S_0\exp\big((\mu - \sigma^2/2)t + \sigma W_t\big)$, its moments, and the distribution of returns. **Not taught:** the median and the chance of ending above the mean, the empirical evidence against the model, and the Feynman-Kac reason behind its risk-neutral version.
- [[7.7 Ornstein-Uhlenbeck Process]]: mean reversion, the solution, the stationary distribution, the half-life, the link to AR(1), and the Vasicek rate model. **Not taught:** the whole note.
- [[7.8 Feynman-Kac Formula]]: why a discounted expected payoff solves a pricing equation, and why the PDE route and the risk-neutral route to the Black-Scholes-Merton price agree. **Not taught:** the whole note.

Read 7.8 after those two notes, since it explains why they agree. Read 7.7 before any interest-rate model.
