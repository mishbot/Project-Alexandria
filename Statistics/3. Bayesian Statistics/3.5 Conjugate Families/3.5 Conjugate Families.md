A **conjugate family** is a prior distribution such that the posterior, obtained by Bayes' rule with a particular likelihood, lies in the same parametric family as the prior. Conjugate priors deliver closed-form posterior updates, so Bayesian inference reduces to tracking a few hyperparameters across observations instead of evaluating intractable integrals. They are the first tool to reach for whenever a model's likelihood admits a conjugate partner.

The practical shape of every conjugate update is the same: the prior's hyperparameters decompose into "prior counts" (successes, trials, sum-of-squares, effective sample size), and the posterior's hyperparameters equal the prior's plus the corresponding data counts. This makes the prior interpretable as "pseudo-data" whose influence is diluted as real data accumulate. When enough data is seen, the posterior is dominated by the likelihood and the prior washes out (see [[3.1 Bayesian Framework|Bayesian Framework]]'s *(Prior Washing Out)* property).

# Shared Structure

Every conjugate pair in this subfolder follows the same template. The conjugate prior is chosen so that its density has the same kernel (the $\theta$-dependent part) as the likelihood, viewed as a function of $\theta$. Multiplying prior by likelihood then produces another density in the same family with updated hyperparameters. For the [[3.5.1 Beta-Binomial Model|Beta-Binomial]], both prior and likelihood are products of $\theta^a(1 - \theta)^b$, so the posterior is $\text{Beta}(\alpha + y,\ \beta + n - y)$. For the [[3.5.3 Gamma-Poisson Model|Gamma-Poisson]], both are products of $\lambda^a e^{-b\lambda}$, so the posterior is $\text{Gamma}(\alpha + \sum y_i,\ \beta + n)$. Every example in this folder has the structure

$$\text{posterior hyperparameters} \;=\; \text{prior hyperparameters} \;+\; \text{data summaries}.$$

This is the mechanical reason Bayesian inference under conjugate priors is fast and tractable — the "sum" is a vector of sufficient statistics, and updating the posterior requires only accumulating those statistics as data arrive.

# When Conjugacy Fails

Conjugate priors cover an important but narrow set of models. Logistic regression, Poisson regression with non-log link, Gaussian processes with non-Gaussian noise, and almost all hierarchical models with more than two levels have no conjugate prior. The standard toolkit once conjugacy fails:

1. **Gibbs sampling with conditionally conjugate updates** — the full joint is not conjugate but each full conditional is. See [[3.11.2 Gibbs Sampling]].
2. **Data augmentation** — introduce latent variables that restore conjugacy of the conditional updates. See probit regression in the Gibbs note.
3. **Laplace approximation** — approximate the posterior by a Gaussian around the MAP. See [[3.9 Laplace Approximation]].
4. **Metropolis-Hastings** — direct sampling without any conjugacy; applies to arbitrary posteriors.
