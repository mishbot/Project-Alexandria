>[!note] Reversibility of Markov Chains
>An irreducible Markov chain $(X_{n})_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ with stationary distribution $\pi^*$ some $Y_{N-n}$, such that $X_{n}=Y_{N-n}$ for all $n=1,\ldots,N$. The reverse transition probabilities $\hat{P}(x_{n+1},x_{n})$ of $Y_{N-n}$ are defined via the **detailed balance equation**, such that
>$$\hat{P}(x_{n+1}, x_{n})=\frac{\pi^*(x_{n})P(x_{n}, x_{n+1})}{\pi^*(x_{n+1})},$$
>where the equality $\hat{P}(x_{n+1}, x_{n})=P(x_{n+1}, x_{n})$ must hold for the chain to be reversible. Note that $y_{n}=x_{n+1}$ and $y_{n+1}=x_{n}$ holds for all $n$.
>
>>[!warning] DBE Generality
>>Note that this is the *local* (and stronger) form of the detailed balance equation. The equation above can be applied to any Markov chain in order to get its reversed form (although a Markov chain is only reversible if it is equal to its reverse!).
