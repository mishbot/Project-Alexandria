> [!note] Likelihood Weighting
> **Likelihood Weighting** is an approximate inference method for Bayes Nets that avoids sample rejection entirely by **fixing evidence variables to their observed values** and weighting each sample by how likely the evidence is given the sampled variables.
> 
> Each sample is generated as follows:
>- Non-evidence variables are sampled normally from their CPTs.  
>- Evidence variables are not sampled; instead, the sample receives a **weight**
>$$w = \prod_i P(e_i \mid \text{Parents}(E_i)),$$
>where the weight is only decided by the likelihoods of evidence variables (hence likelihood weighting).
>
> The posterior is estimated by **weighted counts**:
>$$P(Q \mid e) \approx \frac{\sum_j w_j \mathbf{1}[Q=q]}{\sum_j w_j}.$$
>
> This ensures that **every CPT participates** in the probability calculation, correcting the bias introduced by fixing evidence.
> 
> **Advantage:** significantly more efficient than prior or rejection sampling when evidence is rare.
> **Limitation:** weights can become extremely small when there is a long chain of evidence, leading to high variance.
