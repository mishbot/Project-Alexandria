>[!note] Probability Density Function (PDF)
>The probability [[2.2.1. Probability Distribution|distribution]] of a continuous random variable $X$ is fully described by a non-negative **probability density function (PDF)** $f_{X}: \mathcal{X} \to [0,\infty)$, which illustrates the density around each value $x \in \mathcal{X}$. In particular, the probability that the value of $X$ falls within an interval $[a, b]$ over the real line is given as
>$$\mathbb{P}(a \leq X \leq b)=\int^{b}_{a}f_{X}(x)dx,$$
>and can be interpreted as the area under the graph of the PDF.
>
>The cumulative probability that $X$ takes on a value less than or equal to $x$ in the PDF is given by the non-decreasing **cumulative distribution function (CDF)** $F_X: \mathbb{R} \to [0,1]$ , formally defined as
>$$F_{X}(x)=\mathbb{P}(X \leq x)=\int^{x}_{-\infty}f_{X}(t)dt,$$
>implying that the PDF is the rate of change of the CDF, $f_{X}(x)=\frac{d}{dx}F_{X}(x)$.
>
>>[!warning] PDFs Measure Density
>>For an individual point $x$, $\mathbb{P}(X = x) = \int^{x}_{x}f_{X}(x)dx = 0$. However, an alternative to this is to select the interval $[x, x + \delta]$ for a very small length $\delta$, and compute
>>$$\int^{x+\delta}_{x}f_{X}(x)dx \approx f_{X}(x) \cdot \delta.$$
