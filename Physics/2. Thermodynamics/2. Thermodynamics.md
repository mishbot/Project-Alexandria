>[!note] Thermodynamics
>Thermodynamics describes systems of $10^{22}$ particles with a handful of macroscopic variables: pressure, volume, temperature, particle number, internal energy, and entropy. The subject rests on two ideas. Temperature is a measure of the average energy per particle. That idea is the content of [[2.1 Temperature|section 2.1]], of the [[2.2 Ideal Gas|ideal gas law]] $PV = NkT$ in section 2.2, and of the [[2.3 Kinetic Theory of Gases|kinetic theory]] behind it in section 2.3. Energy is conserved, which becomes the first law once [[2.4 Heat and Internal Energy|heat and internal energy]] are defined in section 2.4 and work is added in [[2.5 First Law of Thermodynamics|section 2.5]]. [[2.6 Heat Transfer|Section 2.6]] describes how heat moves by conduction, convection, and radiation.
>
>The [[2.7 Second Law of Thermodynamics|second law]] in section 2.7 adds a direction to the bookkeeping. Heat flows from hot to cold on its own and never the reverse, no engine turns heat entirely into work, and the entropy of a system together with its surroundings never decreases. The Carnot efficiency $1 - T_L / T_H$ is the ceiling on every engine, and entropy is the state function that enforces it.
>
>These notes follow PHYS 7B at Berkeley, lectures 1 to 11 of Fall 2026, and Giancoli chapters 17 to 20. They write $N k$ rather than $nR$, count accessible degrees of freedom with $d$, and write internal energy as $E_{\text{int}}$. Pressure is capital $P$. Each note lists the lectures that covered it in its `lectures` property. A warning titled Not Taught in Lecture flags each part that lecture skipped. The [[Thermo Lecture Coverage Audit|coverage audit]] maps every lecture to its notes.

# Prerequisites

- [[1.3 Work and Mechanical Energy]], for work, kinetic energy, and energy conservation.
- [[1.7 Fluid Mechanics]], for pressure and density.
- [[2.2c.1 Probability Density Function]], for the speed distribution in section 2.3.

# Contents

- [[2.1 Temperature]]: atoms, temperature scales, the zeroth law, and thermal expansion.
- [[2.2 Ideal Gas]]: the gas laws, absolute temperature, $PV = NkT$, and Avogadro's number.
- [[2.3 Kinetic Theory of Gases]]: pressure from molecular motion, $\tfrac{1}{2} m \overline{v^2} = \tfrac{3}{2} kT$, the rms speed, the Maxwell distribution, and equipartition.
- [[2.4 Heat and Internal Energy]]: internal energy as a state function, heat as energy in transit, specific heat, latent heat, and calorimetry.
- [[2.5 First Law of Thermodynamics]]: work as area on the $P$-$V$ diagram, $\Delta E_{\text{int}} = Q - W$, the four standard processes, and molar specific heats.
- [[2.6 Heat Transfer]]: conduction, convection, and radiation.
- [[2.7 Second Law of Thermodynamics]]: heat engines, the Otto and Carnot cycles, refrigerators, reversibility, and entropy.

# Recommended Path

Read the sections in order, and the notes within each section in order. The numbering is a reading order: no note uses a result that a later note introduces. Sections 2.1 to 2.3 build the molecular picture of temperature and can be read quickly by anyone who has seen the ideal gas law. Sections 2.4 and 2.5 are the core of the course and carry most of the problem types. Section 2.6 stands on its own. Section 2.7 uses the processes of 2.5 on every page and should be read last.
