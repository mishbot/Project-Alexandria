>[!note] Market Noise
>Noise in finance refers to volatility generated by market participants with imperfect information, not driven by fundamentals; it includes non-informational order flow (liquidity/hedging needs, constraints, mechanical rebalancing), sentiment and attention shocks, and misinterpretation or overreaction to signals, all of which can push prices away from intrinsic value. 
>
> In reality, prices reflect both the asset’s intrinsic value, as well as noise due to **noise trading**, which is trading on noise _as if it were information_. As such, noise is defined in contrast to information: it is what makes observations imperfect and keeps investors from cleanly inferring expected returns or fundamentals. 
> 
> Noise trading happens because extracting information from a noisy environment is hard, so investors can mistake noise for signal. Reasons noise trading arises include:
> - **Overconfidence (overprecision):** investors overestimate the precision of their own signals (and/or underestimate others'), so they put too much weight on the noisy part of what they observe and end up trading "on noise."
> - **Noise is required for liquidity:** without noise traders, there may be very little trade, because purely informational trade tends to get competed away (no-trade logic). Noise trading supplies volume and makes markets liquid. 
> - **Limits to arbitrage:** even if some investors suspect prices contain noise, taking larger positions increases risk; it is hard to tell skill from luck and hard to know if one is trading on information or noise, so information trading does not eliminate noise trading. 
> - **Social forces amplify noise:** in fast, crowded markets, communication frictions and limited trust/relationships can fragment trading into cliques, producing dispersion in quotes and higher volatility even with more participants.
