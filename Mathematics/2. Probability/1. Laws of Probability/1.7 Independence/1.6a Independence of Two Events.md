>[!note] Pairwise Independence
>Two events $A, B \subseteq \Omega$ in the same [[1.1 Probability Space|probability space]] are said to be **pairwise independent**, if their joint probability is equal to the product of their marginal probabilities, such that
>$$\mathbb{P}[A \cap B] = \mathbb{P}[A] \times \mathbb{P}[B].$$
>Moreover, if $\mathbb{P}[A] > 0$, independence is equivalent to the condition,
>$$\mathbb{P}[B \mid A] = \mathbb{P}[B],$$
>and vice-versa.

# Intuition
Consider the law of conditional probability, which states that, given events $A,B \in \Omega$, the probability of $B$ given $A$ is $\mathbb{P}[B \mid A] = \frac{\mathbb{P}[A \cap B]}{\mathbb{P}[A]}$. However, if $B$ is not updated given the information that $A$ has occurred, then equivalently, $\mathbb{P}[B \mid A] = \mathbb{P}[B]$. Then, $A$ and $B$ are **independent**, and in order for the equivalence to hold, $\mathbb{P}[A \cap B]=\mathbb{P}[A] \times \mathbb{P}[B]$.

We adopt the latter definition of independence, since it can be used even when $\mathbb{P}[A]=0$, where $\mathbb{P}[B \mid A]$ is undefined.
