>[!note] Heat and Internal Energy
>This section separates three ideas that everyday language runs together. [[2.4.1 Internal Energy|Internal energy]] is the energy a body holds in the motion and arrangement of its molecules. It is a state function, and for an ideal gas it equals $\tfrac{d}{2} NkT$. [[2.4.2 Heat|Heat]] is energy in transit between bodies at different temperatures, counted positive when it flows into the system. Temperature is the average energy per molecule. A bathtub of warm water has less temperature and more internal energy than a cup of boiling water.
>
>The remaining notes are the tools for solids and liquids. [[2.4.3 Specific Heat|Specific heat]] gives $Q = mc\,\Delta T$ within one phase and explains why that formula fails for gases. [[2.4.4 Latent Heat|Latent heat]] handles melting and boiling, where heat enters without any change in temperature. [[2.4.5 Calorimetry|Calorimetry]] applies energy conservation to bodies in contact by setting the sum of all heat flows, including the latent heat terms, to zero.

# Contents

- [[2.4.1 Internal Energy]]: the state function, $E_{\text{int}} = \tfrac{d}{2} NkT$ for ideal gases, and $\Delta E_{\text{int}} = \tfrac{d}{2}\,\Delta(PV)$. **Not taught:** the zero of internal energy as a convention.
- [[2.4.2 Heat]]: heat as energy transfer, the sign convention, calories and joules, and Joule's experiment. **Not taught:** calories, Btu, and Joule's experiment.
- [[2.4.3 Specific Heat]]: $Q = mc\,\Delta T$, heat capacity $C = mc = \rho V c$, the heat capacity $nC_V$ of a gas sample, the Dulong-Petit rule, and why gases need a process.
- [[2.4.4 Latent Heat]]: $Q = mL$, the heating curve, coexistence at the transition temperature, and evaporative cooling. **Not taught:** how pressure shifts the melting and boiling points.
- [[2.4.5 Calorimetry]]: the sum-to-zero method, the weighted average, the three outcomes when ice meets water, and mixing ideal gases. **Not taught:** mixing gases by internal energy, the container's share of the heat, and losses to the room.

# Recommended Path

Read the notes in order. [[2.4.1 Internal Energy]] and [[2.4.2 Heat]] set up the distinction between a state quantity and a transfer. [[2.4.3 Specific Heat]] and [[2.4.4 Latent Heat]] give the two formulas, and [[2.4.5 Calorimetry]] combines them.
