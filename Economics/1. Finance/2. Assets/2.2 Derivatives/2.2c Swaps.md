>[!note] Swaps
>A swap is an OTC derivative in which two parties agree to exchange a stream of cash flows over multiple future dates. It can be interpreted as a series of [[2.2a Forward Contracts|forward contracts]] for each cash flow leg that are calculated differently and netted,  so that only the difference changes hands on each settlement date.
>
>There are many types of swaps, including uncommon (exotic) swaps. Popular swaps include:
>- **Interest-Rate Swaps,** in which two parties exchange a series of fixed‑rate and floating‑rate interest payments on a notional principal over a specified term.
>- **Credit Default Swaps**, where one party pays a periodic fee in exchange for protection ("insurance") against the default of a specified borrower or bond.
>- **Currency Swaps**, where parties exchange principal and interest payments in different currencies over a set period.
>- **Commodity swaps**, where parties exchange cash flows based on the price of a specified commodity over a set period.
>
>In all swaps, one party typically pays the spot (floating) rate, whereas another pays a rate fixed by the swap contract. However, total floating swaps or fixed swaps are common currency swaps.

# Examples
>[!example] I. Interest Rate Swaps and Comparative Advantage
>Suppose AAACorp and BBBCorp are able to obtain the following borrowing rates for 5-year $100M loans from their respective bankers: 
>
>|      | Fixed Rate |      Floating Rate       |
| :--: | :--------: | :----------------------: |
| AAA  |  $4.00\%$  | $\text{Floating}-0.1\%$ |
| BBBB |  $5.20\%$  | $\text{Floating}+0.6\%$  |
>
>The table above shows that AAA has a comparative advantage in fixed rate borrowing, whereas BBB has a comparative advantage in floating rate borrowing.
>
>Suppose further that AAACorp would prefer to pay a floating rate, whereas BBBCorp would prefer to pay a fixed rate. A banker with relationships with both companies notices this, and contemplates the flowing Interest Rate Swap:
>$$\xleftarrow{4\%}\boxed{\text{AAA}}_{\xrightarrow{\text{Floating}}}^{\xleftarrow{4.35\%}}\boxed{\text{BBB}}\xrightarrow{\text{Floating + 0.6\%}}$$
>
>**A)** What would the net effect of the cash flows be for AAACorp, and how does this compare to its direct marketplace opportunity for floating-rate borrowing?
>>[!example]- A) Solution.
>>Since AAA borrows at $4\%$ and is paid at $4.35\%$, AAA's net gain is $0.35\%$. It pays BBB the floating rate, which means that it net pays $\text{Floating}-0.35\%$, which is 25 basis points better than the direct-market price.
>
>**B)** What would the net effect of the cash flows be for BBBCorp, and how does this compare to its direct marketplace opportunity for fixed-rate borrowing?
>>[!example]- B) Solution.
>>Since BBB directly borrows at $\text{Floating}+0.6\%$ and is paid the floating rate by AAA, its net interest is $0.6\%$. Since BBB also pays AAA $4.35\%$, this means that it pays $4.35\%+0.6\%=4.95\%$ in total fixed rate, which is 25bp less than the market fixed rate.
>
>Suppose the banker instead envisions this arrangement:
>$$\xleftarrow{4\%}\boxed{\text{AAA}}_{\xrightarrow{\text{Floating}}}^{\xleftarrow{4.33\%}}\boxed{\text{Financial Inst.}}^{\xleftarrow{4.37\%}}_{\xrightarrow{\text{Floating}}}\boxed{BBB}\xrightarrow{\text{Floating + 0.6\%}}$$
>
>**C)** What is the new net effect of the cash flows for AAACorp, and how does this compare to its direct marketplace opportunity for floating-rate borrowing?
>>[!example]- C) Solution.
>>AAA's  outflow is $\text{Floating}+4\%$, whereas its inflow is $4.33\%$. This equates to a net outflow of
>>$$\text{Floating}+4\%-4.33\%=\text{Floating}-0.33\%,$$
>>which is $23$bp better than their market floating rate.
>
>**D)** What is the new net effect of the cash flows for BBBCorp, and how does this compare to its direct marketplace opportunity for floating-rate borrowing?
>>[!example]- D) Solution.
>>BBB's outflow under this scheme is $\text{Floating}+4.97\%$, whereas its inflow is the floating rate. Therefore, its net outflow is $4.97\%$, which is $5.20\%-4.97\%=0.23\%$ (or 23bp) better than their fixed market rate.
>
>**E)** What is the annual fee (in dollars) effectively earned by the banker under this arrangement?
>>[!example]- E) Solution
>>The banker's outflow is $\text{Floating}+4.33\%$, and their inflow is $\text{Floating}+4.37\%$, which means that their net inflow is $(\text{Floating}+4.37\%)-(\text{Floating}+4.33\%)=0.04\%$ or 4bp.
>>
>>Since the loans are both for $\$100\text{M}$, this means that the banker earns $\$40,000$ per year for 5 years with this scheme.
