>[!note] Gradient
>The **gradient** of a scalar-valued function $f: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ is the **vector of partial derivatives** with respect to each component of its input vector $x = [x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n]^T$ that points in the direction of steepest ascent at $x$:
>$$\nabla f(x) = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_1} & \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_2} & \ldots & \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_n} \end{bmatrix}^T \in \mathbb{R}^n.$$
>
>The magnitude of the gradient $\|\nabla f(x)\|$ gives the **rate of increase** in the direction of $x$. It is important to note that $f(x)=0$ indicates a **stationary point**, and not necessarily an extremum.
>
>>[!warning] The Gradient Lives in the Domain
>>The gradient doesn’t lie on the surface of the function - it lives in the **domain**, not the output space.
>>
>>For a function $E:\mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$, the gradient $\nabla E:\mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ is **2-dimensional**, describing direction and rate of change within the input plane, not in the 3D surface of $E(w)$.
