>[!note] Classification Metrics
>Once a classification model is built, its effectiveness is evaluated using quantities derived from the **confusion matrix**, which summarizes prediction outcomes in terms of true/false positives/negatives, such that false positives are negative cases incorrectly predicted as positive, and vice-versa. From these four outcomes, several key metrics are defined:
>- **Accuracy** is proportion of all predictions that are correct:
>$$\text{Accuracy} = \frac{TP + TN}{TP + TN + FP + FN}.$$
>- **Precision** is the proportion of predicted positives that are actually positive:
>$$\text{Precision} = \frac{TP}{TP + FP}.$$
>- **Recall** (True Positive Rate) is the proportion of actual positives that were correctly identified:
>$$\text{Recall} = \frac{TP}{TP + FN}.$$
>- **False Positive Rate** is the proportion of actual negatives misclassified as positives:
>$$\text{FPR} = \frac{FP}{FP + TN}.$$
>- **F1 Score** is the harmonic mean of precision and recall, balancing both measures:
>$$F_1 = 2 \cdot \frac{\text{Precision} \cdot \text{Recall}}{\text{Precision} + \text{Recall}}$$
>
>These metrics can be used in tandem to determine a classification threshold for binary classifiers. For example,
>- **Precision–Recall Tradeoff:** Precision ($y$) and recall ($x$) can be plotted for all thresholds $\theta \in [0, 1]$ to observe how improving one often reduces the other. The balance point depends on the application’s tolerance for false positives and false negatives.
>⠀
>- **Maximum** $F_1$ **Threshold:** The threshold that maximizes $F_1$ represents the best balance between precision and recall when both are equally important.
>⠀
>- **ROC Curve and AUC:** The ROC curve is constructed by **sweeping the classification threshold** from 0 to 1 and plotting the **TPR/Recall** ($y$) against the **FPR** ($x$) to show how sensitivity and false alarm rates change as the cutoff varies. The **Area Under the Curve (AUC)** summarizes overall separability: an AUC of 1 indicates perfect classification, while 0.5 corresponds to random guessing.
>
>Choosing the optimal classification threshold depends on the tradeoff between false positives and false negatives. When classes are balanced, the threshold is typically set to maximize accuracy (often near $0.5$), while for imbalanced data or when positive predictions matter more, it is chosen to maximize the $F_1$ score. Accuracy and $F_1$ are threshold-dependent, whereas AUC-based metrics are not.
