>[!note] Derivatives
>A derivative is a contract whose value at expiry is set by the price of an underlying asset. [[2.2.1 Contingent Claim]] defines the idea and the payoffs of the basic contracts. The section then covers each family of contracts:
>- [[2.2.2 Forwards]] are agreements to buy or sell at a fixed price on a fixed date. The folder shows how no arbitrage sets that price and the value of an existing contract, for stocks, currencies, and interest rates.
>- [[2.2.3 Futures]] are forwards traded on an exchange and settled every day. [[2.2.4 Cash-and-Carry Arbitrage]] is the trade that ties futures and forward prices to the spot price, with and without trading costs.
>- [[2.2.5 Swaps]] exchange two streams of cash flows: interest rates, currencies, total returns, or default protection.
>
>Options are derivatives too. They give the right, but not the obligation, to trade at a fixed price. They are studied so widely that they have their own section, [[2.3 Options]].
>
>Notes written for IEOR 221 list the modules that covered them in their `modules` property. A warning titled Not Taught in Lecture flags each part that goes beyond the modules.
