>[!note] Electric Current
>This section follows charge as it moves through a conductor. An [[3.6.1. Electric Current|electric current]] is the rate at which charge crosses a surface, $I = dQ/dt$. Its direction is the direction positive charge would move, so electrons in a metal move against it. In electrostatics the field inside a conductor is zero, so a current needs a supply that keeps a field inside. The carriers collide with atoms and move at a constant average [[3.6.2. Current Density|drift velocity]]. The current density $\vec J = nq\vec v_d$ points along $\vec E$ for carriers of either sign, and $\vec J = \sigma\vec E$ with $\sigma$ the conductivity.
>
>The [[3.6.3. Resistivity|resistivity]] $\rho = 1/\sigma$ is a material property, and in metals it rises with temperature. A uniform wire has resistance $R = \rho L/A$. [[3.6.4. Ohm's Law|Ohm's law]] holds when $\rho$ does not depend on the field. Then $V_{ab} = IR$ for an object of any shape, and $R$ follows from slice and dice or from the field. A device takes in [[3.6.5. Electric Power|electric power]] $P = IV_{ab}$, and a resistor turns $I^2R = V_{ab}^2/R$ into heat.

# Prerequisites

- [[3.1.2. Conservation of Charge]], for the same current through every cross-section.
- [[3.3.1. Electric Flux]], for current as the flux of $\vec J$.
- [[3.3.3. Conductors in Electrostatic Equilibrium]], for the zero field inside a conductor that carries no current.
- [[3.4.3. Electric Potential from Electric Field]], for integrating a field into a potential difference.

# Contents

- [[3.6.1. Electric Current]]: $I = dQ/dt$, the ampere, the direction of current, a conductor between plates, the pachinko picture, and the same current along a wire. **Not taught:** nothing.
- [[3.6.2. Current Density]]: drift velocity, $I = n|q|v_dA$, $\vec J = nq\vec v_d$, current as the flux of $\vec J$, $\vec J$ along $\vec E$ for either sign, $\vec J = \sigma\vec E$, and the salt-water demonstration. **Not taught:** numerical drift speeds in metals, and why a lamp lights at once.
- [[3.6.3. Resistivity]]: $\rho = 1/\sigma$, $\vec E = \rho\vec J$, resistance and the ohm, $R = \rho L/A$, units, and the temperature demonstrations. **Not taught:** the table of resistivities, and the linear temperature model.
- [[3.6.4. Ohm's Law]]: when Ohm's law holds, $V_{ab} = IR$ for any shape, the resistor symbol, the sign of $V_{ab}$, slice and dice against the field method, and the spherical shell resistor. **Not taught:** the lamp filament and its curved graph of current against potential difference, and the cylindrical shell resistor.
- [[3.6.5. Electric Power]]: $P = IV_{ab}$ for any device, $I^2R$ and $V_{ab}^2/R$, power ratings, and the fuse demonstration. **Not taught:** the kilowatt-hour.

# Recommended Path

Read [[3.6.1. Electric Current]] and [[3.6.2. Current Density]] first. They set up the picture of carriers drifting through a material. [[3.6.3. Resistivity]] finds $R$ for a straight wire. [[3.6.4. Ohm's Law]] needs the most practice, mainly in finding $R$ for shapes other than a straight wire. Its spherical shell resistor is the main exercise. [[3.6.5. Electric Power]] is short. Read it before [[3.7. DC Circuits]], which adds sources and networks of resistors.
