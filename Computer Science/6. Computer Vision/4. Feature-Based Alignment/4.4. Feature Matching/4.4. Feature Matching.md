>[!note] Feature Matching
>**Feature matching** pairs [[4.3. Feature Description|descriptors]] across two or more images to produce *correspondences* — pixel-to-pixel links between views of the same physical point. It is the last stage of the detect-describe-match pipeline and feeds every downstream geometry estimator: [[3.4. Homographies|homography fitting]], [[5.3. Epipolar Geometry|epipolar geometry]], [[6.1. Structure from Motion (SfM)|SfM]], and visual SLAM.
>
>The standard recipe:
>1. **Nearest-neighbor search.** For each descriptor in image 1, find the closest descriptor in image 2 by Euclidean (or Hamming, for binary descriptors) distance. Efficient implementations use KD-trees or approximate-NN libraries (FLANN).
>2. **Ratio test.** Keep a match only if the nearest neighbor is distinctly closer than the second-nearest: $d_1 / d_2 < 0.8$ (Lowe's threshold). This rejects ambiguous matches that arise from repeated texture and descriptor noise.
>3. **Mutual consistency.** Optionally, keep only matches that are nearest-neighbors in both directions.
>4. **Geometric verification** via [[4.4.1. RANSAC|RANSAC]] using the problem-appropriate model (homography, fundamental matrix, or PnP).

# Intuition
Descriptors turn the matching problem into nearest-neighbor search in a high-dimensional space. In principle, one could just pair each descriptor with its closest counterpart and call it done. In practice, that strategy fails: real images contain repeated texture (bricks, windows, foliage), small-scale variation (noise, compression artifacts), and occlusion (keypoints visible in one image but not the other), all of which produce confident-but-wrong matches.

The two lines of defense are the **ratio test** and **RANSAC**. The ratio test is a *per-match* filter: it catches cases where the descriptor itself is ambiguous (the nearest and second-nearest neighbors are both plausible). RANSAC is a *global* filter: it catches cases where the match is locally plausible but geometrically inconsistent with the rest. Together they reduce the outlier rate from $70\text{–}90\%$ of raw matches to a few percent, which is low enough for downstream geometric estimators to succeed.
