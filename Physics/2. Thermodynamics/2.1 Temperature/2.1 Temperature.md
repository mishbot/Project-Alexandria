>[!note] Temperature
>This section sets up the ideas that the rest of thermodynamics uses. The [[2.1.1 Atomic Theory of Matter|atomic theory of matter]] describes every substance as atoms and molecules in constant random motion. That picture connects what instruments measure to what the particles do. [[2.1.2 Temperature Scales|Temperature scales]] turn hot and cold into numbers. The note defines the Celsius, Fahrenheit, and Kelvin scales and the conversions between them.
>
>The [[2.1.3 Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics|zeroth law of thermodynamics]] explains why those numbers mean something. Two systems that are each in thermal equilibrium with a third system are in equilibrium with each other. A thermometer reading therefore gives the temperature of the object it touches. [[2.1.4 Thermal Expansion|Thermal expansion]] is the first measurable effect of a temperature change. Lengths and volumes grow in proportion to the temperature change, which is also how common thermometers work.
