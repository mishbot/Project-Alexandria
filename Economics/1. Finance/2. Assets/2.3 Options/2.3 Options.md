>[!note] Options
>An **option** gives its holder the right, but not the obligation, to buy (a call) or sell (a put) an asset at a fixed strike price $K$. Options are [[2.2 Derivatives|derivatives]], since their payoff is set by the price of an underlying asset ([[2.2.1 Contingent Claim]]). They get their own section because they are central to finance and widely studied. Their payoff is not linear in the asset price, so pricing them needs a model of how the price moves and a hedge that is adjusted over time. The section builds from the contract to its price, its risks and its variants.
>- [[2.3.1 Option Basics]] defines calls and puts and derives the results that need no model: price bounds, early exercise, and put-call parity.
>- [[2.3.2 Option Strategies]] combines several options into one position, such as spreads, straddles and collars.
>- [[2.3.3 Option Pricing]] prices options with binomial trees and with the Black-Scholes-Merton equation and formula.
>- [[2.3.4 Greeks]] measures how an option's price responds to each input and sets the hedge for each risk.
>- [[2.3.5 Volatility]] reads volatility from option prices and shows how a hedged option trades it.
>- [[2.3.6 Exotic Options]] changes the payoff rule: perpetual and binary options, path-dependent options, basket options, and structured notes.
>

# Contents

- [[2.3.1 Option Basics]]: the option contract, bounds on option prices, early exercise, and put-call parity.
- [[2.3.2 Option Strategies]]: spreads, collars, straddles, strangles, butterflies, and calendar spreads, with a rule for choosing among them.
- [[2.3.3 Option Pricing]]: binomial trees, risk-neutral valuation, delta hedging, and the Black-Scholes-Merton equation and formula.
- [[2.3.4 Greeks]]: delta, gamma, theta, vega, and rho in closed form, the relation among them, and delta-neutral portfolios.
- [[2.3.5 Volatility]]: implied volatility, the volatility surface, and the profit of a delta-hedged option, with variance swaps and the VIX.
- [[2.3.6 Exotic Options]]: perpetual American and binary options, barrier, Asian and lookback options, basket options, autocallables, cliquets, principal-protected notes, and reverse convertible notes.
