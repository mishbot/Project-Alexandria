>[!note] Doppler Effect
>The **Doppler Effect** is the apparent change in frequency or wavelength of a wave due to **relative motion** between the **source** and the **observer**. When the source and observer move toward each other, the observed frequency increases (pitch sounds higher); when they move apart, the observed frequency decreases (pitch sounds lower).
>
>This effect occurs for **all types of waves** - **sound**, **light**, and **water waves** - though the formulas differ for mechanical and electromagnetic waves.
>
>For a sound wave, the observed frequency is given as
>$$f’ = \left( \frac{v \pm v_o}{v \pm v_s} \right) f_s,$$
>where $f_{s}$ is the source frequency, $v$ is the speed of sound in the medium, $v_{o}$ is the speed of the observer (positive towards the source), and $v_{s}$ is the speed of the source emitting the sound (positive away from observer). Intuitively:
>- If the **observer moves toward** the source, wave compressions are encountered more often, and therefore $f’$ increases.
>- If the **source moves toward** the observer, wave compressions again are encountered more often, therefore wavelength decreases and $f’$ increases.
>- If either moves away, $f’$ decreases because compressions are farther apart or encountered less frequently.
