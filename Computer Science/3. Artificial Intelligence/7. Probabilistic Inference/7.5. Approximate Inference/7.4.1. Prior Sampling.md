> [!note] Prior Sampling
> **Prior Sampling** is the simplest approximate inference method for Bayes Nets. It estimates posterior probabilities by **sampling full assignments from the joint distribution** defined by the network and then counting outcomes consistent with the query.
> 
> The goal is to approximate
>$$P(Q \mid e) \approx \frac{\#\{Q=q, e\}}{\#\{e\}}$$
> by generating many samples from the model.
> 
> Sampling proceeds in **topological order** of the Bayes Net:
>- For each variable $X_i$, sample a value from $P(X_i \mid \text{Parents}(X_i))$.  
>- This produces a complete assignment $(x_1,\dots,x_n)$.
>
> All samples are treated equally. After generating $N$ samples, the posterior is estimated by relative frequencies.
> 
> **Limitation:** if the evidence $e$ is unlikely, most samples will be inconsistent with $e$ and therefore wasted. As a result, prior sampling can be extremely inefficient when conditioning on rare evidence.
