>[!note] Heat Engines
>This folder works two engine cycles in full with the method of [[2.7.1 Heat Engine]]. Label the vertices, find $Q_H$ and $Q_L$ leg by leg, then compute $e = 1 - Q_L/Q_H$. The [[2.7.4a Otto Cycle|Otto cycle]] models a gasoline engine and takes in heat at constant volume. The [[2.7.4b Carnot Engine|Carnot cycle]] takes in heat on an isotherm and runs reversibly. Its efficiency $e_C = 1 - T_L/T_H$ is the ceiling for every engine between two temperatures.

# Prerequisites

- [[2.7.1 Heat Engine]], for the cycle method and the reservoir diagram.
- [[2.7.3 Reversible Process]], for why the Carnot cycle can run backward.
- [[2.5.3 Thermodynamic Processes]], for the heat on each leg.

# Contents

- [[2.7.4a Otto Cycle]]: the gasoline engine cycle and $e = 1 - r^{1 - \gamma}$. **Not taught:** the diesel comparison.
- [[2.7.4b Carnot Engine]]: the cycle, the derivation of $e_C = 1 - T_L/T_H$, and Carnot's theorem. **Not taught:** the proof of Carnot's theorem and why the Carnot engine is slow.

# Recommended Path

Read [[2.7.4a Otto Cycle]] first as practice with the cycle method. Then read [[2.7.4b Carnot Engine]], which uses the same method and adds the reversibility argument.
