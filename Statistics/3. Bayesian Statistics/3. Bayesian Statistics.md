Bayesian statistics treats unknown parameters as random variables with probability distributions and uses Bayes' rule to combine a prior distribution with a likelihood to yield a posterior. This differs from frequentist inference, which treats parameters as fixed unknowns and reasons about the distribution of estimators over hypothetical repeated sampling. The payoff is a single coherent object — the posterior — that answers every inferential question (point estimates, credible intervals, model comparisons, predictions) by taking expectations or quantiles.

The challenge is computation. Closed-form posteriors exist only for conjugate models; most real problems require numerical methods, which drives the second half of the folder: MCMC, Gaussian processes, and Laplace approximations.
