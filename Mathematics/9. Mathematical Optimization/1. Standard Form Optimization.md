>[!note] Standard Form 
>A standard  optimization problem is typically stated as follows:
>$$\begin{aligned} p^* = \min_\limits{x} \quad & f_{0}(x) \\ \text{subject to:} \quad & f_{i}(x) \leq 0, \quad i=1,\ldots,m, \end{aligned}$$
>where $p^*$ is the **optimal value**, $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the **decision variable**, $f_{0}:\mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ is the **objective function** (or cost), and $f_{i}: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}, \space i = 1,\ldots,k$ are the $k$ optimization **constraints**.
>
>Other than inequality constraints, an optimization problem may have equality constraints $h_{i} = 0$ (equivalently represented via a pair of inequalities $h_{i} \geq 0$ and $h_{i} \le 0$), and set constraints $x \in \mathcal{X}$.
>
>Furthermore, maximization problems can be readily recast as minimization problems, such that
>$$p^* = \max\limits_{x}f_{0}(x) = -\min\limits_{x}-f_{0}(x).$$
>>[!warning] "Subject To" Notation
>>In the above, the term “$\text{subject to:}$” is sometimes replaced with the shorthand “$\text{s.t.:}$,” or simply by colon notation “$\text{:}$”.

# Properties
#### (*Feasible Set*) $\mathcal{X} = \{ x \in \mathbb{R}^n : f_{i}(x) \leq 0 \enspace \forall i = 1,\ldots,k \}$.
The feasible set is the set of points that satisfy the constraints of an optimization problem. If $\mathcal{X} = \emptyset$, the problem is said to be **infeasible**, such that $p^* = \begin{dcases}+\infty & \text{if }p^*=\min\limits_{x}f_{0}(x), \\ -\infty & \text{if }p^*=\max\limits_{x}f_{0}(x).\end{dcases}$
#### (*Optimal Set*) $\mathcal{X}_{\text{opt}}=\{ x \in \mathcal{X} :  f_{0}(x)=p^*\}=\arg\min\limits_{x \in \mathcal{X}}f_{0}(x)$.
If $\mathcal{X}=\emptyset$, then, naturally $\mathcal{X}_{\text{opt}}=\emptyset$. However, the converse isn't true. There may be feasible solutions to an optimization problem, but the optimal set is empty because:
1. The optimal solution is at a limit (i.e., $f_{0}$ diverges at $x^*$).
2. The constraints of the optimization problem are not satisfied.
#### (*Sub-optimality*) If $x \in \mathcal{X}$ and $p^* < f_{0}(x) \le p^* + \epsilon$, then $x$ is $\epsilon$-suboptimal.
