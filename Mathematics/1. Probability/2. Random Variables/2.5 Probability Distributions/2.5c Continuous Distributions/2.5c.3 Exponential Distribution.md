>[!note] Exponential Distribution
>A non-negative continuous random variable $X \geq 0$ follows the **Exponential distribution** $X \sim \text{Exp}(\lambda)$ with parameter $\lambda > 0$, defined by the [[2.5c.1 Probability Density Function|probability density function]]
>$$f_{X}(x)=\begin{cases}\lambda e^{-\lambda x} &\text{if} \enspace  x \geq 0 \\ 0 &\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}$$
>
>The Exponential distribution represents the waiting time until the first occurrence of an event in a process where events occur continuously, independently, and at a constant average rate $\lambda$.
>
>Additionally, akin to the discrete [[2.5b.5 Geometric Distribution|Geometric distribution]], the exponential distribution is the only continuous PDF that has the **memoryless property**, which states that the probability of needing more than $m$ additional trials to get the first success is independent of the $k$ trials that have already been conducted, such that
>$$\mathbb{P}[X > k + m \mid X > k] = \mathbb{P}[X > m],$$
>showing that whether or not you start fresh or you have already had $k$ failures, the chance of needing more than $m$ additional trials remains the same.

# Properties
#### (*Mean*) $\mathbb{E}(X \sim \text{Exp}(\lambda))= \frac{1}{\lambda}$.
#### (*Variance*) $\text{Var}(X \sim \operatorname{Exp}(\lambda))=\frac{1}{\lambda^2}$.
#### (*Discretization*) If $X \sim \text{Exp}(\lambda)$, $\lfloor X \rfloor + 1 \sim \operatorname{Geom}(1-e^{-\lambda})$.
We know that the CDF of $W$ is $F_{W}(w)=1-e^{-\lambda w}$. Then, $F_{X}(x)=1-e^{-\lambda x}$ for $x \in \mathbb{N}$. We are trying to find the probability of W falling in the interval $[x, x+1)$ (since this continuous interval represents the floor of values of $x$). As such,
$$\begin{align}
f_{X}(x)&=F_{X}(x+1)-F_{X}(x) \\
&=1-e^{-\lambda(x+1)}-1+e^{-\lambda x} \\
&=e^{-\lambda x}(1-e^{-\lambda})\\
&=(e^{-\lambda})^x(1-e^{-\lambda}),
\end{align}$$
which represents a geometric distribution with $p=1-e^{-\lambda}$ by definition.

#### (*Erlang Dist.*) Let $X_{1}, \ldots, X_{n} \sim_{\text{iid}} \operatorname{Exp}(\lambda)$. Then, $Z=\sum^{n}_{i=1}X_{i} \sim \operatorname{Erlang}(n,\lambda)$, such that $f_{Z}(z)=\dfrac{\lambda^nz^{n-1} e^{-\lambda n}}{(n-1)!}$ with $\mathbb{E}[Z]=\dfrac{k}{\lambda}$ and $\text{Var}(Z)=\dfrac{k}{\lambda^2}$.
