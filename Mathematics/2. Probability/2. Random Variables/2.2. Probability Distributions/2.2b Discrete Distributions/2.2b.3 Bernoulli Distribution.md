> [!note] Bernoulli Distribution
>A discrete random variable $X \in \{ 0, 1 \}$ follows the **Bernoulli distribution** $X \sim \text{Bernoulli}(p)$ if it represents a **single trial** with binary outcomes. Formally, the Bernoulli distribution of a random variable is given by the [[2.2b.1 Probability Mass Function|probability mass function]],
>$$p_{X}(x)=\begin{cases}p & \text{if } x=1, \\ 1-p & \text{if } x=0, \end{cases}$$
>where $p$ is the probability of a successful outcome.
### Explanation.
The Bernoulli [[2.2c.1 Probability Density Function|distribution]] is used for probabilities where the event either happens, or does not happen. Following this, the random variable can only take two values (1 or 0).

# Properties
#### (*Mean*) $\mathbb{E}[X \sim \text{Bernoulli}(p)]=p.$
#### (*Variance*) $\text{Var}[X \sim \text{Bernoulli}(p)]=p(1-p)$.
