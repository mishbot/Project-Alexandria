>[!note] Speed and Velocity
>To describe motion, we track how an object’s position changes over time using quantities like displacement, velocity, speed.
>
>The change in position of an object over a time interval is called **displacement**, defined as the difference between the final and initial position vectors, such that
>$$\Delta x = x - x_{0},$$
>where $x_0 \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the initial $n$-dimensional position of the object at time $t_0$, and $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is its final position at another point in time $t$.
>
>Then, **average velocity** can be defined as the overall rate of change of position,
>$$v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t},$$
>whereas **instantaneous velocity** captures the object's average velocity over an infinitesimal time interval:
>$$v = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0}\frac{\Delta r}{\Delta t}=\frac{d x}{dt}.$$
>
>**Speed** is the scalar magnitude of instantaneous velocity, such that $s = \|v\|_{2}$. However, **average speed** is defined as the scalar that measures how much ground was covered regardless of direction, expressed as
>$$s_{\text{avg}} = \frac{d}{\Delta t},$$
>where $d=\|\Delta x\|$ is the total distance that the object traveled.

# Examples
>[!example] Tricky Laps
>Suppose Abel is running a two-lap race, and his coach wants him to attain an average speed $v$ during the race. Abel has just finished the first lap when the coach holds up a sign to tell him that your current average speed is only $v / 2$ for lap one. 
>>[!example]- At what average speed must Abel run the second lap in order to reach his goal? Is this possible?
>>Don't be quick like me to say that your lap 2 speed should be $3v / 2$. As mentioned above, we note that **average speed is given as the overall rate of change of position with respect to time, not velocity**. 
>>
>>We know Abel ran Lap 1 in $t_{1} = 2d / v$, where $d$ is the length of the track. However, with an average speed of $v$, Abel has to run both laps in $t_{\text{total}}=t_{1}+t_{2}=2d / v$ ($2d$ since Abel runs the distance twice). Since all of Abel's time is already used up running the first lap, $t_{2}$ must be zero (i.e., Abel has to run the second lap instantaneously, with $v=\infty$). This is not humanly possible.
