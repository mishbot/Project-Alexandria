>[!note] Preferences and Utility
>These notes build consumer theory from a consumer's ranking of bundles of goods. [[6.1.1 Preference Relations]] defines a preference relation $\succsim$ on a commodity set $X$, with indifference $\sim$ and strict preference $\succ$. A relation is rational if it is complete and transitive, and continuous if its upper and lower contour sets are closed. The same note covers two failures, intransitivity from barely noticeable differences and the discontinuous lexicographic order. [[6.1.2 Monotone and Convex Preferences]] adds two shape assumptions. Under monotonicity, more of a good is never worse. Under convexity, a mixture of two bundles is at least as good as the worse of the two.
>
>[[6.1.3 Utility Functions]] replaces the ranking with a number. A rational and continuous $\succsim$ has a continuous representation $u$, with $\mathbf{x} \succsim \mathbf{y} \iff u(\mathbf{x}) \ge u(\mathbf{y})$. Utility is ordinal, so $f(u)$ represents the same preferences for every strictly increasing $f$. A statement like "twice as happy" has no meaning. [[6.1.4 Indifference Curves and MRS]] studies the level sets $u(x_1, x_2) = \bar u$ and their slope,
>$$\text{MRS} = \frac{dx_2}{dx_1}\bigg|_{u = \bar u} = -\frac{u'_{x_1}}{u'_{x_2}},$$
>a negative number that gives the consumer's rate of exchange between the two goods. [[6.2 Consumer Choice]] sets this slope equal to $-p_1/p_2$ to find the best affordable bundle.

# Prerequisites

- [[2.1 Convex Sets]], for convex upper contour sets in 6.1.2.
- [[2.2 Convex Functions]], for concave functions and the Hessian test used in 6.1.3.
- [[2.3. Partial Derivatives]], for marginal utilities.
- [[2.5. Implicit Function Theorem]], for the slope of an indifference curve in 6.1.4.

# Contents

- [[6.1.1 Preference Relations]]: commodity set, completeness, transitivity, indifference and strict preference, continuity, intransitive and lexicographic counterexamples.
- [[6.1.2 Monotone and Convex Preferences]]: monotonic, strictly monotonic, and convex preferences, and what each does to the indifference curves.
- [[6.1.3 Utility Functions]]: representation, the existence theorem, ordinality, and why represented preferences are rational. **Not taught:** that lexicographic preferences have no utility representation, and the link between concave or quasi-concave utility and convex preferences.
- [[6.1.4 Indifference Curves and MRS]]: indifference curves as implicit functions, the MRS and its sign convention, diminishing MRS, invariance of the MRS, and the MRS of each standard family.

# Recommended Path

Read the notes in order. Each note uses only earlier notes and the math notes listed above. With little time, read the callouts of 6.1.1 and 6.1.3. Then work the Easy and Medium examples of 6.1.1, which cover the usual checks of completeness and transitivity. Finally, read all of 6.1.4, since every later section in consumer theory uses the MRS.
