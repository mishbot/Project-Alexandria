>[!note] Feature Detection
>**Feature detection** finds image locations — *keypoints* or *interest points* — that are distinctive enough to be re-identified across images of the same scene. A good detector is **repeatable** (the same physical point yields a keypoint in every view), **localized** (precise to a pixel or better), **sparse** (few enough points to be matched efficiently), and **invariant** to nuisance transformations (rotation, scale, illumination, moderate viewpoint change).
>
>The standard pipeline pairs a detector with a [[4.3. Feature Description|descriptor]] and a [[4.4. Feature Matching|matcher]] to produce the correspondences that feed [[3.4. Homographies|homography]] estimation, [[5.3. Epipolar Geometry|epipolar geometry]], and [[6.1. Structure from Motion (SfM)|structure from motion]].

# Intuition
A pixel on a plain wall looks like every other pixel on the plain wall — matching it across images is hopeless. A pixel at the corner of a window, by contrast, has a unique local neighborhood that cannot be confused with its surroundings. Detection is the problem of locating these *distinctive* pixels automatically, before any descriptor is computed.

The design tension is between **distinctiveness** (pick points with unique neighborhoods) and **invariance** (pick points whose response is stable under zoom, rotation, and illumination change). Early detectors (Harris) optimize the first; modern detectors (DoG, Hessian-Laplace, SuperPoint) add scale selection and learned robustness on top.
