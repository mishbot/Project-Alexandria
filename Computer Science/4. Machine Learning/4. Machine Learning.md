**Machine learning** fits models to data so that they make good predictions or find structure on new data. This folder follows the order of *An Introduction to Statistical Learning* (James, Witten, Hastie and Tibshirani). It first sorts problems by the data available, then shows how to score a model, and then covers the models. Neural networks live in [[5. Deep Learning]].

## Prerequisites

- [[13.1 Singular Value Decomposition]] and [[6.1. Least Squares]], for the linear algebra behind regression and PCA.
- [[1.2.1 Maximum Likelihood Estimation]], for the probabilistic view of most losses.
- [[6.4. Gradient Descent]], for fitting models without a closed form.

## Contents

- [[1. Learning Paradigms]]: supervised, unsupervised, semi-supervised, self-supervised and reinforcement learning, compared by the data each needs.

### Supervised learning

- [[2. Model Evaluation]]: regression metrics, classification metrics, generalization, bias and variance, and cross-validation.
- [[3. Regression]]: linear and multiple regression, basis functions, ridge, lasso and elastic net, and nonlinear least squares.
- [[4. Classification]]: the Bayes classifier, linear discriminants, logistic and softmax regression, support vector machines and k-nearest neighbors.
- [[5. Tree-Based Methods]]: decision trees, random forests and gradient-boosted trees, for both regression and classification.

### Unsupervised learning

- [[6. Clustering]]: k-means, Gaussian mixture models and hierarchical clustering.
- [[7. Dimensionality Reduction]]: PCA, the curse of dimensionality and t-SNE.

## Recommended path

Read [[1. Learning Paradigms]] first, then [[2.3. Generalization]] and the two metric notes [[2.1.1. Mean Squared Error]] and [[2.2.1. Confusion Matrix]]. Continue with [[3.1. Linear Regression]] and [[4.3. Logistic Regression]], the two models most other notes build on.
