>[!note] K-Means Clustering
>**K-Means** is a clustering algorithm that finds $K$ cluster centers $\mu_k \in \mathbb{R}^D$ and assigns each data point $x_n \in \mathbb{R}^D$ to one cluster $z_n \in \{1, …, K\}$, such that the total within-cluster squared error is given by the objective function
>$$\arg\min_{\mu, z} \sum_{n=1}^{N} \|x_n - \mu_{z_n}\|_2^2.$$
>
>The optimal clusters $\mu^*$ are found programmatically using **Lloyd's Algorithm**. Start by randomly choosing $K$ data points as initial cluster centers. Then, repeat until until cluster assignments stop changing, such that $z^{(t)}=z^{(t-1)}$:
>1. Assign each data point $x_{n}$ to its nearest cluster center $z_{n}$, such that $$z_n = \arg\min_{k} \|x_n - \mu_k\|_2^2$$
>2. Recompute each cluster center as the mean of its assigned points:
>$$\mu_k = \frac{1}{N_k}\sum_{i=1}^{N_k} x_{k_i}.$$
>
>K-Means is **guaranteed to converge**, because **alternating minimization** of assignments ($z$) and centers ($\mu$) never increases the objective. Most often, the hyperparameter $K$ is chosen using the **elbow method** by plotting the K-Means objective vs. $K$ and choosing the point where improvement sharply decreases (i.e., "the elbow").

# Proof
### **Mathematical Derivation (Optional Detail)**

  

To minimize

\sum_{n=1}^N \|x_n - \mu_{z_n}\|2^2,

_we rearrange by cluster:_

\sum{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i=1}^{N_k}\sum_{d=1}^{D}(x_{k_i,d}-\mu_{k,d})^2

Taking the derivative w.r.t. \mu_{k,d} and setting to zero:

\mu_{k,d} = \frac{1}{N_k}\sum_{i=1}^{N_k}x_{k_i,d}

→ Each cluster center is the **mean** of its assigned points.
