>[!note] Startup Financing Lifecycle
>The startup financing lifecycle can be separated in four stages. Founders’ equity claim and control rights are diluted at each stage:
>1. Startups begin in the **pre-seed** stage, with founders contributing initial capital, known as **sweat equity**. They are issued **common stock**, which has the lowest liquidation priority but full voting rights.
>	- Optionally, startups in the early stages may rely on **incubators**, which offer resources such as workspace and support for idea validation, but do not provide capital. Nonprofits do not take equity, whereas for-profits may request a $1\%-5\%$ stake.
>2. As startups build their product and test market fit, **seed funding** through **angel investors** and **accelerators** is done to obtain a larger initial investment. These agents pre-purchase Series A equity with a built-in discount through [[2.3a.2iii Convertible Securities|convertible securities]] due to the unknown valuation of the company. Employees and prospects are incentivized by [[2.3a.2iv Stock Options|stock options]] (in this and all subsequent stages), and a stock option pool (usually $10\%-20\%$ of equity) is reserved for them.
>	- **Angel investors** are accredited investors who provide mentorship and connections, and invest (usually $\leq \$1\text{M}$) in exchange for equity.
>	- **Accelerators** are organizations that support startups with a viable MVP (minimum viable product) that generates some revenue, providing both a workspace, mentorship and capital ($\$25K-\$150K$) in exchange for $5\%-10\%$ equity.
>3. When startups validate and perfect their business plan, **Series A funding** (usually after $\geq 4\text{M}$ of seed investment) is the first external capital raised from angel investors or VCs. It is often the first priced round, where the company's valuation is formally negotiated and set. Series A investors are provided Series A preferred stock.
>4. Subsequent raises (i.e., Series B, C, ...) may occur given that the startup achieves certain growth milestones and no d**own-rounds** (lower valuation at a subsequent raise).
>
>>[!warning] Financing Only Applicable for Unicorns
>>All of the above investment mediums are on the lookout for **unicorns**, which are extremely high-growth potential startups. Unless a startup is a unicorn, it will not get such treatment and should look at other means of financing, such as
>>- **Bootstrapping**, where the startup relies solely on its own savings for growth.
>>- **Revenue-Based Financing**, where the startup relies on a revenue-based payment model (i.e., a fixed percentage of future revenues over a fixed period of time).
>>- **Traditional Loans**, provided by banks, and often backed by the U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA).
