>[!note] Futures
>**Futures** are standardized [[2.2a Forward Contracts|forward contracts]] that are traded on public exchanges (e.g. CME, CBOT, ICE) rather that OTC. Standardization requirements of futures include:
>- Specifying the **notional value** per individual contract (e.g. 5,000 bushels of corn, or $100,000 face amount of 30-year treasury bonds).
>- A cash collateral, known as **margin**, adjusted daily to account for changes in futures prices, known as **marking to market**. It is done to 
>- Delivery month and location(s).
>- Quality requirements for commodities.
>
>Since futures prices converge in maturity towards the spot price of the underlier per **Covered Interest Parity**, the difference between the spot price and the futures price is known as the **futures basis**. Investors may bet on the convergence or divergence of the futures and spot prices in a **basis trade**.
>
>If an investor does not require the actual commodities associated with a future contract, they may close it out with an offsetting trade. However, there are tales of investors forgetting to close out contracts and being forced to take/receive cattle and corn.
