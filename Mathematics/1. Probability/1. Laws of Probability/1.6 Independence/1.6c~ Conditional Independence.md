>[!note] Conditional Independence (IN PROGRESS)
>Events $A_{1}, \ldots, A_{N} \in \mathbb{\Omega}$ in the same probability space are **conditionally independent** given any event $B$ with $\mathbb{P}[B]>0$ if, for every subset $S \subseteq \{ 1, 2, \ldots, N \}$ where $|S| \geq 2$, the joint probability of the events $A_{i}$ for all $i \in S$ , given $B$, equals the product of their individual conditional probabilities given $B$. Formally, this means:
>$$\mathbb{P}\Big[\bigcap_{i \in S} A_{i} \mid B] = \prod_{i \in S}\mathbb{P}[A_{i} \mid B].$$
>If in addition, for any specific event $A_{k}$ with $k \in S$, if $\mathbb{P}[\cap_{S \backslash k} A_{i} \cap B] >0$, then $A_{k}$ is conditionally independent of the other events given $B$, such that
>$$\mathbb{P}[A_{k} \mid \cap_{i \in S \setminus k} A_{i} \cap B] = \mathbb{P}[A_{k} \mid B].$$

# Intuition
Consider events $A$, $B$ and $C$ in the same sample space. Then, $A$ and $B$ are considered conditionally independent if, for any $C$,
$$\mathbb{P}[A \cap B \mid C] = \mathbb{P}[A \mid C] \times \mathbb{P}[B \mid C].$$
Let's see why this is the case: with a quick proof:
$$\mathbb{P}[A \cap B \mid C] = \frac{\mathbb{P}[A \cap B \cap C]}{\mathbb{P}[C]}$$
