Spectral analysis treats a [[2.1. Time Series|time series]] as a sum of sinusoids and asks how its variance is distributed across frequencies. The lag-domain language of [[2.2.2 Autocovariance Function|autocovariance]] and [[2.2.3 Autocorrelation Function|autocorrelation]] reorganizes into the frequency-domain language of *spectral density*, with the two perspectives connected by the Fourier transform. Periodic structure that is hard to read off an autocorrelation plot becomes obvious as a peak in a periodogram; a filter that is awkward to write as a convolution becomes a multiplication in the frequency domain. The toolkit covered here is the standard one used in audio, neuroscience, geophysics, and any setting where the data carry meaningful oscillations.

## Prerequisites

- [[2.1. Time Series|Time series]] and weak stationarity
- [[2.2.2 Autocovariance Function|Autocovariance function]]
- Complex exponentials and Euler's formula
- [[2.4 Time Series Regression|Sinusoidal regression]] (motivates the Fourier basis)

## Contents

- [[2.3.1 Discrete Fourier Transform]]: the finite Fourier basis and the complex coefficients $d(\omega_j)$ that decompose a length-$n$ record
- [[2.3.2 Periodogram]]: the squared modulus $P(\omega_j) = (4/n)|d(\omega_j)|^2$, a noisy but unbiased estimate of the spectral density
- [[2.3.3 Spectral Density]]: the Fourier pair of the autocovariance, $f(\omega) = \sum_h \gamma(h) e^{-2\pi i h\omega}$
- [[2.3.4 Linear Filters and Frequency Response]]: convolution in time as multiplication in frequency, with $f_y = |A|^2 f_x$ as the central identity
- [[2.3.5 Time-Frequency Analysis]]: the short-time Fourier transform and spectrogram for non-stationary signals

## Recommended path

A reader new to the frequency domain should read [[2.3.1 Discrete Fourier Transform]] first to anchor the basis, then [[2.3.3 Spectral Density]] to see what the population quantity is, and finally [[2.3.2 Periodogram]] to connect data to estimation. [[2.3.4 Linear Filters and Frequency Response]] and [[2.3.5 Time-Frequency Analysis]] are extensions that build on those three.
