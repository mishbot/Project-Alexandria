>[!n] Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation
>**Gravitational force** is the attractive force between any two objects that have mass. Gravity is a **universal force**, such that it acts between all masses, regardless of type or composition.
>
>Newton’s **Law of Universal Gravitation** states that any two point masses attract each other with a force proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them, such that
>$$|\vec{F}| = G\frac{\mathcal{M}_1 \mathcal{M}_2}{r^2},$$
>where $G=6.67 \times 10^{-11} N \cdot m^2 / kg^2$ is known as the gravitational constant, and the $\mathcal{M}_{i}$'s are the **gravitational masses** of the two objects (i.e., how strongly an object interacts with a gravitational field). Note that although $\mathcal{M}=M$ as proven by Einstein's **equivalence principle**, gravitational mass differs from inertial mass.
>

# Examples
>[!example] (Easy) Particles
>Two particles of masses $m$ and $M$ are placed at $(3d,0)$ and $(0,4d)$ respectively, as shown in the graph.
>```tikz
>\usepackage{tikz}
>\begin{document}
>
>\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.75]
>  % Axes
>  \draw[->, thick] (-0.5, 0) -- (4.5, 0) node[right] {\(x\)};
>  \draw[->, thick] (0, -0.5) -- (0, 5.5) node[above] {\(y\)};
>  
>  % Points
>  \fill[] (3, 0) circle (5pt) node[above right] {\(m\)} node[below=2pt] {\((3d, 0)\)};
>  \fill[] (0, 4) circle (5pt) node[above right] {\(M\)} node[left=2pt] {\((0, 4d)\)};
>\end{tikzpicture}
>
>\end{document}
>```
>>[!example]- Find the gravitational force exerted on $m$ due to $M$.
>>One can express the vector of force to have the same direction as the vector $\vec{r}=(-3d, 4d)$. Take the magnitude of this vector and obtain $r=5d$. As such, the magnitude of the gravitational force exerted on $m_{1}$ due to $m_2$ is $|\vec{F}|=G\dfrac{Mm}{25d^2}$.
>>
>>Now find the angle $\theta$ that vector $\vec{r}$ makes with the $x$-axis. Per trigonometry, $5d=\dfrac{4d}{\sin\theta}$, and therefore $\sin\theta = 4/5$. Similarly, $\cos\theta=-3/5$. As such, $F_{x}=|F|\cos\theta=-G\dfrac{3Mm}{125d^2}$ and $F_{y}=|F|\sin\theta=G\dfrac{4Mm}{125d^2}$.
>
>Another particle of mass $m$ is placed at $(-3d, 0)$, as shown in the graph.

>>[!example]- What is the net gravitational force exerted on $M$ due to the other two particles of mass $m$?
>>Since the particles on the $x$-axis are of the same mass, the $x$-components of the gravitational force cancel out. Therefore, the total force will be $F_{x}=0$ and $F_{y}=-G\dfrac{8Mm}{125d^2}$, since in the last question the gravitational force was reversed to solve for $m$. 
>>
>>You can perform the calculation yourself as before to verify this.
