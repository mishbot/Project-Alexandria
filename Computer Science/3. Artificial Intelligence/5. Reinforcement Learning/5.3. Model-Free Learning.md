>[!note] Model-Free Learning
>**Model-free learning** bypasses explicit modeling of $T$ and $R$ by learning **values** or **Q-values** directly from experience. There are several model-free learning algorithms:
>- **Direct Evaluation** is a **passive** algorithm that evaluates a fixed policy $\pi$ by following it for many episodes and recording the total utility obtained from each state. Then, it computes
>	$$V^{\pi}(s) = \frac{1}{N_{s}}\sum_{k=0}^{T-t_{s}-1}\gamma^k\, r_{t+k},$$
>	where $N_{s}$ is the number of visits to state $s$, $T$ is the total length of the episode, $t_{s}$ is the time-step of the first appearance of state $s$ in the current episode, $\gamma$ is the discount factor, and $r_{t}$ is the reward at time-step $t$. Although direct evaluation is guaranteed to converge, it is **slow**, since it ignores transition structure and learns only from complete episodes.
>	⠀
>- **Temporal Difference Learning** improves on direct evaluation by learning **after every step** rather than after each episode using an **exponential moving average**, slowly iterating over
>	$$V^{\pi}(s) \leftarrow (1 - \alpha)V^{\pi}(s) + \alpha [R(s,\pi(s),s’) + \gamma V^{\pi}(s’)],$$
>	where $0 \leq \alpha \leq 1$ is the **learning rate**, which starts at $1$ and slowly converges to $0$, thereby making all subsequent samples have no affect on the model.
>	⠀
>- **Q-Learning** is an **active, model-free** algorithm that learns optimal **Q-values** directly, without needing an optimal policy. It uses a similar update rule to TDL, such that
>	$$Q(s,a) \leftarrow (1-\alpha)Q(s,a) + \alpha [R(s,a,s’) + \gamma \max_{a’} Q(s’,a’)].$$
>	This enables the agent to learn the **optimal policy** while still exploring - known as **off-policy learning** - since updates can be made even from suboptimal or random actions. Over time (with sufficient exploration and decaying $\alpha$), Q-learning converges to $Q^*(s, a)$, and the agent can **act greedily** with respect to $Q^*$ for **reward maximization**.
