>[!note] Arrow-Debreu Securities
>An **Arrow-Debreu security** is a contract that pays exactly one unit of currency *if a specific future state occurs*, and nothing otherwise.
>
>In an [[4.2b Arbitrage|arbitrage-free]] market with $N$ assets and $s$ possible future states, every asset’s price can be expressed as a linear combination of exactly $s$ Arrow-Debreu securities, with *no two securities paying in the same state*. The price of an Arrow–Debreu security is called the **state price** $q_i$ for state $i \in \{ 1, \ldots, N \}$. For a payoff matrix $X \in \mathbb{R}^{s \times N}$ ($N \geq s$) and a state price vector $q \in \mathbb{R}^s$, the vector of today's asset prices $p \in \mathbb{R}^N$ is expressed as
>$$p=X^Tq.$$
>
>The existence of a distinct Arrow–Debreu security for every possible state implies that the **market is complete**. In other words, the market is complete if $\operatorname{Rank}(X) = s$. This means the $s$-dimensional space of all possible state payoffs is fully spanned by the assets, so every contingent claim can be replicated as a linear combination of these Arrow–Debreu securities.

# Properties
#### (*State Price Decomposition*) $q=\begin{dcases}(X^T)^{-1}p & \text{if } N=s, \\ (XX^T)^{-1}Xp & \text{if } N \ge s, \\ \varnothing & \text{otherwise.}\end{dcases}$
#### (*Uniqueness of State Price*) If LOOP holds and market is complete, then $q$ exists and is unique.

#### (*Market Completeness*) If $\min(q) > 0$, the market is complete.
# Examples
>[!example] Asset Pricing With Sate Prices
>We can describe a 3-state, 3-asset *complete* market as follows:
>
>|     **State/Asset**    |  $\textbf{AD}_{1}$  |  $\textbf{AD}_{2}$  |  $\textbf{AD}_{3}$  | $\textbf{A}_{1}$ | $\textbf{A}_2$ |
|:-------:|:-----:|:-----:|:-----:|:-------:|:-------:|
| $S_{1}$ |   1   |   0   |   0   |    3    |   $u_{1}$    |
| $S_{2}$ |   0   |   1   |   0   |    2    |   $u_{2}$   |
| $S_{3}$ |   0   |   0   |   1   |    1    |   $u_{3}$    |
| **Price**   |  $q_{1}$   |  $q_{2}$   |  $q_{3}$   |         |         |
>Then, the price of asset 1 can be expressed as the linear combination $q_{\text{A}_{1}}=3q_{1}+2q_{2}+q_{3}$. Similarly, the price of asset 2 is $q_{\text{A}_{2}} = u_{1}q_{1}+u_{2}q_{2}+u_{3}q_{3}$.
