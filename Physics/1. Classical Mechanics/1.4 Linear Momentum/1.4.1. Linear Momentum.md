>[!note] Linear Momentum
>**Linear momentum** (SI: $\text{kg} \cdot \text{m} / \text{s}$) is a vector quantity that measures how difficult it is to stop an object in motion. For a particle of mass $m$ moving with velocity $\vec{v}$, its momentum is defined as
>$$\vec{p} = m \vec{v}.$$
>Linear momentum is also the integral of [[1.2.1. Newton's Laws|net force]] with respect to time, such that $\frac{d}{dt}\vec{p}=m\vec{a}$. Integrating both sides over a time interval $[t_{i}, t_{f}]$ gives the **Impulse–Momentum Theorem**,
> $$\vec{J} = \int_{t_i}^{t_f} \vec{F}_{\text{net}}\,dt = \Delta \vec{p} = m\vec{v}_f - m\vec{v}_i.$$ This implies that **if no external force** acts on objects in a system, then the total momentum of said objects is **conserved**, such that $\vec{P}_{i}=\vec{P}_{f}$, where $\vec{P}$ represents the total momentum of the system.
>
>This conservation law is especially useful when analyzing **collisions**, where two or more objects transfer momentum over short timescales:
>- In an **elastic collision**, both **momentum and mechanical energy** are conserved.
>
>- In an **inelastic collision**, **momentum is conserved**, but **mechanical energy is not**. Some of the energy is converted to other forms (e.g., thermal energy or sound).
>	- Furthermore, a **perfectly inelastic collision** is one where the objects stick together after impact, maximizing energy loss from the system’s mechanical energy.

>Two particles of mass $m_{1}$ and $m_{2}$ travel at an initial speed $v_{0}$, but with different angles as shown in the figure. After the two particles collide, they both fly out with an angle $45\degree$ with respect to the horizontal.
>
>![[Screenshot 2025-08-04 at 14.58.18.png|225]]
>
>>[!example]- Find the final speeds $v_{1}$ and $v_{2}$ of each particle.
>>Considering the $x$ and $y$ components of the speed of the particles, one obtains the following momentum conservation system:
>>$$\begin{dcases} \frac{1}{2}m_{1}v_{0}+\frac{\sqrt{ 3 }}{2}m_{2}v_{0} = \frac{\sqrt{ 2 }}{2}m_{1}v_{1}+\frac{\sqrt{ 2 }}{2}m_{2}v_{2} \\ -\frac{\sqrt{ 3 }}{2}m_{1}v_{0}+\frac{1}{2}m_{2}v_{0} = \frac{\sqrt{ 2 }}{2}m_{1}v_{1}-\frac{\sqrt{ 2 }}{2}m_{2}v_{2}, \end{dcases}$$
>> yielding $v_1 = \dfrac{m_1(\sqrt{2}-\sqrt{6}) + m_2(\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{6})}{4 m_1}v_{0}$ and $v_2 = \dfrac{m_1(\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{6}) + m_2(-\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{6})}{4 m_2}v_{0}$.

>[!example] (Medium) Elastic Spring Collision
>Two blocks of mass $m_{1}$ and $m_{2}$ respectively are placed on a frictionless surface. The block $m_{2}$ is initially at rest, and attached with a spring of spring constant $k$ on its back. The block $m_{1}$ is then given an initial speed $v_{0}$ and collides head onto the block $m_{2}$.
>
>![[Screenshot 2025-08-04 at 15.35.51.png|300]]
>
>>[!example]- What is the maximum compression of the spring throughout the entire collision process?
>>Maximum compression occurs when both objects have the same instantaneous speed. Since the collision contains an elastic spring, both momentum and kinetic energy are preserved: 
>>- For energy, $E_{i}=\frac{1}{2}m_{1}v_{0}^2$ is purely kinetic energy, whereas $E_{f}=\frac{1}{2}(m_{1}+m_{2})v_{1}+\frac{1}{2}kx^2$ is both elastic potential and the kinetic energy of both objects moving together at the same speed $v_{1}$.
>>- For momentum, $P_{i}=m_{1}v_{0}$, whereas $P_{f}=(m_{1}+m_{2})v_{1}$.
>>
>>As such, we obtain the following system of equations:
>>$$\begin{dcases} \frac{1}{2}m_{1}v_{0} = \frac{1}{2}(m_{1}+m_{2})v_{1}^2+\frac{1}{2}kx^2 \\ m_{1}v_{0}=(m_{1}+m_{2})v_{1}, \end{dcases}$$
>>solve for the displacement to obtain $x=\sqrt{ \dfrac{m_{1}m_{2}}{k(m_{1}+m_{2})} }v_{0}$.
>
>>[!example]- Find the velocity of the two blocks after the collision process.
>>In this elastic collision, both energy and momentum are preserved. After the collision, the objects go their separate ways. Therefore,
>>$$\begin{cases} \frac{1}{2}m_{1}v_{0}^2 = \frac{1}{2}m_{1}v_{1}^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_{2}v_{2}^2 \\ m_{1}v_{0} = m_{1}v_{1} + m_{2}v_{2}, \end{cases}$$
>>which yields $v_{1}=\dfrac{m_{1}-m_{2}}{m_{1}+m_{2}}v_{0}$ and $v_{2}=\dfrac{2m_{1}}{m_{1}+m_{2}}v_{0}$.
>
>>[!example]- What is the condition on $m_{1}$ and $m_2$, such that block $m_{1}$ will move backwards after the collision?
>>It must hold that $v_{1} = \dfrac{m_{1}-m_{2}}{m_{1}+m_{2}}v_{0} < 0$ from the last part. As such, $m_{1} < m_{2}$ in order for block $m_{1}$ to move backwards after the collision.
