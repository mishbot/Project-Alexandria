> [!note] The Forward Algorithm and Mini-Forward
> In [[6.4.1. Markov Models|Markov Models]], inference over time is performed using the **mini-forward algorithm**, which propagates a belief distribution forward using only the transition model. Given a belief distribution at time t, the mini-forward update computes the belief at time t+1 by marginalizing over the previous state:
>$$B'(X_{t+1}) \;=\; \sum_{x_t} P(X_{t+1}\mid x_t)\,B(x_t).$$
> This operation accounts only for **time elapse** and assumes no new evidence is observed between timesteps. As such, mini-forward applies to standard Markov chains where states are directly observable.
> 
> [[6.4.2. Hidden Markov Models|Hidden Markov Models]] (HMMs) extend this setting by introducing **evidence variables** $E_t$ that are probabilistically generated from hidden states $X_t$. Because new observations arrive at each timestep, belief propagation must incorporate both **state transitions** and **observations**. This leads to the **forward algorithm**, which generalizes mini-forward by adding an observation update.
> 
> The forward algorithm maintains the **belief state**
>$$B(X_t) = P(X_t \mid e_{1:t}),$$
> the posterior distribution over the hidden state given all evidence observed so far. Each iteration consists of two conceptually distinct steps:
>1. **Time elapse update (prediction):**
>$$B'(X_{t+1}) \;=\; \sum_{x_t} P(X_{t+1}\mid x_t)\,B(x_t),$$
>which is identical in form to the mini-forward algorithm.
>2. **Observation update (correction):**
>$$B(X_{t+1}) \;\propto\; P(e_{t+1}\mid X_{t+1})\,B'(X_{t+1}),$$
>which incorporates the new evidence using the emission (sensor) model. Normalization is typically deferred until the end of the update, since the proportionality constant is shared across states.
