>[!note] Kinetic Theory of Gases
>This section derives the behavior of a gas from the motion of its molecules. The [[2.3.1 Molecular Interpretation of Temperature|molecular interpretation of temperature]] is the central result. Molecules hitting the walls produce the pressure. Matching that pressure to the ideal gas law shows that absolute temperature measures the average translational kinetic energy per molecule, $\tfrac{3}{2} kT$. The [[2.3.2 Root-Mean-Square Speed|root-mean-square speed]] turns that energy into a speed, about $500\ \text{m/s}$ for air at room temperature. The [[2.3.3 Maxwell Speed Distribution|Maxwell speed distribution]] gives the full spread of speeds around that average, derived from the Boltzmann factor, and its long tail of fast molecules controls evaporation, reaction rates, and escape from a planet.
>
>The [[2.3.4 Equipartition of Energy|equipartition theorem]] generalizes the $\tfrac{3}{2} kT$ result: every accessible degree of freedom holds $\tfrac{1}{2} kT$, so a gas with $d$ of them per molecule has energy $\tfrac{d}{2} NkT$. The count $d$ appears in every formula of the next two sections. The last two notes step beyond the ideal gas and follow the textbook. The [[2.3.5 Van der Waals Equation of State|van der Waals equation]] corrects the ideal gas law for molecular size and attraction. The [[2.3.6 Mean Free Path|mean free path]] measures how far a molecule flies between collisions.

# Contents

- [[2.3.1 Molecular Interpretation of Temperature]]: the assumptions of kinetic theory, $P = \tfrac{1}{3}(N/V) m \overline{v^2}$, and $\overline{K} = \tfrac{3}{2} kT$. **Not taught:** the round-trip derivation.
- [[2.3.2 Root-Mean-Square Speed]]: $v_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{3kT/m}$, the four speeds of a gas, and the rms speed as a standard deviation. **Not taught:** the speed of sound, escape speed, and free flight.
- [[2.3.3 Maxwell Speed Distribution]]: the Boltzmann factor, the distribution function, its normalization, the integral toolkit, and the three characteristic speeds. **Not taught:** the high-speed tail, the fraction in a speed range, and the worked average for molecules moving one way.
- [[2.3.4 Equipartition of Energy]]: $\tfrac{1}{2} kT$ per accessible degree of freedom, the counts $3$, $5$, $7$, and $6$, and freeze-out. **Not taught:** the count for nonlinear molecules.
- [[2.3.5 Van der Waals Equation of State]]: the two corrections to the ideal gas law, the constants $a$ and $b$, and the critical temperature. **Not taught:** the whole note.
- [[2.3.6 Mean Free Path]]: distance between collisions, collision frequency, and the random walk of a molecule. **Not taught:** the whole note.

# Recommended Path

Read the first four notes in order. Each builds on the one before, and [[2.3.4 Equipartition of Energy]] is the result that sections 2.4 and 2.5 use on every page. The last two notes stand on their own and can be read in either order or skipped.
