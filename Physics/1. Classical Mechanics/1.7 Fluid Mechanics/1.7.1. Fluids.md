>[!note] Fluid
> A **fluid** is any substance that can flow and take the shape of its container, unable to resist shear stress when at rest. Its molecules are free to move relative to each other, allowing deformation under even small forces. **Liquids** have a definite volume but no fixed shape, while **gases** have neither and expand to fill any space.
> 
> To describe a fluid quantitatively, each infinitesimal element is treated as a continuous medium with measurable properties such as **density**, **pressure**, and **temperature**: 
>- **Density** (SI: $\text{kg / m}^3$) expresses the mass contained per unit volume,
> $$\rho = \frac{m}{V}.$$
> When density is effectively constant under pressure and temperature, the fluid is called **incompressible**, a common assumption for liquids.
> ‎ 
>- **Pressure** (SI: Pascals $\text{Pa}=\text{N / m}^2$) measures the intensity of normal force distributed over an area:
>$$P = \frac{F_\perp}{A}.$$
>It acts equally in all directions at a point in a static fluid, since any imbalance would create motion. Because pressure always acts perpendicular to surfaces, it is treated as a **scalar field**, not a vector quantity.
>
>Together, density connects a fluid’s mass to its weight, while pressure describes how that weight produces force throughout the medium.
