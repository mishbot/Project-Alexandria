>[!note] Scanning Networks
>A **scanning network** is a [[2. Neural Networks|neural network]]architecture that applies the _same local computation_ repeatedly across many overlapping regions of a structured input. Instead of connecting every neuron to the entire previous layer, a scanning network restricts each neuron to a **local receptive field** and **reuses the same weights** at every valid position. 
>
>Formally, a scanning network consists of a local function $g: \mathbb{R}^k \to \mathbb{R}$ operating on a small patch of the input, and a **scanning rule** that applies $g$ at each spatial location $i$ of the input, producing outputs $y_i = g(x_{i+u_1}, \ldots, x_{i+u_k})$, where $\{u_1, \ldots, u_k\}$ are fixed offsets defining a **receptive field**.
>
>This leads to two defining properties:
>- **Sparse connectivity:** The weight matrix is block-sparse; most connections do not exist.
>- **Shared connectivity pattern:** Every output neuron uses _the same_ local pattern of connections, just shifted spatially.
>
>As such, scanning networks can be interpreted as  a network where each neuron is connected to only a local subset of neurons from the previous layer, and this local connectivity pattern is repeated across positions:
