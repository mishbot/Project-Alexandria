>[!note] Fundamental Theorem of Linear Algebra
>The **Fundamental Theorem of Linear Algebra** reveals that every matrix $A$ (or, equivalently, every linear transformation) naturally splits the domain and codomain into two complementary parts. Formally, if $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$, then $A$ can be perceived as a linear map $A:\mathbb{R}^m \to \mathbb{R}^n$, such that
>$$\begin{alignat}{0}\mathbb{R}^n = \mathcal{R}(A^T) \oplus \mathcal{N}(A), \\ \text{and} \\ \mathbb{R}^m = \mathcal{R}(A) \oplus \mathcal{N}(A^T).\end{alignat}$$
>
>This implies that any vector $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ can be decomposed as a sum of two vectors orthogonal to each other; $z \in \mathcal{R}(A^T)$, and $\zeta \in\mathcal{N}(A)$, such that
>$$x = A^Tz + \zeta.$$

# Proof
We start by observing that every $x \in \mathcal{R}(A^T)$ in the row space of $A$ is orthogonal to every $z \in \mathcal{N}(A)$. This follows from the fact that for some $x=A^Ty$, it can be said that
$$x^Tz=y^TAz = y^T 0 = 0.$$
Thus, $\mathcal{R}(A^T) \perp \mathcal{N}(A)$, or equivalently, $\mathcal{N}(A)=\mathcal{R}(A^T)^\perp$. We know that a direct sum of a subspace and its orthogonal complement equals the whole subspace, and thus,
$$\mathcal{R}(A^T) \oplus \mathcal{R}(A^T)^\perp = \mathcal{R}(A^T) \oplus \mathcal{N}(A)=\mathbb{R}^n.$$
By the same reasoning, we can prove the fact for $\mathbb{R}^m$.
