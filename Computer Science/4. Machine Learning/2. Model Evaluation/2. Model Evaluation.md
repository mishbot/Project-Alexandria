A trained model is useful only if it performs well on data it has not seen. [[2.1. Regression Metrics]] and [[2.2. Classification Metrics]] show how to score a model, which depends on whether it predicts a number or a class. [[2.3. Generalization]] shows on which data to score it, so that the score reflects performance in deployment.

## Prerequisites

- [[1. Learning Paradigms]], for the supervised setting that the metrics assume.
- [[1.2.1 Maximum Likelihood Estimation]], for the likelihood view of squared error and log loss.
- [[2.2c.3 Gaussian Distribution]] and [[2.2b.3 Bernoulli Distribution]], the noise models behind that view.
- [[3.1. Linear Regression]], the model used in most regression examples.

## Contents

- [[2.1. Regression Metrics]]: compares the metrics for numeric predictions and gives a default choice.
- [[2.1.1. Mean Squared Error]]: MSE and RMSE, the conditional mean as minimizer, the Gaussian likelihood view, sensitivity to outliers.
- [[2.1.2. Mean Absolute Error]]: the median as minimizer, the Laplace likelihood view, robustness to outliers.
- [[2.1.3. Huber Loss]]: quadratic for small residuals and linear for large ones, the threshold $\delta$, smooth L1.
- [[2.1.4. R-Squared]]: the fraction of variance explained, adjusted $R^2$, negative $R^2$ on test data.
- [[2.2. Classification Metrics]]: compares the metrics for classifiers and gives a default choice.
- [[2.2.1. Confusion Matrix]]: accuracy, precision, recall, specificity, F1, and macro and micro averaging.
- [[2.2.2. ROC Curve]]: the threshold sweep, AUC as a ranking probability, and the pneumonia example.
- [[2.2.3. Precision-Recall Curve]]: average precision, the prevalence baseline, and rare positives.
- [[2.2.4. Log Loss]]: cross-entropy, calibration, proper scoring rules and the Brier score.
- [[2.3. Generalization]]: empirical and population risk, overfitting, the training, validation and test roles, and data leakage.
- [[2.3.1. Bias-Variance Decomposition]]: test error as noise plus squared bias plus variance, the tradeoff curve, double descent.
- [[2.3.2. Cross-Validation]]: $k$-fold, leave-one-out, stratified and nested variants, and splits for dependent data.

## Recommended Path

Read [[2.3. Generalization]] first, since every metric means little unless it is computed on held-out data. Then read [[2.1.1. Mean Squared Error]] and [[2.2.1. Confusion Matrix]], the most common starting points for each task. Finish with [[2.3.1. Bias-Variance Decomposition]] and [[2.3.2. Cross-Validation]].
