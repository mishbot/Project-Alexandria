>[!note] Capital Asset Pricing Model
>The Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM) is a financial framework built on top of [[4.3b Modern Portfolio Theory|Modern Portfolio Theory]] that links the expected return of an asset to its risk. It is based on a few key assumptions:
>- All investors are rational and risk-averse.
>- Only systematic risk matters, because idiosyncratic risk is diversified away.
>- Markets are frictionless (i.e., no transaction costs, borrowing constraints, etc.)
>
>CAPM is a single-factor model, which means it explains an asset’s expected return solely through its sensitivity to market risk through the factor, **beta**:
>$$\mathbb{E}(R_{p})=R_{f}+\beta_{p}[\mathbb{E}(R_{M})-R_{f}],$$
>where $\beta_{p}=\dfrac{\sigma_{p,M}}{\sigma_{M}}=\rho_{p,M} \cdot \dfrac{\sigma_{p}}{\sigma_{M}}$ is a measure of the relative sensitivity of the portfolio to market movements.
>
>This fundamental equation gives rise to the **security market line**, which measures the expected return of a portfolio against its beta.
>
>>[!warning] CAPM for Assets vs. Porfolios
>>CAPM can be used for both quantifying individual assets and whole portfolios.
