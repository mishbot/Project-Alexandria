>[!note] Graph Neural Networks
>This section covers networks whose input is a graph: nodes joined by edges, with a feature vector on each node. Molecules, social networks and road maps all have this form. A CNN relies on a fixed grid, and a general graph has none. A graph network therefore applies the same update at every node and combines the neighbors without an order. [[6.1.1. Message Passing]] defines this update and its properties. [[6.1.2. Graph Convolutional Networks]] writes the update as one matrix product with a normalized adjacency matrix. [[6.1.3. Graph Pooling]] shrinks a graph to fewer nodes or to one vector, for graph-level outputs. All three notes use the same five-node graph, a triangle with a two-edge tail.

# Prerequisites

- [[3.1 Graph]], for adjacency matrices, degrees and walks.
- [[5.2.1. Convolutional Layer]], the grid case that message passing generalizes.
- [[5.2.1b. Equivariance and Invariance]], for the symmetry argument behind the aggregators.

# Contents

- [[6.1.1. Message Passing]]: graph types and edge labels, the three task levels, the message-passing update, aggregators, permutation equivariance, $K$-hop neighborhoods and walk counts with $\mathbf A^k$, the image as a labeled graph, oversmoothing, oversquashing, normalization on graphs and masked nodes. **Not taught:** edge and global vectors, walk counts with $\mathbf A^k$, and the comparison of aggregators. Shortest paths with a min aggregator come from discussion.
- [[6.1.2. Graph Convolutional Networks]]: the GCN layer, self-loops, sum, mean and symmetric propagation matrices, their spectra, oversmoothing in linear GCNs, inductive and transductive learning, and one-hot inputs. **Not taught:** the matrix form and the named propagation matrices, the renormalization trick, graph attention, batching graphs, and edge graphs.
- [[6.1.3. Graph Pooling]]: readouts, the global node, DiffPool with its assignment matrix, the link and entropy losses, and why hard clustering blocks gradients. **Not taught:** the separate embedding GNN of the DiffPool paper, and other pooling layers such as SortPool.

# Recommended Path

Read [[6.1.1. Message Passing]] first, since the other notes specialize its update. Then read [[6.1.2. Graph Convolutional Networks]], whose examples compute oversmoothing on small graphs. Read [[6.1.3. Graph Pooling]] last. Masked nodes return in [[8.1. Self-Supervised Learning]] as a pretext task.
