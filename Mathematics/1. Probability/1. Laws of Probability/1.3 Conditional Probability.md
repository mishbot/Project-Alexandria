>[!note] Conditional Probability
>For two events $A, B \subseteq \Omega$ in the same [[1.1 Probability Space|probability space]], the law of conditional probability states that the probability of $B$ given that $A$ has happened is
>$$\mathbb{P}[B \mid A]=\frac{\mathbb{P}[A \cap B]}{\mathbb{P}[A]}.$$
>This implies that the probability of $A$ is nonzero ($\mathbb{P}[A]>0$).
>
>>[!warning] Non-Associativity
>>Conditional probabilities are non-associative. This means that generally, $\mathbb{P}[B \mid A] \neq \mathbb{P}[A \mid B]$, unless $\mathbb{P}[A]=\mathbb{P}[B]$.
# Intuition
The joint probability $\mathbb{P}[B \cap A]$ is the probability that both $A$ and $B$ happen. It is restricted to the sample points that occur in both events. In a Venn diagram, it can be represented as the overlap between two circles, $A$ and $B$:
```tikz
\begin{document}
\begin{tikzpicture}
  % Define circles for A and B
  \draw[thick] (0,0) circle (2cm) node[left] {\(A\)};
  \draw[thick] (2.5,0) circle (2cm) node[right] {\(B\)};

  % Label A ∩ B
  \begin{scope}
    \clip (0,0) circle (2cm);
    \fill[gray, opacity=0.15] (2.5,0) circle (2cm);
  \end{scope}
  \node at (1.25,0) {\(A \cap B\)};

  % Label sample space
  \draw[thick] (-3,-2.5) rectangle (6,2.5) node[below left] {\(\Omega\)};
\end{tikzpicture}

\end{document}
```
