
>[!note] Linear Least Squares Estimate (LLSE)
>The **linear least‐squares estimator (LLSE)** of a random variable $Y$ given another random variable $X$ is the best linear predictor of $Y$. Concretely, we seek constants $a$ and $b$ such that the linear function $\hat{Y}=aX+b$ minimizes the squared error $\mathbb{E}[(Y-\hat{Y})^2]$:
>$$\mathbb{L}[Y \mid X] = \min_{a,b}\mathbb{E}[(Y−(aX+b))^2],$$
>which is achieved by the optimal value
>$$\mathbb{L}[Y \mid X]=\mathbb{E}[Y]+\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)}{\operatorname{Var}(X)}(X-\mathbb{E}[X]).$$
>The LLSE is unbiased, meaning that $\mathbb{E}[\hat{Y}_{\text{LLSE}}]=\mathbb{E}[Y]$.

# Intuition
In the geometric sense, things become much simpler to explain. Let the random variables $X,Y$ be equivalent to vectors $x, y \in L^n(\Omega)$ in the Hilbert space of dimension $n$, such that $x,y,u$, are linearly independent, and their span forms the 3-dimensional Hilbert subspace of random variables. Here, $u$ is the unit-norm uniform vector (i.e., $u=\frac{1}{\|1\|_{2}}$). Then:
1. $\mathbb{E}[XY] \equiv \langle x,y\rangle$. 
2. $\mathbb{E}[X^2] \equiv \|x\|^2_{2}$.
3. $\rho=\dfrac{\mathbb{E}[XY]}{\sqrt{ \mathbb{E}[X]\mathbb{E}[Y] }} \equiv \dfrac{\langle x,y \rangle}{\|x\|_{2}\|y\|_{2}}$.

Now, let $\bar{x}=\dfrac{x-\langle x, u \rangle u}{\|x-\langle x, u \rangle u\|_{2}}$ be the Gram-Schmidt orthonormalized vector of $x$, such that $\{ \bar{x}, u \}$ form a basis in 2-dimensional space. We can project $y$ onto this basis, such that
$$\mathbb{L}[Y \mid X]=\operatorname{proj}_{\operatorname{Span}(\bar{x}, u)}(x)=\operatorname{proj}_{\operatorname{Span}(u)}(x)+\operatorname{proj}_{\operatorname{Span}(\bar{x})}(x).$$
This is equivalent to
$$\mathbb{L}(Y \mid X)=\langle y, u \rangle u+\langle y, \bar{x} \rangle \bar{x}=\mathbb{E}[Y]+\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}Y] \cdot \bar{X}.$$
Here, $\bar{X}=\frac{X-\mathbb{E}[X]}{\sqrt{ \operatorname{Var}(X) }}$. Substituting, we get the standard LLSE equation:
$$\begin{align}
\mathbb{L}(Y \mid X)&=\mathbb{E}[Y]+\frac{\mathbb{E}[XY]-\mathbb{E}[X] \cdot \mathbb{E}[Y]}{\sqrt{ \operatorname{Var}(X)} } \cdot \frac{X-\mathbb{E}[X]}{\sqrt{ \operatorname{Var}(X) }} \\ \\
&= \mathbb{E}[Y] + \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)}{\operatorname{Var}(X)}(X-\mathbb{E}[X]).
\end{align}$$
# Properties
#### (*Orthogonality*) $\operatorname{Cov}(E, Y)=0$, where $E=Y-\hat{Y}_{\text{LLSE}}$.
#### (*Independence Partition*) It holds that $\mathbb{L}[Y \mid X,Z]=\mathbb{L}[Y \mid X]+\mathbb{L}[Y \mid Z]$ if $X, Z$ are independent and $\mu=\begin{bmatrix}0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}^T$.
Let $x,y,z$ represent $X,Y,Z$ in the Hilbert space. Since $y,z$ are orthogonal, $\left\{  \bar{y}=\frac{y}{\|y\|_{2}}, \bar{z}=\frac{z}{\|z\|_{2}}  \right\}$ form an orthonormal basis. We can decompose the projection into
$$\begin{align}
\mathbb{L}(X \mid Y,Z) &= \operatorname{proj}_{\operatorname{span}(\bar{y},\bar{z})}(x) \\
&= \operatorname{proj}_{\operatorname{span}(\bar{y})}(x)+\operatorname{proj}_{\operatorname{span}(\bar{z})}(x) \\
&= \langle x, \bar{y} \rangle \bar{y} + \langle x, \bar{z} \rangle \bar{z} \\
&= \mathbb{E}[X\bar{Y}]\bar{Y}+\mathbb{E}[X\bar{Z}]\bar{Z},
\end{align}$$
where $\bar{Y}=\frac{Y}{\sqrt{ \mathbb{E}[Y^2] }}$ and $\bar{Z}=\frac{Z}{\sqrt{ \mathbb{E}[Z^2] }}$. As such,
$$\begin{align}
\mathbb{L}(X \mid Y,Z)&=\frac{\mathbb{E}[XY]}{\mathbb{E}[Y^2]}Y+\frac{\mathbb{E}[XZ]}{\mathbb{E}[Z^2]}Z \\
&= \mathbb{L}(X \mid Y) + \mathbb{L}(X \mid Z).
\end{align}$$
#### (Zero-Mean Partition) $\mathbb{L}[Y \mid X,Z]=\mathbb{L}[Y \mid X]+\mathbb{L}[Y \mid Z - \mathbb{L}[Z \mid X]]$ for any $X,Y,Z$ if $\mu=\begin{bmatrix}0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}^T$.
We proceed in the same manner as above, but with an extra step: Gram-Schmidt.
