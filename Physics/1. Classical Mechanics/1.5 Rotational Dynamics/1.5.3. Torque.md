>[!note] Torque
>In rotational motion, the angular equivalent of [[1.2.1. Newton's Laws|force]] is called **torque**, and measures the effectiveness of a force in producing angular acceleration about an axis. It is defined as
>$$\tau = r F \sin \phi,$$
>where $r$ is the distance from the axis of rotation to the point of force application, $F$ is the magnitude of the applied force, and $\phi$ is the angle between the force vector and the position vector $\vec{r}$.
>
>Just as force changes linear motion, torque changes rotational motion following the rotational form of Newton’s Second Law, such that
>$$\sum \tau = I\alpha,$$
>where $I$ is the object's rotational inertia and $\alpha$ is its angular acceleration.

# Derivation
Let's start at $\alpha = a / r$. Since $a=F \sin \phi / m$, where $\phi$ is the angle between the force vector and the position vector, where the position vector is in the direction from the axis of rotation to the point:

```tikz
\begin{document}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=1]

% Axis
\fill (0,0) circle (2pt);
\node[below left] at (0,0) {axis};

% Radius r
\draw[dashed] (0,0) -- (4,0) node[midway,below] {$r$};

% Mass
\fill (4,0) circle (2pt);
\node[above right] at (4,0) {$m$};

% Force vector F
\draw[->,thick] (4,0) -- ++(35:2) node[above right] {$\vec{F}$};

% Tangential acceleration a_t (perpendicular to r)
\draw[->,thick,dotted] (4,0) -- ++(90:1.5) node[right] {$\vec{a}_t$};

% Line of action (dotted)
\draw[dotted] (4,0) -- ++(35:3);

% r_eff
\draw[dashed] (0,0) -- ++(35-90:2.3) node[midway,below left] {$r_{\mathrm{eff}}$};

% Angle phi at mass
\draw (4,0) ++(0:0.6) arc (0:35:0.6);
\node at (4.7,0.25) {$\phi$};

% Right angle marker
\draw (0.4,-0.15) -- (0.55,0) -- (0.4,0.15);

\end{tikzpicture}
\end{document}
```

We then have that $\alpha = \dfrac{F \sin \phi}{mr}$, or equivalently, 
$$I \alpha = r F \sin \phi = \tau,$$where $I=mr^2$ by definition, since this is a system with a single "particle" object.

>You apply a force $F$ at an angle $\phi$ with respect to the handle of the wrench at a point with a distance $r$ away from the screw:

>>[!example]- Find the torque $\tau$ applied by the force. Express it first using $r, F, \phi$, and then using $d, F$.
>>The torque can be expressed as $\tau = rF \sin \phi$, since the perpendicular component of force is the one pointing counterclockwise, indicating a positive movement. Similarly, using $d, F$, torque can be expressed as $\tau = dF$, since $d=r\sin \phi$, as shown in the diagram.


>[!example] (Easy) Seesaw
>A seesaw with length $L/2$ on each side of the pivot makes a $30\degree$ angle with respect to the horizontal. Three forces $F_{1}, F_{2}, F_{3}$ act on the seesaw.
>
>![[Screenshot 2025-08-11 at 20.14.22.png|300]]
>
>>[!example]- Find the net torque applied by these forces.
>>The component perpendicular to the seesaw for each force is:
>>- $-F_{1}\cos 30\degree$ (negative since it is clockwise)
>>- $F_{2}\sin 30\degree$
>>- $F_{3}\sin 30\degree$
>>
>As such, the net torque is 
>$$\begin{align}\tau &= -\frac{L}{2}F_{1}\cos 30\degree + \frac{L}{4}F_{1}\sin 30\degree + \frac{L}{2}F_{3}\sin 30\degree \\ &= -\frac{\sqrt{ 3 }}{4}LF_{1} + \frac{1}{8}LF_{2} + \frac{1}{4}LF_{3}. \end{align}$$

>>[!example]- Find the torque exerted by gravity on the small sphere with respect to the hinge when it was released.

>>[!example]- Show that the kinetic energy of the small sphere due to its translational motion is the same as its rotational kinetic energy with respect to the hinge.
>>We have that $K=\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2=\dfrac{1}{2}mL^2 \omega^2=\dfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2=K_{\text{rot}}$.
>
>>[!example]- Find the speed of the small sphere right before it hits the ground.
>>From conservation of energy, we know that $E_{i}=E_{f}$, such that $mgL \theta_{0} = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$. Therefore, $v=\sqrt{ 2gL \theta_{0} }$.
>

>[!example] (Medium) Co-Axial Structure w/ Two Forces
>Consider an object with an co-axial structure, where the inner axis has a radius $R_{1}$ and the outer radius is $R_2$ as shown in the figure.
>
>![[Screenshot 2025-08-13 at 0.15.49.png|300]]
>
>Suppose a force $F_{1}$ is applied to the inner axis at a point shown in the figure and has a direction with an angle $\theta_{1}$ with respect to the horizontal line.
>
>Another force $F_{2}$ is applied on the outer radius at a point and direction shown in the figure with angle $\theta_{2}$ with respect to the horizontal line.
>
>The object is fixed so that it cannot move translationally, but is allowed to rotate about its central axis that is perpendicular to the radii. Suppose the object has a rotational inertia $I$ with respect to the central axis.
>
>>[!example]- Find the angular acceleration of the object.
>>For your ease, please draw a diagram of this setup in 2D.
>>
>>The equation for the torque becomes $I \alpha = R_{1}F_{1} \sin \theta_{1}+R_{2}F_{2}\cos \theta_{2}$ since both forces are clockwise, and we take the component that's tangential to the surface (and equivalently, perpendicular to the radius). Therefore, $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{I}(R_{1}F_{1} \sin \theta_{1}+R_{2}F_{2}\cos \theta_{2})$.

>[!example] (Medium) Co-Axial Pulley
>Shown in the figure is a “co-axial” pulley with an inner core of radius $R_{1}$ protruding from a larger disk of radius $R_{2}$. Two blocks of mass $m_{1}$ and $m_{2}$ with $m_{2} > m_{1}$ are hung by two ropes that are wound on $R_{1}$ and $R_{2}$ respectively. The rotational inertia of the pulley is $I$.
>
>![[Screenshot 2025-08-11 at 21.52.56.png|150]]
>
>>[!example]- Find the acceleration of the block $m_{1}$.
>>We have that $T_{1}-m_{1}g=m_{1}a_{1}$ and $T_{2}-m_{2}g=-m_{2}a_{2}$. The boxes have different accelerations because they are not on the same disk. We also have that $a_{1}=\alpha R_{1}$ and $a_{2}=\alpha R_{2}$. 
>>
>>Now look at the torque equation. Since the smaller pulley is being pulled counterclockwise by $T_{1}$ and the bigger pulley is being pulled clockwise by $T_{2}$, it follows that 
>>$$-I \alpha = T_{1}R_{1} - T_{2}R_{2} \iff I \alpha = T_{2}R_{2} - T_{1}R_{1}.$$
>>
>>Plugging for $T_{1}$ and $T_{2}$, as well as $a_{1}$ and $a_{2}$, we obtain
>>$$I \alpha = m_{2}R_{2}(-\alpha R_{2} + g) - m_{1}R_{1}(\alpha R_{1} + g),$$
>>such that $\alpha = \dfrac{m_{2}R_{2}-m_{1}R_{1}}{I+m_{1}R_{1}^2+m_{2}R_{2}^2}g$, and therefore $a_{1} = \alpha R_{1}=\dfrac{R_{1}(m_{2}R_{2}-m_{1}R_{1})}{I+m_{1}R_{1}^2+m_{2}R_{2}^2}g$.
>
>>[!example]- Find the tension of the rope suspending $m_{1}$.
>>Since $T_{1}=m_{1}(a_{1}+g)$, and we found $a_{1}$ in the last part, then
>>$$T_{1}=\frac{m_{1}m_{2}R_{1}R_{2})+Im_{1}}{I+m_{1}R_{1}^2+m_{2}R_{2}^2}g$$
