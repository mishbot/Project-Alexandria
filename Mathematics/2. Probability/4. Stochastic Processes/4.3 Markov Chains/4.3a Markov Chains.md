>[!note] Markov Chain
>A **discrete-time Markov chain** is a sequence of random variables $(X_{n})_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, where the future only depends on the current state. Formally, $(X_{n})_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ is a Markov chain if and only if it satisfies the Markov property,
$$\mathbb{P}(X_{n+1} = x_{n+1} \mid X_{n} = x_{n}, \ldots, X_{0} = x_{0}) = \mathbb{P}(X_{n+1} = x_{n+1} \mid X_{n} = x_{n})=P(x_{n}, x_{n+1}).$$
>
>When $\mathcal{X}$ is finite, the set of one-step transition probabilities $\{ P(x_{n}, x_{n+1}) : x_{n},x_{n+1} \in \mathcal{X} \times \mathcal{X}\}$ can be organized into a **transition probability matrix** $P \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{X}| \times |\mathcal{X}|}$, such that its rows sum to one. Then, the $k$-step transition equation is given as $$P_{k}(x,y)=\mathbb{P}(X_{k}=y \mid X_{0} = x) = P^k(x,y).$$
>
>Similarly, a sequence of random variables $(X_{t})_{{t \in \mathbb{R_{+}}}}$ is a **continuous-time Markov chain** on $\mathcal{X}$ if it satisfies the continuous-time Markov property,
>$$\mathbb{P}(X_{t+\delta t} = x_{t+\delta t} \mid X_{t} = x_{t}, \ldots, X_{0} = x_{0}) = \mathbb{P}(X_{t+\delta t} = x_{t+\delta t} \mid X_{t} = x_{t}).$$
>
>Different from DTMCs, CTMCs employ a **rate matrix** 
>
>>[!warning] Dependence on Past States
>>The fact that knowledge of the current state $X_{n}$ fully determines the distribution of the next state $X_{n+1}$ does not imply that $X_{n+1}$ is independent of all $X_{n-k}$ for $k \geq 1$. In general, the random variables in a Markov chain are *not independent*.
