>[!note] Decision Network
>A **decision network** (also called an **influence diagram**) is a graphical model that extends a [[6.3. Bayesian Networks|Bayesian network]] to include **actions (decisions)** and **preferences (utilities)**, allowing an agent to **reason about what it should do**, rather than what is true.
>
>A decision network contains three types of nodes:
>1. **Chance nodes (ovals)** represent random variables, like in Bayesian networks. Denoted as $s$.
>2. **Decision nodes (rectangles)** represent choices the agent can make. Denoted as $a$.
>3. **Utility nodes (diamonds or rounded rectangles)** represent the agent’s preferences or payoff (numerical utility). Denoted by the output of the function $U(s, a)$.
>
> The goal in a decision network is to choose the action that maximizes **expected utility**. Given evidence $e$, the expected utility of an action $a$ is
>$$\text{EU}(a \mid e) = \sum_{x_1,\dots,x_n} P(x_1,\dots,x_n \mid e)\,U(a, x_1,\dots,x_n),$$
>where the sum ranges over all relevant chance variables. The optimal action is the one achieving the **maximum expected utility (MEU)**, such that 
>$$\text{MEU}(e)=\max_{a} \sum_{s}P(s \mid e)U(s, a),$$
>where $U(s, a)$ is the utility of the utility function evaluated at a specific state–action pair.
>
>In practice, we run inference to compute posteriors and select the action with highest expected utility.
>
> Within decision networks, the **Value of Perfect Information (VPI)** quantifies how much an agent would benefit, in expectation, from observing an additional variable _before_ making a decision. Let $e$ denote the agent’s current evidence and $E'$ a potential new observation. The VPI of observing $E'$ given $e$ is defined as
>$$\text{VPI}(E' \mid e) = \text{MEU}(e, E') - \text{MEU}(e),$$
> where $\text{MEU}(e)$ is the maximum expected utility without the new observation, and $\text{MEU}(e, E')$ is the _expected_ maximum expected utility after observing $E'$, averaged over the possible values that $E'$ could take.
