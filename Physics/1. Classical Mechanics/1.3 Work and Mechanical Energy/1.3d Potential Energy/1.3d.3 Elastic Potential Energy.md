>[!note] Elastic Potential Energy
>**Elastic potential energy** $U_s$ is the scalar quantity stored in a system when it is deformed from its equilibrium configuration by an internal restoring force, assuming it obeys [[1.2.6. Spring Force|Hooke's Law]].
>
>Mathematically, the elastic potential energy given a displacement $x$ from equilibrium is
>$$U(x) = \frac{1}{2}kx^2,$$
>where $k$ is the spring constant (a measure of stiffness), and $x$ is the displacement from the system’s equilibrium length. 
>
>This energy is always non-negative and reaches zero when the system is at rest in its natural, undeformed configuration.

# Examples
>[!example] (Easy) Spring-Loop Track Energy Transfer
>A block of mass $m$ is first pushed against a spring of force constant $k$ by a length $R$ and released from rest. After the release it moves toward a vertical circular track of radius $R$, as shown in the figure.
>
>![[Screenshot 2025-08-03 at 20.35.22.png|300]]
>
>>[!example]- Assuming the block gains enough speed to reach to the top of the vertical track, what is the kinetic energy of the block when it reaches the top?
>>The elastic potential energy before release is $U=\dfrac{1}{2}kR^2$. The potential energy is transformed to kinetic energy and as such, the total energy before the loop is $E_i=\dfrac{1}{2}kR^2+0$. After the loop, the box is displaced by $2R$ in the $y$ direction. This means that the total energy $E_{f}=K_{f}+U_{f}$, where $U_{f}=2mgR$ is the gravitational potential energy, as it is conservative, and therefore relies only on the initial and final displacement of $0$ and $2R$ respectively. Since the system is conserved (i.e., there are no other outside forces to consider since we assume the surface is frictionless and there is no drag), $E_{f}-E_{i}=0$. Therefore, 
>>$$K_{f}+2mgR-\frac{1}{2}kR^2=0 \iff K_{f}=\frac{1}{2}kR^2-2mgR.$$
>
>>[!example]- Find the normal force between the track and the block when the block reaches the top of the track.
>>The block pushes on the loop, and therefore the loop pushes black at the block. Since the two vertical forces in play here are the normal force and the force of gravity $mg$, and both are downward, then $\dfrac{mv^2}{R}=N+mg$ per circular motion. Given the answer for $K_{f}$ in the previous step,
>>$$\frac{1}{2}mv^2=\frac{1}{2}kR^2-2mgR = \frac{1}{2}(RN+mgR),$$
>>
>>which when solved yields $N=kR-5mg$.

>[!example] (Medium) Critical Friction in a Coupled Spring-Pulley System
>A block of mass $m_2$ is placed on a frictionless table with another block $m_3$ placed on top of it. The surface between $m_2$ and $m_3$ has a coefficient of static friction $\mu_s$. 
>
>The block $m_2$ is connected with another block $m_1$ by a massless rope which wraps around a massless pulley as shown in the figure. The rope does NOT slip over the pulley when moving. 
>
>![[Screenshot 2025-08-03 at 21.18.12.png|300]]
>
>The block $m_1$ is connected with a spring of force constant $k$ whose other end is fixed to the ground. The spring is stretched with an amount $h$ away from its equilibrium point and the whole system is released from rest.
>
>
>>[!example]- Find the minimum value of $\mu_{s}$ such that the block $m_{3}$ can remain on block $m_{2}$ without falling off.
>>For block $m_{3}$, static friction is what accelerates the block rightward. Therefore, $f_{s}=m_{3}a$. 
>>
>>For block $m_{2}$, tension opposes static friction, such that $T-f_{s}=m_{2}a \iff T=(m_{2}+m_{3})a$. 
>>
>>For block $m_{1}$, tension opposes gravity and the spring force, such that $T-F_{s}-m_{1}g=-m_{1}a$, as block $m_{1}$ accelerates downward, in the negative direction. Additionally, since the displacement of the spring force from equilibrium is in the positive direction, $F_{s}=-kh$. Solving for tension, one obtains
>>$$(m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3})a=m_{1}g-kh \iff a=\frac{m_{1}g-kh}{m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}}.$$
>>
>>From the definition of static friction force, it is known that $F_{\text{s}} \leq\mu_{s}N$, where $N-m_{3}g=0$. As such,
>>$$m_{3}\frac{m_{1}g-kh}{m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}} \leq \mu_{s} m_{3}g,$$
>>and therefore $\mu_{s} \geq \dfrac{m_{1}g-kh}{g(m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3})}$.
>
>>[!example]- After we release the system, what is the speed of $m_{1}$ when the spring returns to its equilibrium position?
>>Before release, $E_{i}=0+\dfrac{1}{2}kh^2+m_{1}gh$. However, because the whole system moves, $E_{f}=\dfrac{1}{2}(m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3})v^2$. Per conservation of energy, $E_{i}=E_{f}$, and therefore,
>>$$v=\sqrt{\frac{2m_{1}gh+kh^2}{m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}}}.$$
