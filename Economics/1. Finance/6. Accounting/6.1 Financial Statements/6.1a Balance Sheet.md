>[!note] Balance Sheet
>The **balance sheet** is a financial statement that provides a snapshot of a company’s financial position at a specific point in time. It follows the fundamental accounting equation
>$$\text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Equity}.$$
>It provides information about the current financial health of the company, including liquidity, leverage, and asset financing. It contains three general financial groups:
>- **Assets** (for accounting purposes) is any resource that is expected to provide future economic benefits. This includes any resource with quantifiable value, such as cash, inventory, machines, buildings, and even contracts with a monetary value. **Goodwill**, which arises when a company acquires another company for more than the fair market value of the acquired company's equity, is also included as part of assets. Goodwill can include brand reputation, customer loyalty, etc.
>- **Liabilities** are financial obligations. This includes money that the corporation owes to lenders, suppliers, employees, etc.
>- **Equity** is the residual value left for owners of a company after the value of its liabilities is subtracted from the value of its assets. This is referred to as the company’s **Book Value**.
>
>>[!warning] The Balance Sheet is a Snapshot
>>Since the balance sheet reflects a single point in time, it should be analyzed alongside the **income statement** and **cash flow statement** for a full financial picture.
