>[!note] Moment Generating Function (MGF)
>A **moment generating function (MGF)** encodes all of the *moments* $\mathbb{E}[X^n]$ of a [[2.1 Random Variables|random variable]] into a single function, making it easier to study its distributional properties. Formally, the moment-generating function of a random variable $X$ is defined as
>$$M_{X}(t)=\mathbb{E}[e^{tX}] = \begin{dcases}\sum^{}_{x \in \mathcal{X}}e^{tx}p_{X}(x) & x \text{ discrete}, \\ \int^{\infty}_{-\infty}e^{tx}f_{X}(x)dx & x \text{ continuous}.\end{dcases}$$
>The MGF of $X$ can then be used to compute the $n$-th moment of $X$, such that
>$$\frac{d^n}{dx^n}M_{X}(t) \bigg |_{x=0}=\mathbb{E}[X^n].$$

# Properties
#### (Linearity) If $Y=\alpha\\X+\beta$, then $M_{Y}(t)=e^{t\beta}M_{X}(\alpha x)$.

#### (Independence) If $X,Y$ are independent, then $M_{X+Y}(t)=M_{X}(t)M_{Y}(t)$.
