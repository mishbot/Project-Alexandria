>[!note] Isobaric Process
>An **isobaric process** changes the state of a gas while its pressure stays fixed. On a $P$-$V$ diagram it is a horizontal line. With $P$ constant, the [[2.2.3 Ideal Gas Law|ideal gas law]] gives $V \propto T$, so the gas expands when heated and contracts when cooled. The [[2.5.1 Thermodynamic Work|work]] done by the gas is the area of a rectangle,
>$$W = P\,\Delta V = P\,(V_2 - V_1) = Nk\,\Delta T,$$
>positive for an expansion and negative for a compression. The change in [[2.4.1 Internal Energy|internal energy]] is $\Delta E_{\text{int}} = \tfrac{d}{2}\, P\,\Delta V$, and the [[2.5.2 First Law of Thermodynamics|first law]] gives the heat,
>$$Q = \Delta E_{\text{int}} + W = \left( \tfrac{d}{2} + 1 \right) P\,\Delta V = \left( \tfrac{d}{2} + 1 \right) Nk\,\Delta T.$$
>Written per mole, $Q = n C_P\,\Delta T$ with the [[2.5.4 Molar Specific Heats|molar specific heat at constant pressure]] $C_P = \left( \tfrac{d}{2} + 1 \right) R$. Of the heat added, the fraction $\tfrac{d}{2} / \left( \tfrac{d}{2} + 1 \right)$ becomes internal energy and the fraction $1 / \left( \tfrac{d}{2} + 1 \right)$ becomes work. For a diatomic gas that is $5/7$ and $2/7$.
>
>An isobaric process is realized by a cylinder with a frictionless piston of area $A$ carrying a mass $m$ under atmospheric pressure $P_{\text{atm}}$. The piston rises or falls until the gas pressure balances the load, so
>$$P = P_{\text{atm}} + \frac{mg}{A},$$
>and it stays at that value however the gas is heated or cooled. With a vacuum above the piston, $P_{\text{atm}}$ drops out and $P = mg/A$. The gas then fills a height $h$ with $V = Ah$, so $P = mgh/V$. Most processes open to the air, such as a pot on a stove, are isobaric at one atmosphere.

# Intuition

A gas under a weighted piston cannot change its pressure. The piston settles where the upward push $PA$ of the gas balances the downward pull of the load and the atmosphere, and any imbalance moves the piston until the balance returns. Heat the gas and it expands, lifting the load. Part of the heat becomes the kinetic energy of the molecules, which is the rise in temperature, and part becomes the work of lifting. The gas therefore needs more heat per kelvin than it would in a sealed container, and the extra is exactly the work done. The sketch shows the setup.

# Properties

#### *(Volume Tracks Temperature)* With $P$ fixed, $V_2 / V_1 = T_2 / T_1$ in kelvins.

This is the constant-pressure [[2.2.2 Gas Laws|gas law]], and it makes the work easy to write in either variable: $P\,\Delta V$ from the diagram or $Nk\,\Delta T$ from the thermometer. Doubling the absolute temperature doubles the volume. A balloon warmed from $20\ ^\circ\text{C}$ to $40\ ^\circ\text{C}$ grows by $7$ percent, not by a factor of two.

#### *(A Fixed Share Becomes Work)* At constant pressure a fixed fraction of the heat, $2/(d + 2)$, leaves as work.

The split between internal energy and work does not depend on how much heat is added or on the size of the sample. For helium two fifths of the heat lifts the piston. For nitrogen two sevenths does. The rest raises the temperature. This fixed share is the reason $C_P$ exceeds $C_V$ by exactly $R$: the work per mole per kelvin is $P\,\Delta V / (n\,\Delta T) = R$.

#### *(The Piston Sets the Pressure)* The load on the piston, not the gas, decides the pressure.

Newton's second law on a piston at rest reads $PA = P_{\text{atm}} A + mg$. The gas has no say in the matter. If the gas pressure momentarily exceeds this value, the piston accelerates upward and the gas expands until the pressure drops back. The process is isobaric only if the piston moves slowly enough for the gas to stay uniform. A piston that shoots up leaves a pressure wave behind it, and the gas has no single pressure to plot.

#### *(Compression Releases Heat)* An isobaric compression has $W < 0$, $\Delta E_{\text{int}} < 0$, and $Q < 0$.

Run the process backward and every sign flips. The gas is cooled, it shrinks, the surroundings do work on it, and it gives up heat equal to the internal energy lost plus the work received. The heat rejected in an isobaric compression is larger than the internal energy change, because the work done on the gas also has to leave as heat.

# Examples

>[!example] (Easy) Heating Air Under a Free Piston
>Air at $1.00\ \text{atm}$ is warmed at constant pressure from $1.00\ \text{L}$ to $1.50\ \text{L}$.
>
>>[!example]- Find the work done, the change in internal energy, and the heat added.
>>The work is the rectangle,
>>$$W = P\,\Delta V = (1.013 \times 10^{5}\ \text{Pa})(0.50 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{m}^3) = 50.7\ \text{J}.$$
>>Air is diatomic, $d = 5$, so $\Delta E_{\text{int}} = \tfrac{5}{2} P\,\Delta V = 127\ \text{J}$ and $Q = \tfrac{7}{2} P\,\Delta V = 177\ \text{J}$. The work is $2/7 = 29$ percent of the heat. The temperatures were never needed.

>[!example] (Medium) A Weighted Piston
>A cylinder with a frictionless piston of area $0.0100\ \text{m}^2$ carries a $5.00\ \text{kg}$ block under atmospheric pressure. It holds $2.00\ \text{L}$ of nitrogen at $300\ \text{K}$, and the gas is heated to $400\ \text{K}$.
>
>>[!example]- Find the pressure, the work done by the gas, and the heat added.
>>The piston sets the pressure,
>>$$P = P_{\text{atm}} + \frac{mg}{A} = 1.013 \times 10^{5} + \frac{(5.00)(9.80)}{0.0100} = 1.062 \times 10^{5}\ \text{Pa}.$$
>>At fixed pressure the volume grows with $T$, so $V_2 = (2.00\ \text{L})(400/300) = 2.67\ \text{L}$ and
>>$$W = P\,\Delta V = (1.062 \times 10^{5})(0.667 \times 10^{-3}) = 70.8\ \text{J}.$$
>>With $d = 5$, $Q = \tfrac{7}{2} W = 248\ \text{J}$. As a check, $Nk = PV_1/T_1 = 0.708\ \text{J/K}$, so $W = Nk\,\Delta T = 70.8\ \text{J}$, the same. Of the $248\ \text{J}$, $177\ \text{J}$ warmed the gas and $70.8\ \text{J}$ lifted the block and pushed back the atmosphere.
