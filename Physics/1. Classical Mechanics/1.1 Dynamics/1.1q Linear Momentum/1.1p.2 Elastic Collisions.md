>[!note] Elastic Collision
>An **elastic collision** is a type of collision in which the total **kinetic energy** and the total **momentum** of the system are conserved. In an elastic collision, ***no energy is lost*** to heat, sound, or deformation; instead, the ***kinetic energy is fully retained*** in the system, such that
>$$\begin{alignat}{0}
>\underbrace{m_A\vec{v}_A + m_B\vec{v}_B}_\text{Momentum before collision} = \underbrace{m_A\vec{v}'_A + m_B\vec{v}'_B}_\text{Momentum after collision} \\
>\text{and} \\
>\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}m_A\vec{v}_A^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_B\vec{v}_B^2}_\text{KE before collision} = \underbrace{\frac{1}{2}m_A\vec{v}'^2_A + 
>\frac{1}{2} m_B\vec{v}'^2_B}_\text{KE after collision}
\end{alignat}.$$
> This implies that in $1$-dimensional calculations, the relative speed of the two objects before the collision equals their speed of separation after the collision, such that
> $$v_{A}+v_{A}'=V_{B}+V_{B}'.$$
> A consequence of this means that in an elastic collision, the colliding objects ***change their direction after the collision***.

# Examples
>[!example] Two Blocks, One Spring
>A 3.0-kg block slides along a frictionless tabletop at 8.0 m/s toward a second block (at rest) of mass 4.5 kg. A coil spring, which obeys Hooke's law and has spring constant $k = 760 \text{ N/m}$, is attached to the second block in such a way that it will be compressed when struck by the moving block. 
>
>A collision involving a spring will lead to the blocks sticking together first. Therefore, we can say that
>$$m_{A}v_{A}+m_{B}v_{B}=(m_{A}+m_{B})v_{f} \iff v_{f}=\frac{m_{A}}{m_{A}+m_{B}}v_{A}$$
>Since block $B$ is at rest, $m_{B}v_{B}=0$. Since the system is elastic, $v_{f}=v_{A}$, as all energy is conserved. We can then apply the law of conservation of energy in the same way, such that
>$$\frac{1}{2}m_{A}v_{A}^2=\frac{1}{2}(m_{A}+m_{B})v_{f}^2+\frac{1}{2}kx^2 \iff kx^2 = m_{A}v_{A}^2-(m_{A}+m_{B})\left(\frac{m_{A}}{m_{A}+m_{B}} \right)^2v_{A}^2$$
>This yields $x \approx 0.39$.
