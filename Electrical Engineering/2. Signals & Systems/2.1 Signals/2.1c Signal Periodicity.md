>[!note] Periodicity of Signals
>A ***continuous-time*** signal $x$ is said to be periodic if there is a real number $p > 0$, such that
>$$x(t + p) = x(t) \enspace \text{for all } t.$$
>The smallest $p$ for which the above equation holds is called the fundamental period of $x$. The fundamental frequency of $x$ is denoted by
>$$w_{0}=\frac{2\pi}{p}.$$
>
>Additionally, all periodic ***discrete-time*** signals share a fundamental period $p=1$, as well as a fundamental frequency $\omega_{0} = 2\pi$.
>
>>[!warning] Note 1 (Constant Signals)
>>Constant signals (i.e. DC signals) have an undefined period and a fundamental frequency $\omega_{0} = 0$.
>
>>[!warning] Note 2 (Compound Signals)
>>If a signal  $x$ is formed by combining several periodic signals, its overall period is the least common multiple of their individual periods.
