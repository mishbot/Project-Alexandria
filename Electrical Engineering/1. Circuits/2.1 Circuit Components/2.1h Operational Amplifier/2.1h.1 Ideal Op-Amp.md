>[!note] Operational Amplifier
>An operational amplifier (op-amp) is a high-gain, direct current (DC) coupled electronic device commonly used in analog signal processing. It amplifies the difference between its two input voltages $V_{+}$ and $V_{-}$ (non-inverting and inverting), and produces an output voltage $V_{\text{out}}=A(V_{+}-V_{-})$ for some op-amp gain $A$. Additionally, an op-amp contains power supply inputs $\pm V_{cc}$, which act as voltage rails, such that $|V_{\text{out}}| \leq V_{cc}$, or $[-V_{cc}, V_{cc}]$ is the op-amp's linear dynamic range.

>An ideal operational amplifier follows the following "golden" rules:
>1. The current of both differential inputs is zero, such that $i_{+}=i_{-}=0$.
>2. The voltages of both differential inputs are equal to one another, such that $V_{+}=V_{-}$.
>3. This is all caused by setting $R_{i}=\infty$. Additionally, $R_{o}=0$, and the op-amp gain $A=\infty$.
