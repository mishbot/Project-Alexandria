>[!note] Vector Spaces
>A **vector space** $\mathcal{X}$ is a set of [[3.1. Vector|vectors]] that is **closed under addition** and **scalar multiplication**, such that it remains unchanged when those operations are performed. Formally, for any $u,v \in \mathcal{X}$ and $\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{R}$, 
>$$\alpha u+ \beta v \in \mathcal{X}.$$
>
>A nonempty subset $\mathcal{V} \subseteq \mathcal{X}$ of the vector space is called a **subspace** of $\mathcal{X}$ if it is also closed under addition and scalar multiplication.
>
>An **affine subspace** is a subspace shifted away from the origin) $\mathcal{A} \subseteq \mathcal{V}$, where every elements satisfies
>$$\mathcal{A}=\{ \alpha \vec{x} + (1-\alpha)\vec{y} : \vec{x},\vec{y} \in \mathcal{V}, \alpha \in \mathbb{R} \}.$$
>
>>[!warning] Zero Vector
>>Both vector spaces and subspaces **always contain the zero vector** (confirmed by setting $\alpha = 0$ and $\beta = 0$).

# Properties
#### (*Sum of Subspaces*) For all subspaces $\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{V}$, their sum $\mathcal{U}+\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{W}$ is also a subspace.

#### (*Direct Sum*) For all subspaces $\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{V}$, if $\mathcal{U} \cap \mathcal{V}=\{ 0 \}$, then $\mathcal{U} \oplus \mathcal{V}=\mathcal{U}+\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{W}$ and every vector in $\mathcal{W}$ can be uniquely represented as $w=u+v$.

#### (Dimension of Direct Sum) For any $\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{V}$, $\dim(\mathcal{U}+\mathcal{V})=\dim\mathcal{U}+\dim\mathcal{V}$.
