>[!note] Projectile Motion
>**Projectile motion** is the motion of an object thrown or projected into the air, subject only to the force of gravity and initial velocity. It follows a parabolic trajectory, with horizontal motion being uniform and vertical motion influenced by constant acceleration due to gravity:
>```tikz
>\begin{document}
>\begin{tikzpicture}
>
>    % Axes
>    \draw[->] (0,0) -- (7,0) node[right] {$x$};
>    \draw[->] (0,0) -- (0,3.5) node[above] {$y$};
>
>    % Parabolic trajectory
>    \draw[thick, dashed, domain=0:6.5] plot(\x, {1.5*\x - 0.25*\x*\x});
>
>    % Initial velocity and components
>    \draw[thick,->,green] (0,0) -- (1,2) node[pos=0.6,right] {$\vec{v}_0$};
>    \draw[->,green,dashed] (0,0) -- (1,0) node[pos=0.5,below] {$\vec{v}_{x0}$};
>    \draw[->,green,dashed] (0,0) -- (0,2) node[pos=0.5,left] {$\vec{v}_{y0}$};
>
>    % Angle theta
>    \draw[->] (0.8,0) arc[start angle=0,end angle=54,radius=0.8];
>    \node at (1,0.5) {$\theta_0$};
>
>    % Tangential velocity vectors at different points on the parabola
>    \draw[thick,->,green] (4.75,1.5) -- +(0.5,-0.5) node[pos=0.8,right] {$\vec{v}$};
>    \draw[thick,->,green] (6,0) -- +(1,-2) node[pos=0.8,right] {$\vec{v}$};
>
>    % Velocity components at points along the parabola
>    \draw[thick,->,green] (2.85,2.25) -- +(0.5,0) node[right] {$\vec{v}$};
>    \draw[thick] (2.85, 2.25) -- (3.5, 3) node[right] {$\vec{v}_y = 0$};
>
>    \draw[thick,->,green,dashed] (4.75,1.5) -- +(0.5,0) node[above] {$\vec{v}_x$};
>    \draw[thick,->,green,dashed] (4.75,1.5) -- +(0,-0.5) node[left] {$\vec{v}_y$};
>
>\end{tikzpicture}
>\end{document}
>```
>Then, the horizontal length $R$ between the two roots of the parabola is the total distance traveled by the object, also called the level horizontal range of the object. It is generally given by
>$$R = v_{x_0}t.$$
>When $y_0 = y_f$ (i.e. the object lands at the same height that it was projected from), the level horizontal range is given as
>$$\begin{align}&&R=\frac{v_0^2 \sin 2 \theta}{g} && (\text{for } y_0 = y_f)\end{align},$$
>since $t=\frac{2v_{y_0}}{g}$ is the total time of flight for any projectile launched from and landing back at the same height.
