**Regression** predicts a numeric response from one or more inputs. The notes in this folder cover the canonical machine-learning treatment: ordinary least squares, its matrix-form extension to many predictors, nonlinear models, robust estimation, regularized estimators, and the cross-validation machinery that selects between them. Classification (predicting a categorical response) lives in [[2. Classification Models|the next folder]]; the regression side here is the foundation it builds on.

## Contents

- [[1.1. Regression]]: what regression *is*, the supervised-learning framing, and the bias-variance setup.
- [[1.2. Linear Regression]]: simple linear regression by OLS; the closed-form $\hat\beta_1 = \widehat{\text{Cov}}(x,y)/\widehat{\text{Var}}(x)$, Gauss-Markov, and Gaussian-noise MLE.
- [[1.2a. Multiple Linear Regression]]: matrix-form OLS with $\hat{\boldsymbol\beta} = (\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{y}$, the hat matrix, residual variance, and adjusted $R^2$.
- [[1.3. RANSAC]]: robust regression by random consensus, for designs corrupted by outliers.
- [[1.4. Nonlinear Regression]]: nonlinear least squares, Gauss-Newton, Levenberg-Marquardt, asymptotic inference.
- [[1.5. Regularization]]: ridge, lasso, elastic net; geometric picture, bias-variance trade-off, scale-equivariance.
- [[1.6. Cross-Validation]]: $k$-fold, leave-one-out, train-test, stratified and nested variants, with rolling-origin reserved for the time-series adaptation.

## Cross-domain applications

- [[2.4 Time Series Regression]] applies this machinery in the time-indexed setting (trend + seasonal + lag predictors), with [[2.4.5 Time-Series Cross-Validation|rolling-origin CV]] replacing random $k$-fold.
