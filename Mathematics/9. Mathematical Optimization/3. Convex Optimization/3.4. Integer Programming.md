> [!note] Integer Programs
>An **Integer Program (IP)** is an optimization problem in which the objective function and constraints are affine, as in a [[3.2. Linear Programming|linear program]], but some or all decision variables are restricted to take integer values. A common binary formulation is
> 
>$$\begin{aligned}
> \min_{x \in \mathbb{R}^n} \quad & a_0^\top x \\
> \text{s.t.} \quad & Ax = b, \\
> & Cx \le d, \\
> & x_i \in \{ 0,1 \}, \quad i = 1,\dots,n.
> \end{aligned}$$
>   
>Without the integrality constraints, this problem is exactly a **linear program**, whose feasible set is a convex polyhedron formed by the intersection of hyperplanes and half-spaces. Imposing $x_i \in \{0,1\}$ restricts feasibility to a finite set of points inside that polyhedron, destroying convexity. As a result, integer programs are **not convex optimization problems**, and convex combinations of feasible solutions are generally infeasible.
> 
>The standard way to analyze and solve integer programs is through their LP [[3.3. Convex Relaxation|relaxation]], obtained by replacing $x_{i} \in \{ 0, 1 \}$ with $0 \le x_{i} \le 1$. This relaxed problem is a linear program and therefore convex. Its optimal value provides a lower bound on the optimal value of the integer program.
>
> Geometrically, integer programs can be viewed as selecting discrete points from a polyhedron, while LP relaxations optimize over the entire polyhedron. This explains both why LPs are efficiently solvable and why IPs are generally NP-hard. Thus, integer programs are best understood as **linear programs with additional integrality structure**.

>[!example] Vertex Cover as IP and Relaxation
>Given an undirected graph $G = (V, E)$ with a cost function $c : V \to R^+$, the vertex cover problem asks us to find the minimum cost subset of vertices such that every edge in $G$ is incident on some vertex in that subset.
