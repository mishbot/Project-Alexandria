>[!note] Thermodynamic Processes
>A thermodynamic process is any path a gas can follow on a $P$-$V$ diagram, and there are infinitely many. Four of them have names because they hold something fixed and therefore have closed forms for the work and the heat. The [[2.5.3a Isovolumetric Process|isovolumetric process]] fixes the volume, the [[2.5.3b Isobaric Process|isobaric process]] fixes the pressure, and the [[2.5.3c Isothermal Process|isothermal process]] fixes the temperature. Each of these comes from fixing one variable in $PV = NkT$. The [[2.5.3d Adiabatic Process|adiabatic process]] fixes none of them and instead fixes the heat flow at zero, so its path $PV^{\gamma} = \text{const}$ has to be derived from the first law.
>
>Every process is analyzed the same way. The path gives the work $W$ as the area under it. The endpoints give $\Delta E_{\text{int}} = \tfrac{d}{2}(P_2 V_2 - P_1 V_1)$. The [[2.5.2 First Law of Thermodynamics|first law]] then gives $Q = \Delta E_{\text{int}} + W$. The table collects the results for an ideal gas with $d$ accessible degrees of freedom and $\gamma = 1 + 2/d$.
>
>| Process | Fixed | Path | $W$ | $\Delta E_{\text{int}}$ | $Q$ |
>|---|---|---|---|---|---|
>| Isovolumetric | $V$ | vertical line | $0$ | $\tfrac{d}{2} V \Delta P$ | $\tfrac{d}{2} V \Delta P$ |
>| Isobaric | $P$ | horizontal line | $P \Delta V$ | $\tfrac{d}{2} P \Delta V$ | $\left(\tfrac{d}{2} + 1\right) P \Delta V$ |
>| Isothermal | $T$ | $PV = \text{const}$ | $NkT \ln(V_2/V_1)$ | $0$ | $NkT \ln(V_2/V_1)$ |
>| Adiabatic | $Q = 0$ | $PV^{\gamma} = \text{const}$ | $\tfrac{d}{2}(P_1 V_1 - P_2 V_2)$ | $\tfrac{d}{2}(P_2 V_2 - P_1 V_1)$ | $0$ |
>
>Every $\Delta$ is final minus initial, and the arrow on the path says which is which. The same entries in molar form replace $Nk$ by $nR$ and $\tfrac{d}{2} Nk\,\Delta T$ by $n C_V\,\Delta T$.

# Prerequisites

- [[2.5.2 First Law of Thermodynamics]], for $Q = \Delta E_{\text{int}} + W$.
- [[2.5.1 Thermodynamic Work]], for the area under a path.
- [[2.3.4 Equipartition of Energy]], for $d$ and $\Delta E_{\text{int}} = \tfrac{d}{2} Nk\,\Delta T$.

# Contents

- [[2.5.3a Isovolumetric Process]]: a sealed container, no work, all heat into internal energy, $C_V = \tfrac{d}{2} R$.
- [[2.5.3b Isobaric Process]]: a weighted piston, $P = P_{\text{atm}} + mg/A$, $W = P\,\Delta V$, a fixed share of the heat becomes work, $C_P = (\tfrac{d}{2} + 1) R$.
- [[2.5.3c Isothermal Process]]: a bath and a slow piston, the logarithm formula, $Q = W$. **Not taught:** why no heat capacity describes it.
- [[2.5.3d Adiabatic Process]]: insulation or speed, $PV^{\gamma} = \text{const}$, $W = -\Delta E_{\text{int}}$, the fire syringe, the family of paths $PV^x = \text{const}$, and sudden compression. **Not taught:** paths $PV^x$ with other exponents and sudden compression.

# Recommended Path

Read [[2.5.3a Isovolumetric Process]] and [[2.5.3b Isobaric Process]] first, since both follow from the gas law in one line and together explain $C_P - C_V = R$. Then read [[2.5.3c Isothermal Process]] for the first curved path, and finish with [[2.5.3d Adiabatic Process]], whose derivation uses the first law in differential form.
