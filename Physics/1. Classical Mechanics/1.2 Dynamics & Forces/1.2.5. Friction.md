>[!note] Friction
>**Friction** is a resistive force that opposes the relative motion or tendency of motion between two surfaces in contact, and it always **acts parallel to the contact surface**:
>1. **Static friction** acts when two surfaces are in contact but not moving relative to each other. It prevents motion from starting and adjusts its magnitude up to a maximum limit, such that
>$$|\vec{F}_{\text{fr}}| \leq \mu_s |\vec{F}_N|,$$
>where $\mu_s$ is the coefficient of static friction, and $\vec{F}_N$ is the normal force.
>‎ 
>2. **Kinetic friction** (or sliding friction) acts when two surfaces slide against each other. It has a constant magnitude given by
>$$|\vec{F}_{\text{fr}}| = \mu_k |\vec{F}_N|,$$
>where $\mu_k$ is the coefficient of kinetic friction. Kinetic friction is typically smaller than the maximum static friction.

# Examples

>[!example] (Easy) Pushing a Box on a Bigger Box on a Frictionless Surface
>A smaller block of mass $m$ is placed on a larger block of mass $M$: 
>
>![[Screenshot 2025-08-01 at 12.57.27.png|250]]
>
>The surface between the larger block and the horizontal surface is frictionless. On the other hand, there is friction between the two blocks and the coefficient of static friction is $\mu_{s}$. A horizontal force $F$ is applied on the smaller block.
>>[!example]- Find the acceleration of the blocks.
>>In the horizontal direction, the smaller block $m$ has a resistive backward static friction force $F_{s}$, as well as the pushing force applied forward $F$. Similarly, since the blocks move together, there is a forward static friction force $F_{s}$ applied to the larger box $M$. As such, per Newton's Second Law,
>>$$\begin{cases}F-F_{s}=ma \\ F_{s}=Ma\end{cases}$$
>>which when solved for accleration leads to $a=\dfrac{F}{M+m}$.
>
>>[!example]- Find the normal force $N$ between the two blocks.
>>In the vertical direction of mass $m$, an upward normal force is exerted on it and a downward force of gravity $mg$ too. As such, it holds that $N-mg=0$, since the blocks do not move vertically. Therefore, $N=mg$.
>
>>[!example]- What is the maximal value of the force $F$, denoted as $F_{max}$, such that the smaller block can remain on the larger block without sliding on it?
>>As seen before, it holds that $F-F_{s}=ma$. Given the acceleration from part 1, one obtains$F_{s} = F-ma \leq \mu_{s}mg$, and therefore
>>$$F\left( 1-\frac{m}{M+m} \right) \leq \mu_{s}mg \iff F \leq \frac{\mu_{s}(M+m)m}{M}g=F_{\text{max}}.$$
