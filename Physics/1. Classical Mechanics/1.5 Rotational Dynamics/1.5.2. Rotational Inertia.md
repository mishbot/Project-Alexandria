>[!note] Rotational Inertia
>**Rotational inertia** (also called the **moment of inertia**) is the rotational analogue of mass. While mass $m$ in translational motion measures resistance to changes in linear velocity, rotational inertia measures a rigid body’s resistance to changes in angular velocity. It depends on both the distribution of mass and the distance of each mass element from the axis of rotation, such that
>$$I = \sum_i m_i r_i^2,$$
>where $m_{i}$ is the mass of each object in the system, and $r_{i}$ is its distance from the axis of rotation.

# Derivation
Given the definition of rotational inertia, we can redefine kinetic energy $K=\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$ as rotational kinetic energy $K_{\text{rot}}=\dfrac{1}{2}I \omega^2$. Now imagine a circle, and draw its radius as a line. Given two points on the same line, one will have traveled more distance than the other. As such, we can say that **the rotational kinetic energy of a rotating object is the sum of translational kinetic energy of the points that make up the object**, such that
$$K=\sum_{i} \frac{1}{2}m_{i}v_{i}^2.$$
This is shown in the following graph, given a 2D disk:

```tikz
\begin{document}
\begin{tikzpicture}[line width=0.8pt, scale=0.95, rotate=60]
  % Disk outline (no fill)
  \draw (0,0) circle [radius=3cm];

  % Axis (pivot)
  \fill (0,0) circle (2pt);
  \node[below left] at (0,0) {axis};

  % Dotted radius (instead of the red rod)
  \draw[dotted] (0,0) -- (3,0);

  % Rotation arrow and label
  \draw[->] (1.1,0.7) arc[start angle=35, end angle=155, radius=1.2cm];
  \node at (0,1.5) {$\omega$};

  % Mass points along the radius
  \def\rone{2.7}  % r1
  \def\rtwo{1.7}  % r2
  \fill (\rone,0) circle (2pt);
  \fill (\rtwo,0) circle (2pt);

  % Velocity vectors (perpendicular to radius)
  \draw[->] (\rone,0) -- ++(0,0.9);
  \draw[->] (\rtwo,0) -- ++(0,0.6);

  % Labels
  \node[below] at (\rone,-0.15) {$m_1$};
  \node[below] at (\rtwo,-0.15) {$m_2$};
  \node[right] at (\rone,0.85) {$v_1$};
  \node[right] at (\rtwo,0.55) {$v_2$};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{document}
```

As such, given $v_{i}=\omega r_{i}$, this becomes
$$K=\sum_{i} \frac{1}{2}m_{i}r_{i}^2\omega^2,$$
which leads us to the definition of the moment of inertia, $I=\sum_{i}m_{i}r_{i}^2$.
