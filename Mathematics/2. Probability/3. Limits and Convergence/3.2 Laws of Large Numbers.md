>[!note] Laws of Large Numbers
>**The Weak Law of Large Numbers** is a direct consequence of [[3.1 Concentration Inequalities|Chebyshev’s inequality]] and describes the convergence of sample averages to the expected value. Formally, if $X_1, X_2, \dots$ are i.i.d. with $\mathbb{E}[X_i] = \mu$ and $\mathrm{Var}(X_i) = \sigma^2 < \infty$, then for every $\varepsilon > 0$,
$$P\left(\left|\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n X_i - \mu\right| \ge \varepsilon\right) \to 0 \quad \text{as} \quad n \to \infty.$$
Since the variance of the sample mean is $\sigma^2/n$, Chebyshev’s inequality shows that the probability of a deviation of size $\varepsilon \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$, establishing convergence in probability.
