>[!note] Discrete-Time Fourier Transform (DTFT)
>The Discrete-Time Fourier Transform is a mathematical tool used in signal processing to analyze the frequency content of **non-periodic discrete-time signals** into a **continuous functions of frequency**, providing a complete representation of the signal’s frequency spectrum, such that
>$$x[n]=\frac{1}{2\pi} \int^{\pi}_{-\pi}X(\omega)e^{j\omega n}d\omega \iff X(\omega)=\sum^{\infty}_{n=-\infty}x[n]e^{-j\omega n}.$$


# Intuition
We can easily take the analysis equation of a discrete-time Fourier transform just as
$$X(\omega)=\sum^{\infty}_{n=-\infty}x[n]e^{-j\omega n},$$
since this is simply the frequency response of $x[n]$. The intuition behind this equivalence is that both $X(\omega)$ and $H(\omega)$ identically describe how $x[n]$ behaves in the frequency domain.

However, the question is to find the synthesis equation. To proceed, we can equate each of the basis vectors to $\phi_n(\omega)=e^{-j\omega n}$ and find an orthogonal basis by imagining $X(\omega)$ as a weighted linear combination of the basis vectors. We know that $X(\omega)$ is periodic over $2\pi$ (and therefore the DTFT is periodic in frequency). How do we define an inner product in a periodic frequency space? We proceed by defining an inner product of any two $2\pi$-periodic frequency vectors $F, G$, such that the inner product is integrated over a period of $2\pi$:
$$\langle F,G \rangle = \int^{\pi}_{-\pi} F(\omega) G\textasteriskcentered(\omega) d\omega.$$
Then,
$$\langle H, \phi_k \rangle = \sum^{\infty}_{n=-\infty}x[n]\langle \phi_n, \phi_k \rangle=x[k]\langle \phi_k, \phi_k \rangle \iff x[n]= \frac{\langle H, \phi_k \rangle}{\langle \phi_k, \phi_k \rangle}.$$
Since $\langle \phi_k, \phi_k \rangle = 2\pi$ (the Fourier basis of $X(\omega)$ is $2\pi$-periodic), then
$$x[n] = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int^{\pi}_{-\pi}X(\omega)\phi_n\textasteriskcentered(\omega)d\omega = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int^{\pi}_{-\pi}X(\omega)e^{j\omega n}d\omega.$$

# Examples
>[!example] DTFT Analysis of $x[n] = \delta[n]$
>The DTFT of $x[n] = \delta[n]$ can be written as
>$$X(\omega) = \sum^{\infty}_{n=-\infty}\delta[n]e^{-j\omega n} = 1.$$
>Therefore, the Fourier transform $x[n] = \delta[n]$ is $X(\omega) = 1 \enspace \forall \omega$.

>[!example] DTFT Synthesis of $X(\omega) = \delta(\omega)$
>The DTFT synthesis of $X(\omega) = \delta(\omega)$ can be written as
>$$x[n] = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}\delta(\omega)e^{j\omega n}d\omega = \frac{1}{2\pi}.$$
>Therefore, the Fourier synthesis results in $x[n] = \frac{1}{2\pi} \enspace \forall n$.
