>[!note] Markov Model
>A **Markov Model** is an abstract probabilistic model that represents a system evolving through time where the **future depends only on the present**, not on the past. This is known as the [[5.1 Markov Chains|Markov property]] (or _memoryless property_), formally expressed as
>$$P(X_t | X_{0}, X_{1}, \ldots, X_{t-1}) = P(X_t | X_{t-1}),$$
>such that if each state $X_t$ is **directly observable** (i.e., can be measured without uncertainty), the model reduces to a [[5.1 Markov Chains|Markov Chain]]. In the space of probabilistic models, a Markov Model is best explained as a **special case of a [[6.3.1. Bayesian Networks|Bayes Net]]** with a repeated, time-indexed structure.
>
>Each variable $X_t$ captures the system’s state at time $t$. To fully specify the model, we need:
>- An **initial distribution** $P(X_0)$, describing where the process begins.
>- A **transition model** $P(X_{t+1} | X_t)$, describing how it evolves between timesteps.
>
>Using these, the **joint distribution** across $n$ variables expands by the chain rule and the Markov assumption as
>$$P(X_0, X_1, \dots, X_n) = P(X_0) \prod_{i=0}^{n-1} P(X_{i+1} | X_i).$$
>
>To compute the distribution of the variable at a future timestep without expanding the entire joint distribution, we use the **Mini-Forward Algorithm**, which recursively advances the belief state through time:
>$$P(X_{t+1}) =\sum_{x_t}P(x_{t}, X_{t+1}) = \sum_{x_t} P(X_{t+1} \mid x_t) \, P(x_t).$$
>
>This propagation continues iteratively, starting from $P(X_0)$, to obtain the marginal at any future $t$. Over time, the model may converge to a **stationary distribution**, satisfying
>$$P(W_{t+1}) = P(W_t),$$
>which represents the equilibrium probabilities of each state once the process stabilizes.
