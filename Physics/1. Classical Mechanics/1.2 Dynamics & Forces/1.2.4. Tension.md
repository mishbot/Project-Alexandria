>[!note] Tension Force
>**Tension**, denoted $\vec{F}_{T}$ or $\vec{T}$, is the contact force transmitted through a string, rope, cable, or any other flexible connector when it is pulled tight by forces acting from opposite ends. It arises from Newton’s Third Law–the object pulls on the rope, and the rope pulls back on the object with equal and opposite force:
>```tikz
>\usepackage{amsmath}
>\usepackage{tikz}
>\begin{document}
>
>\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=1.2, line cap=round, line join=round, >=stealth]
>  % Axes
>  \draw[->] (-0.5,0) -- (5,0) node[right] {$x$};
>  \draw[->] (0,-0.5) -- (0,2.5) node[above] {$y$};
>
>  % Block
>  \draw[thick] (0,0) rectangle (1.2,1.2) node[midway] {$m$};
>
>  % Rope
>  \draw[thick] (1.2,0.6) -- (3.5,0.6);
>
>  % Hand (point)
>  \filldraw[black] (3.5,0.6) circle (2pt) node[right] {Hand Pulling};
>
>  % Tension Vectors
>  \draw[->,thick] (1.2,1) -- +(0.7,0) node[midway, above] {$\vec{T}$};
>  \draw[->,thick] (3.5,1) -- +(-0.7,0) node[midway, above] {$\vec{T}$};
>
>\end{tikzpicture}
>
>\end{document}
>```
>
>
>For a massless and inextensible string, the tension is **uniform** throughout its length.

>[!example] (Easy) Simple Pulley
>A window washer pulls themselves upward using the following Atwood machine:
>![[Screenshot 2025-07-31 at 13.31.33.png|100]]
>The window washer and the triangular frame have masses of $m_{1}$ and $m_{2}$ respectively. The window washer lifts themself up slowly with a constant acceleration $a$.
>>[!example]- Find the force at which the window washer pulls the rope.
>>Since two vertical segments of rope support the same object, $$(m_{1}+m_{2})a=2T-(m_{1}+m_{2})g \iff T=\dfrac{1}{2}(m_{1}+m_{2})(a+g).$$
>
>>[!example]- Find the normal force between the frame and the window washer.
>>Now consider only the triangular frame. Gravity pulls down on it with a force of $m_{2}g$, and the window washer exerts a downward force of $N$ on the frame. However, the frame is also pulled up by $T$. As such,
>>$$m_{2}a=T-N-m_{2}g \iff N=\dfrac{1}{2}(m_{1}-m_{2})(a+g).$$

>[!example] (Medium) Double Pulley
>A window washer pulls themselves upward using the following double Atwood machine:
>![[Screenshot 2025-07-31 at 13.35.34.png|150]]
>The window washer and the triangular frame have masses of $m_{1}$ and $m_{2}$ respectively. The window washer lifts themself up slowly with a constant acceleration $a$.
>>[!example]- If the washer pulls down the rope by an amount $|\Delta y|$, how much will the washer and the frame go up?
>>Let $L=\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}$ represent the initial length of the rope based on the length from the ceiling to the first pulley, the length from the first pulley to the second, and from the second to the window washer's hand. Then, if the window washer pulls down $\ell_{3}$, this means that both $\ell_{1}$ and $\ell_{2}$ are shortened by some $x$. As such, $L=(\ell_{1}-x)+(\ell_{2}-x)+(\ell_{3}+|\Delta y|)$, which simplifies to $2x=|\Delta y|$ or $x=\dfrac{1}{2}|\Delta y|$.
>
>>[!example]- Find the acceleration of the system.
>>By Newton's Third Law, the rope segment pulled by the window washer has a tension of $T$. 
>>
>>Additionally, the frame and the window washer are pulled up by two tensions, which means that the total tension on the pulley is $2T$.
>>
>>As such, in the $y$-direction one can conclude that $(m_{1}+m_{2})a=3T-(m_{1}+m_{2})g$, which yields an acceleration of $a=\dfrac{3T}{m_{1}+m_{2}}-g$.
>
>>[!example]- Find the normal force between the frame and the window washer.
>>Observe the window washer. An upward tension force of $T$ is exerted on them by the rope, as well as the downward force of gravity by Earth. Then, $N$ is the upward normal force exerted on the washer by the frame. As such,
>>$$m_{1}a=N+T-m_{1}g,$$
>>which simplifies to $N=\dfrac{T(2m_{1}-m_{2})}{m_{1}+m_{2}}$.

>[!example] (Hard) Accelerating Pulley Suspended by Another Pulley
>Consider a double Atwood machine where block $A$ and $B$ have masses $m_{A}$ and $m_{B}=2m_{A}$ respectively. The pulley that hangs block $A$ and $B$ is movable, while the pulley that connects with block $C$ is fixed. The system is shown below:
>![[Screenshot 2025-07-31 at 16.54.17.png|150]]
>>[!example]- What mass should block $C$ have to remain stationary?
>>Examine the right rope of the upper Atwood, which is attached to the lower Atwood. Let the tension in that side of the rope be $T_{2}$, such that the total tension of the rope is $2T_{2}$. 
>>
>>By Newton’s Second Law applied to the movable pulley, the total upward force $T_2$ must balance the two downward tensions from the Atwood rope, each of magnitude $T_1$, so $T_2 = 2T_1$.
>>
>>Now, examine the lower Atwood. For blocks $A$ and $B$, one obtains the following system of equations. Note that the second equation has a negative sign to denote that block $B$ is moving downward:
>>$$\begin{cases}\hphantom{-}m_{A}a = T_{1}-m_{A}g \\ -m_{B}a = T_{1}-m_{B}g \end{cases}.$$
>>Solving this system leads to $T_{1} = \dfrac{2m_{A}m_{B}}{m_{A}+m_{B}}g = \dfrac{4}{3}mg$.
>>
>>Now observe weight $C$, where $T_{2}-m_{C}g=0$. Since $T_{2}=2T_{1}$, then $m_{C}=\dfrac{4m_{A}m_{B}}{m_{A}+m_{B}}=\dfrac{8}{3}m$.
>
>>[!example]- Let block $C$ have a mass of $3m$. What is the acceleration of block $C$ after all blocks are released from rest?
>>Note that $T_{2}=2T_{1}$ still holds.
>>
>>One must first check whether the pulley outweighs block $C$ in order to determine the sign of the acceleration. Since the equilibrium weight of $C$ is $\frac{8}{3}m < 3m$, then $C$ will accelerate downward upon release.
>>
>>Now proceed as follows. It holds that $T_{2}-m_{C}g=-m_{C}a$. It is known from the last problem that the weights of a pulley accelerate at $a_{w}=2a$. As such, one obtains the following system:
>>$$\begin{cases}T_{1}-m_{A}g=2m_{A}a \\ T_{1}-m_{B}g=-2m_{B}a \\ 2T_{1}-m_{C}g=-m_{C}a.\end{cases}$$
