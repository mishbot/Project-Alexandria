>[!note] Binary Channels
>The **Binary Erasure Channel (BEC)** is a discrete channel with an **input alphabet** $\mathcal{X}=\{ 0,1 \}$ and an **output alphabet** $\mathcal{Y}=\{ 0,1,e \}$, where $e$ represents an **erasure symbol**, indicating that the transmitted bit was lost with probability $p \in [0,1]$:

>In an abstract communications system, the pipeline can be outlined as follows:
>$$M^{(L)} \xrightarrow[f_n: \mathcal{M}^{L} \to \mathcal{X}^{n}]{\text{Encoder}} X^{(n)} \xrightarrow{\text{(Noisy) Channel}} Y^{(n)} \xrightarrow[g_{n}: \mathcal{Y}^{n} \to \mathcal{M}^{L}]{\text{Decoder}} \hat{M}^{(L)}$$
>1. A message $M^{(L)}$ of binary length $L$ goes through an encoder $f_{n}: \mathcal{M}^L \to \mathcal{X}^n$, and is transformed into a codeword $X^{(n)}$ of length $n$ in channel symbols (i.e., in bits if the channel is binary). 
>2. It then goes through the channel, which outputs the random map $Y$ (also of length $n$). 
>3. Finally, the output is ran through a decoder $g_{n}: \mathcal{Y}^{n} \to \mathcal{M}^{L}$, which yields the output $\hat{M}$ of length $L$.
>
>The rate at which the message is conveyed through the channel is given as $R = L/n$ bits per channel symbols (i.e, decoded bits per encoded bits). In binary channels, $R \in [0,1]$. The capacity of a channel is the maximum asymptotic rate at which it can reliably communicate a symbol, given as
>$$C=\max_{p_{X}} I(X;Y) \text{ bits/symbol},$$
>where $C_{\text{BEC}}=1-p$, and $C_{\text{BSC}}=1-H_{b}(p)$. In other words, the channel is stable if $R < C$.
>
>For a BEC, the **maximum probability of erasure** is given as the worst-case probability that the decoder fails to recover the original message:
>$$P_{e}(n)=\mathbb{P}(\hat{M} \neq M)=\max_{x \in \mathcal{X}^L}\mathbb{P}(g(Y^n) \neq x^n \mid X^n = f_{n}(x^n)).$$
>
>Together, these quantities yield the **Shannon Channel Coding Theorem,** which states that as $n \to \infty$, there exists a rate-$R$ communication scheme that achieves 
>$$P_{e}(n) \to\begin{cases}0 & R<C, \\ c \in (0,1) & R=C \\ 1 & R>C.\end{cases}$$
