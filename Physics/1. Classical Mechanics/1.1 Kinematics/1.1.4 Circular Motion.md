>[!note] Circular Motion
>**Circular motion** occurs when an object moves along a circular path of radius $r$, meaning its direction of velocity constantly changes even if its speed stays constant.
>
>In **uniform circular motion**, the speed remains constant while direction changes:
>```tikz
>\begin{document}
>
>\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=2.5, rotate=60]
>
>    % Draw the circle (part of it)
>    \draw[thick] (1.5,1.25) arc (42:-3:2cm);
>
>    % Points A and B on the circle
>    \node[fill=black,circle,inner sep=1.5pt,label=above right:{B}] at (2,0) {};
>    \node[fill=black,circle,inner sep=1.5pt,label=above:{A}] at ({2*cos(30)},{2*sin(30)}) {};
>
>    % Center of the circle C
>    \node[fill=black,circle,inner sep=1.5pt,label=below:C] at (0,0) {};
>
>    % Draw radii
>    \draw[thick] (0,0) -- (2,0) node[midway,right] {$r$}; % Radius to A
>    \draw[thick] (0,0) -- ({2*cos(30)},{2*sin(30)}) node[midway, left] {$r$}; % Radius to B
>
>    % Arc and angle
>    \draw[->] (1,0) arc[start angle=0,end angle=30,radius=1] node[midway, below]{$\Delta \theta$};
>
>    % Tangential displacement arc (delta l)
>    \draw[dashed,thick] (2,0) -- ({2*cos(30)},{2*sin(30)}) node[pos=0.25, below] {$\Delta \ell$};
>
>    % Velocity vectors
>    \draw[->,thick,green] (1.55,1.25) -- +(0.35,-0.5) node[right] {$\vec{v}_1$}; % Velocity at A
>    \draw[->,thick,green] (2.025, 0.25) -- +(0.025, -0.65) node[right] {$\vec{v}_2$}; % Velocity at B
>
>	% Downward acceleration
>	\draw[->,thick,orange] (1.925,0.5) -- +(-0.45,-0.125) node[below] {$\vec{a} = \lim\limits_{\Delta t \to 0}\frac{\Delta \vec{v}}{\Delta t}$};
>
>\end{tikzpicture}
>
>\end{document}
>``` 
>
>The velocity $\vec{v}$ is tangent to the circle, and the acceleration $\vec{a}$ points toward the center (centripetal), such that
>$$a_R = \frac{v^2}{r}.$$
>Thus, an object revolving in a circle is continuously accelerating, even when the speed remains constant.
>
>In **non-uniform circular motion**, the speed itself changes. The acceleration then has two components: a **radial component** $a_R = v^2/r$ toward the center, and a **tangential component** $a_T = dv/dt$ along the motion’s direction. The total magnitude of acceleration is then
>$$a = \sqrt{a_R^2 + a_T^2}.$$
>The tangential part alters speed, the radial part alters direction, and together they describe any general motion along a circular path.
