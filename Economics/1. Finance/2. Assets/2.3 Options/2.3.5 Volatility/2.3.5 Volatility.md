>[!note] Volatility
>Volatility $\sigma$ is the one input of the Black-Scholes-Merton formula that no market quotes. [[2.3.5a Implied Volatility]] reads it from option prices. The implied volatility $\sigma_I$ is the volatility at which the formula matches the market price. [[2.3.5b Volatility Surface]] shows how $\sigma_I$ changes with strike and expiry, as a smile, a skew, and a term structure. No arbitrage limits how steep the skew and how inverted the term structure can be. [[2.3.5c Volatility Trading]] splits the profit of a delta-hedged option into two parts:
>- **Gamma P&L** $\frac{1}{2}\Gamma S^2(\sigma_R^2 - \sigma_I^2)\,\delta t$ is positive for a long option when the stock moves more than the price assumed.
>- **Vega P&L** $\mathcal{V}\,\delta\sigma_I$ is positive for a long option when the market raises $\sigma_I$.
>
>The same note covers covered calls, the VIX, and variance swaps. All three notes come from IEOR 221 Module 8 and use the sensitivities in [[2.3.4 Greeks]].

# Contents

- [[2.3.5a Implied Volatility]]: realized, historical, and implied volatility, when a price has exactly one implied volatility, how to solve for it, and why a European call and put share it. **Not taught:** bisection and Newton's method; the size of the shift from a wrong dividend forecast; the call-put equality (homework only).
- [[2.3.5b Volatility Surface]]: the term structure, smile, and skew, the skew since the 1987 crash, the no-arbitrage limits from vertical and calendar spreads, and trading the shape. **Not taught:** the slope and total-variance forms of the limits; matching "sell expensive vol, buy cheap vol" to particular strategies.
- [[2.3.5c Volatility Trading]]: the gamma and vega P&L of a delta-hedged option, the daily breakeven move, path dependence, covered calls, the VIX, and variance swaps. **Not taught:** how the VIX is computed and how its futures settle; the exact realized-variance formula, the vega notional, and the convexity of a variance swap.
