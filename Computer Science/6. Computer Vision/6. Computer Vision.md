# Computer Vision

Computer vision studies how to extract geometric and semantic structure from images. The field's two grand questions — *where is this in 3D?* and *what is this?* — drive the two halves of this section: classical geometry-based methods (sections 1–6) and modern representation-learning methods (sections 7–8).

The geometric pipeline flows roughly:
1. **[[1. Image Formation & Cameras|Image Formation]]** — how scenes become pixels.
2. **[[2. Image Processing|Image Processing]]** — per-pixel operations: filtering, gradients, edges.
3. **[[3. Image Transformations|Image Transformations]]** — 2D geometric warps: affine, projective, homographies.
4. **[[4. Feature-Based Alignment|Feature-Based Alignment]]** — detecting, describing, and matching keypoints across images.
5. **[[5. 3D Image Geometry|3D Image Geometry]]** — the pinhole model, stereo, and epipolar geometry.
6. **[[6. Multi-View Reconstruction|Multi-View Reconstruction]]** — Structure from Motion and Multi-View Stereo.

Modern methods continue:

7. **[[7. Neural Fields|Neural Fields]]** — NeRF and related volumetric representations.
8. **[[8. Image Generation|Image Generation]]** — diffusion and generative models.
