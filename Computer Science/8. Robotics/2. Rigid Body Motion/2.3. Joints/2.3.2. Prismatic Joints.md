>[!note] Prismatic Joint
> A **prismatic joint** is a one-DOF joint that produces pure translation along a fixed axis (no rotation).
> 
> We choose a fixed translation direction v (typically unit) so that the joint displacement $\theta$ **represents distance traveled along that direction**.
> 
> Because pure translation moves every point of the rigid body with the same instantaneous velocity (independent of where the point is), the point velocity is:
>$$\dot p(t)=\dot\theta(t)\,v.$$
>
> Since there is no angular velocity component for a prismatic joint, the twist must have $\omega=0$, so:
>$$\xi=\begin{bmatrix}v\\ 0\end{bmatrix}.$$
>
> Because $\hat{\omega}=0$ in this case, the twist in $\mathfrak{se}(3)$ is:
>$$\hat{\xi}=\begin{bmatrix}0 & v\\ 0&0\end{bmatrix}.$$
>
> Then, $\hat{\xi}$ is the infinitesimal generator of the motion in homogeneous coordinates. The instantaneous and finite kinematics satisfy:
>$$\begin{bmatrix}\dot p(\theta)\\ 0\end{bmatrix}=\hat{\xi}\begin{bmatrix}p(\theta)\\ 1\end{bmatrix},\qquad \begin{bmatrix}p(\theta)\\ 1\end{bmatrix}=e^{\hat{\xi}\theta}\begin{bmatrix}p(0)\\ 1\end{bmatrix}.$$
>
> Exponentiating a pure-translation generator yields identity rotation and a translation of magnitude $\theta$ along $v$. Therefore, the transform is:
>$$e^{\hat{\xi}\theta}= \begin{bmatrix} I & v\theta\\ 0&1 \end{bmatrix}.$$
>
> If $v$ is unit, then $\theta$ has units of length and is exactly the distance traveled; if $v$ is not unit, the actual translation is still $v\theta$.
