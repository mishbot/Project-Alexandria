This section covers 2D geometric transformations of images, which move pixels to new positions without changing their values. It builds the hierarchy from linear maps through affine maps to homographies. It then applies these maps to pixels with inverse warping and interpolation. Per-triangle affine warps plus cross-dissolving extend this to morphing one image into another.

## Prerequisites

- [[4.3a Matrix-Vector Multiplication]] and [[4.3b Matrix-Matrix Multiplication]]: transformations are matrices, and composition is multiplication.
- [[2.8b Change of Basis]]: the columns of a transformation matrix are a new set of axes.
- [[3.4c Hulls]]: the convex hull, which a triangulation covers.
- [[1.1. Digital Images]]: array layout and the downward $y$-axis of images.

## Contents

- [[3.1. Affine Transformations]]: linear maps, homogeneous coordinates, affine maps, coordinate frames, the transformation hierarchy with DoF and correspondences, and triangle-to-triangle maps.
- [[3.2. Image Warping]]: forward versus inverse warping, splatting, the output canvas, and the inverse-warping loop.
- [[3.3. Image Interpolation]]: nearest neighbor and bilinear interpolation, with Gaussian and bicubic as alternatives.
- [[3.4. Homographies]]: projective transformations with $8$ DoF, their estimation from correspondences, and mosaicing.
- [[3.5. Image Morphing]]: cross-dissolve, intermediate shapes, per-triangle warps, mean faces and caricatures.
- [[3.6. Delaunay Triangulation]]: the angle-optimal triangulation, edge flips, the empty circle property and Voronoi duality.

## Recommended Path

For the core, read 3.1, 3.2 and 3.3 in order. For morphing, read [[3.6. Delaunay Triangulation]] before [[3.5. Image Morphing]]. [[3.4. Homographies]] leads on to [[4. Feature-Based Alignment|feature-based alignment]] and panoramas.
