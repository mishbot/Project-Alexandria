>[!note] Linear Program
>A **Linear Program (LP)** is an [[1. Standard Form Optimization|optimization problem]] where the objective function and constraints are all affine. Its **standard form** is given as
>$$\begin{aligned} p^* = \min_\limits{x} \quad & c^T x \\ \text{s.t.:} \quad & Ax \leq b, \\ & x \geq 0\end{aligned}$$
>where $f_{0}(x)=\langle c, x \rangle$ for some decision variable $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and constants $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$, $b \in \mathbb{R}^m$, $c \in \mathbb{R}^n$, and $d \in \mathbb{R}$.
>
>The feasible set of an LP is a *polyhedron* (or a *polytope* if fully bounded). 
# Intuition
Intuitively, in linear programming:
- You have a collection of variables $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$.
- You form a linear expression $c_1 x_1 + c_2 x_2 + \dots + c_n x_n$ (the “objective”) that you want to make as large (maximize) or as small (minimize) as possible.

At the same time, each variable combination must satisfy a series of linear constraints, such as:
- $a_{1,1} x_1 + a_{1,2} x_2 + \dots + a_{1,n} x_n \;\le\; b_1,$
- $a_{2,1} x_1 + a_{2,2} x_2 + \dots + a_{2,n} x_n \;\le\; b_2,$
and possibly some equalities, like
- $a_{3,1} x_1 + a_{3,2} x_2 + \dots + a_{3,n} x_n \;=\; b_3.$

Each constraint chops off a portion of the space of all possible $x$. Because the constraints are linear, the feasible region (the set of all allowable solutions) is a **convex polytope** (think of a polygon in 2D, a polyhedron in 3D, etc.).

>[!example] Slack Variables
>Express the following problems as LPs:
>$$\begin{aligned}\min_\limits{x \in \mathbb{R}^n} \quad & \left[\max\limits_{i=1,\ldots,n} x_{i} - \min\limits_{j=1,\ldots,n}x_{j} \right] \\ \text{s.t.:} \quad & Ax = b\end{aligned}$$
>>[!example]- Solution
>>**Solution:** We can introduce *slack variables* that form a high and low bound on the elements of $x$, such that
>$$\begin{aligned}\min_\limits{x, u, t} \quad & u-t \\ \text{s.t.:} \quad & Ax = b, \\ & u \geq x_{i}, \space i=1,\ldots,n, \\ & t \leq x_{i}, \space i=1,\ldots,n. \end{aligned}$$
>
>$$\begin{aligned}\min_\limits{x \in \mathbb{R}^n} \quad & \sum^{n}_{i=1}|x_{i}| \\ \text{s.t.:} \quad & Ax = b\end{aligned}$$
>>[!example]- Solution
>>We introduce a single bounded slack variable $t$, such that
>>$$\begin{aligned}\min_\limits{x \in \mathbb{R}^n} \quad & \sum^{n}_{i=1}t \\ \text{s.t.:} \quad & Ax = b, \\ \quad & t \geq x_{i}, \space i = 1,\ldots,n \\ \quad & t \geq -x_{i}, \space i = 1, \ldots, n, \end{aligned}$$
>>to cover both positive and negative elements of $x$.
