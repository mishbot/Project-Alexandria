>[!note] Electromagnetism
>Electromagnetism describes the forces between electric charges, at rest and in motion. [[3.1. Electric Charge|Section 3.1]] defines charge and gives Coulomb's law for two point charges. [[3.2. Electric Field|Section 3.2]] replaces that force with a field, $\vec E = \vec F/q$. It also finds the fields of charge spread over lines and surfaces. [[3.3. Gauss's Law|Section 3.3]] relates the flux through a closed surface to the charge inside it. With enough symmetry it finds fields without integrals. It also shows that a conductor in equilibrium has no field inside. [[3.4. Electric Potential|Section 3.4]] describes the same forces with energy per unit charge, the potential $V$. [[3.5. Capacitance|Section 3.5]] uses fields and potentials to find the capacitance $C = Q/V$ and the energy a capacitor stores.
>
>[[3.6. Electric Current|Section 3.6]] describes charge moving through a resistor. [[3.7. DC Circuits|Section 3.7]] analyzes circuits with batteries, resistors, and capacitors. Sections 3.8 to 3.12 are not written yet. [[3.8. Magnetic Force|Section 3.8]] gives the magnetic force on moving charges and currents. [[3.9. Sources of Magnetic Field|Section 3.9]] finds the magnetic fields that currents produce. [[3.10. Electromagnetic Induction|Section 3.10]] shows that a changing magnetic flux induces an emf. [[3.11. Inductance and AC Circuits|Section 3.11]] covers inductance and alternating current. [[3.12. Maxwell's Equations|Section 3.12]] collects the laws and derives electromagnetic waves.
>
>These notes follow PHYS 7B at Berkeley, Fall 2026, and Giancoli chapters 21 to 31. Sections 3.1 to 3.7 cover lectures 11 to 27. Until Gauss's law, the notes write $k = 8.99 \times 10^{9}\ \text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{C}^2$. From Gauss's law on, they write $\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}$, with $\varepsilon_0 = 8.85 \times 10^{-12}\ \text{C}^2/(\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2)$. Each note lists the lectures that covered it in its `lectures` property. A warning titled Not Taught in Lecture flags each part that lecture skipped.

- [[1.2.1. Newton's Laws]], for free-body diagrams and $\vec F_{\text{net}} = m\vec a$.
- [[1.3d.1 Potential Energy]], for conservative forces and potential energy.
- [[3.3. Vector Multiplication]], for the dot product in flux and in work.
- [[3.6. Gradients]], for $\vec E = -\nabla V$.

# Contents

- [[3.1. Electric Charge]]: charge and its quantization, conservation of charge, conductors and insulators, Coulomb's law, superposition, and induced charge.
- [[3.2. Electric Field]]: the field of a point charge, field lines, symmetry, slice and dice, charged particles in a field, and the dipole.
- [[3.3. Gauss's Law]]: flux, Gauss's law with spherical, cylindrical, and planar symmetry, and conductors in equilibrium.
- [[3.4. Electric Potential]]: potential energy, the potential from the field and the field from the potential, point charges, dipoles, continuous charge, and equipotentials.
- [[3.5. Capacitance]]: $C = Q/V$ for plates, spheres, and cylinders, series and parallel networks, stored energy, and dielectrics.
- [[3.6. Electric Current]]: current, current density, resistivity, Ohm's law, and electric power.
- [[3.7. DC Circuits]]: emf, equivalent resistance, terminal voltage, Kirchhoff's junction and loop rules, and the RC circuit.
- [[3.8. Magnetic Force]]: the magnetic field, the force on wires and moving charges, cyclotron motion, torque on a current loop, the Hall effect, and the mass spectrometer. Not written yet.
- [[3.9. Sources of Magnetic Field]]: fields of wires, solenoids, toroids, and loops, from Ampere's law and the Biot-Savart law. Not written yet.
- [[3.10. Electromagnetic Induction]]: magnetic flux, Faraday's and Lenz's laws, motional emf, generators, eddy currents, transformers, and induced electric fields. Not written yet.
- [[3.11. Inductance and AC Circuits]]: mutual and self-inductance, magnetic energy, LR and LC circuits, and alternating current. Not written yet.
- [[3.12. Maxwell's Equations]]: displacement current, the four equations, electromagnetic waves, the Poynting vector, and radiation pressure. Not written yet.

# Recommended Path

Read the sections in order, and the notes within each section in order. The numbering is a reading order. No note uses a result that a later note introduces. Sections 3.1 and 3.2 set up charge, force, and field. Sections 3.3 and 3.4 hold most of the calculations and need the most practice. Section 3.5 uses every earlier section. Sections 3.6 and 3.7 move from charges at rest to charges in motion, and 3.7 builds on the capacitors of 3.5.
