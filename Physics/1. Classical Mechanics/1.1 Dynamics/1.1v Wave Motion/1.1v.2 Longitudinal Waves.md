>[!note] Longitudinal Wave
>A **longitudinal wave** is a wave in which particles of the medium move parallel to the direction of wave propagation (e.g., sound waves, movement of a slinky). A series of compressions and expansions travel along a longitudinal wave. 
>1. **Compressions** are those areas where the coils are momentarily close together. 
>2. **Expansions** (sometimes called rarefactions) are regions where the coils are momentarily far apart. 
>
>Compressions and expansions correspond to the crests and troughs of a transverse wave. As such, a longitudinal wave can be graphically represented by plotting density versus position, resembling a transverse wave and visually clarifying the wave’s behavior.
>
>Moreover, the speed of a longitudinal wave over a solid is given as
>$$v = \sqrt{\frac{E}{\rho}},$$
>whereas its speed over a fluid is given as
>$$v = \sqrt{\frac{B}{\rho}},$$
>where $E$ is the elastic modulus of the material, $B$ is the bulk modulus of the liquid, and $\rho$ is the material's density.
>>[!warning] Note!
>>Since longitudinal waves can be represented as transverse, we can use the equation $v=\lambda f$ to find the wave's wavelength, frequency and period.
