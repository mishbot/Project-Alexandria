>[!note] Inner Product
>The **inner product** is a function that takes two vectors and returns a real scalar quantifying how much the vectors align in direction. 
>
>For a vector space on $\mathbb{R}$, the inner product of vectors $\vec{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n$  and  $\vec{y} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is also called the **dot product**:
>$$\langle \vec{x}, \vec{y} \rangle = \vec{x} \cdot \vec{y} = \vec{x}^T\vec{y} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_iy_i.$$
>Furthermore, the dot product of two vectors in $\mathbb{R}^n$ can be given by the geometric definition,
>$$\vec{x}^T\vec{y} = \|\vec{x}\|\|\vec{y}\|\cos\theta,$$
>where $\theta$ is the angle between the two vectors.
>
>However, for vector spaces on $\mathbb{C}$, the inner product introduces the [[7.3 Complex Conjugate|complex conjugate]], which keeps the output scalar on the real line. Therefore, for two complex vectors  $\vec{x} \in \mathbb{C}^n$  and  $\vec{x} \in \mathbb{C}^n$, the inner product is defined as
>$$\langle \vec{x}, \vec{y} \rangle = \vec{x}^T\vec{y}\textasteriskcentered =\sum_{i=1}^n x_iy_i\textasteriskcentered.$$


# Properties
#### (*Cauchy-Schwartz Inequality*) $|\vec{x}^T\vec{y}| \leq \|\vec{x}\|\|\vec{y}\|$.
This follows from the fact that $\cos\theta \leq 1$.
