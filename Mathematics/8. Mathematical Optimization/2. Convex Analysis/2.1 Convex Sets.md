>[!note] Convex Set
>A subset $C \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ is called **convex** if, for any two points $\vec{x},\vec{y} \in C$ and for every scalar $t \in [0,1]$, the points on the entire line segment joining $\vec{x}$ and $\vec{y}$ is within $C$, such that $t\vec{x} + (1-t)\vec{y} \in C$:

>The dimension of $C$ is equal to the dimension of its [[3.4c Hulls|affine hull]] (i.e., $\operatorname{dim}C=\dim\operatorname{aff}(C)$). 
>>[!warning] Convex Hull
>>One can be quick to notice that $C$ is convex if $\operatorname{co}(\{ \vec{x}, \vec{y} \}) \subset C$ for all $\vec{x}, \vec{y} \in C$.


# Properties
#### (*Separation Theorem*) For a closed convex set $C \subset X$ , and $x_{0} \in X \backslash C$, there exist some $x^* \in X^*$ and $\delta > 0$, such that $\langle x^*, x_{0} \rangle + \delta \leq \langle x^*, c \rangle$ holds for all $c \in C$.
Removing all the abstraction, the separation theorem states that a separating hyperplane $x^*$ exists, represented as a linear functional (i.e., as a vector of function coefficients), that separates $C$ from $x_{0}$. We now prove the separation theorem.

#### (*Support Function Representation*) Let $C$ be a closed convex set, $x \in X$, and $h_{C}(x^*)=\sup\limits_{x \in X}\langle x^*, x \rangle$ be a support function. Then, $C=\bigcap\limits_{x^* \in X^*}\{ x:\langle x^*, x \rangle \leq h_{C}(x^*) \}$.
This outcome of the separation theorem states that any closed and convex set $C$ can be represented as the intersection of all of its supporting half-spaces. Think of it as intersecting the 3 half-spaces of a triangle, in order to form that triangle based on their overlap.

Why is $h_{C}(x^*)$ a support function? We can imagine this closely if $C$ is also compact (i.e., well-defined). Then, the hyperplane $H=\{ x : \langle x^*, x \rangle = h_{C}(x^*) \}$ for all $x^* \in X^*$ is tangent to $C$.
