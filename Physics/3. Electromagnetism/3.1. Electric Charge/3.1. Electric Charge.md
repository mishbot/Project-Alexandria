>[!note] Electric Charge
>This section sets up the basic object of electromagnetism and the force between two of them. [[3.1.1. Electric Charge|Electric charge]] comes in two signs and in whole multiples of $e = 1.602 \times 10^{-19}\ \text{C}$. Objects charge up when electrons move between them, and [[3.1.2. Conservation of Charge|charge is conserved]] in every transfer. Whether electrons can move inside an object depends on the material, which sorts matter into [[3.1.3. Conductors and Insulators|conductors and insulators]].
>
>[[3.1.4. Coulomb's Law|Coulomb's law]] gives the force between two point charges, $F = k|q_1||q_2|/r^2$, with like charges repelling and opposite charges attracting. The [[3.1.5. Superposition Principle|superposition principle]] adds the forces from several charges as vectors, using a free-body diagram and symmetry. [[3.1.6. Induced Charge|Induced charge]] combines the two ideas. A charge near a neutral conductor separates its charges. The nearer induced charge has the opposite sign and is pulled harder, so charged objects attract neutral ones.

# Prerequisites

- [[2.1.1 Atomic Theory of Matter]], for atoms and molecules.
- [[1.2.1. Newton's Laws]], for free-body diagrams and $\vec F_{\text{net}} = m\vec a$.
- [[1.2.2. Law of Universal Gravitation]], the inverse-square law that Coulomb's law mirrors.
- [[3.1. Vector]], for adding forces by components.

# Contents

- [[3.1.1. Electric Charge]]: the two signs, the proton, neutron, and electron, the elementary charge $e$, quantization, net charge, and charging by rubbing. **Not taught:** nothing.
- [[3.1.2. Conservation of Charge]]: the net charge of an isolated system is constant, so charges appear in equal and opposite pairs. **Not taught:** nothing.
- [[3.1.3. Conductors and Insulators]]: free electrons in metals, bound electrons in insulators, and where extra charge goes on each, and charge sharing by contact. **Not taught:** semiconductors, charge sharing between touching spheres.
- [[3.1.4. Coulomb's Law]]: the force between two point charges, $k = 8.99 \times 10^{9}\ \text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{C}^2$, magnitudes on free-body diagrams, and the comparison with gravity. **Not taught:** nothing.
- [[3.1.5. Superposition Principle]]: the net force as a vector sum, the free-body procedure, and symmetry. **Not taught:** points of zero net force.
- [[3.1.6. Induced Charge]]: charge separation in neutral conductors, why neutral objects are attracted, and polarized insulators. **Not taught:** charging by induction with a ground.

# Recommended Path

Read the notes in order. [[3.1.1. Electric Charge]] and [[3.1.3. Conductors and Insulators]] describe what charge is and where it can move. [[3.1.4. Coulomb's Law]] and [[3.1.5. Superposition Principle]] contain most of the problem-solving methods. [[3.1.6. Induced Charge]] comes last because it uses both.
