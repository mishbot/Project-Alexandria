>[!note] Revolute Joint
>A **revolute joint** is a one-DOF joint that produces pure rotation about a fixed axis.
>
>We choose a unit axis direction $\omega$ (following the right-hand rule) and pick any point $q$ on that axis so we can measure the radius from the axis to a moving point $p$.
>
> Because the instantaneous motion of a point under pure rotation is tangent to its circular path (perpendicular to both $\omega$ and the radius vector $r=p-q$), its velocity is:
>$$\dot p(t)=\omega\times\bigl(p(t)-q\bigr).$$
>
>Rotating about an axis that passes through $q$ induces a translational component at the origin equal to “angular velocity $\times$ moment arm,” so the linear part of the twist must satisfy:
>$$v=-\omega\times q=q\times\omega,\qquad \omega^Tv=0.$$
>A twist stacks the linear and angular velocity components:
>$$\xi=\begin{bmatrix}v\\ \omega\end{bmatrix} =\begin{bmatrix}-\omega\times q\\ \omega\end{bmatrix}.$$
> Because $\hat{\omega}$ is the matrix that implements cross products ($\hat{\omega}x=\omega\times x$), the twist in $\mathfrak{se}(3)$ is:
> $$\hat{\xi}=\begin{bmatrix}\hat{\omega} & v\\ 0&0\end{bmatrix} =\begin{bmatrix}\hat{\omega} & -\hat{\omega}q\\ 0&0\end{bmatrix}.$$
> Then, $\hat{\xi}$ is the infinitesimal generator of the motion in homogeneous coordinates. The instantaneous and finite kinematics satisfy:
>$$\begin{bmatrix}\dot p(\theta)\\ 0\end{bmatrix}=\hat{\xi}\begin{bmatrix}p(\theta)\\ 1\end{bmatrix},\qquad \begin{bmatrix}p(\theta)\\ 1\end{bmatrix}=e^{\hat{\xi}\theta}\begin{bmatrix}p(0)\\ 1\end{bmatrix}.$$
> Exponentiating the generator yields a rotation $e^{\hat{\omega}\theta}$ about the axis, and the induced translation for an axis passing through $q$. Therefore, the transform is:
>$$e^{\hat{\xi}\theta}= \begin{bmatrix} e^{\hat{\omega}\theta} & \bigl(I-e^{\hat{\omega}\theta}\bigr)q\\ 0&1 \end{bmatrix}.$$
>
> If the axis passes through the origin, then $q=0$, so no translation is induced and $v=0$.

# Example
>[!example] Finding $se(3)$
>Find $\hat{\xi}$, such that $\dot{\bar{p}} = \hat{\xi}\bar{p}.$
>>[!example] Solution.
>>Start from first principles by defining:
>>$$\hat{p} = \begin{bmatrix} p & 1 \end{bmatrix}^T,$$
>>and use the given relation
>>$$\dot{\hat{p}} = \begin{bmatrix} \hat{\omega} & v \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}\hat{p}.$$
