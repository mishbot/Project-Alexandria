>[!note] Fluid Dynamics
>When a [[1.7.1. Fluids|fluid]] moves, pressure differences cause acceleration rather than simply balancing weight. The study of these motions is called **fluid dynamics**, describing how fluids flow and how energy and forces transfer through motion. Each element of fluid moves along a **streamline**, always tangent to its velocity.
>- **Steady flow:** velocity at each point remains constant in time.
>- **Laminar flow:** smooth, ordered motion in parallel layers with no mixing.
>- **Turbulent flow:** chaotic motion with eddies and energy loss, occurring at high speed or large scale.
>
>Because matter cannot accumulate or vanish within the flow, mass must be conserved. For a small segment of fluid moving through a tube of varying cross-section, the **mass flow rate** of the stream — which denotes the amount of mass passing through a cross-sectional area A per unit time — remains constant:
>$$\frac{dm}{dt}=\frac{d}{dt}\rho Ah = \rho A v = C,$$
>where $h$ is the depth of the fluid. This is the **continuity equation**, expressing that the same amount of fluid passes every cross-section each second. If the fluid is **incompressible** ($\rho$ constant), it simplifies to
>$$A_1 v_1 = A_2 v_2 = Q,$$
>showing that fluid speeds up when its path narrows. The **volume flow rate** $Q = A v$ therefore stays constant throughout the motion.
>
>Since each element of mass carries momentum, the **momentum flux** — the rate at which momentum is transported — is given by
>$$\dot{p} = \dot{m} v = \rho A v^2.$$
>This quantity has units of force, indicating that **forces on surfaces are generated by the rate of change of momentum in the flow**. In other words, if a fluid jet strikes or deflects off a surface, the change in momentum per second produces a measurable **pressure force**, following Newton’s second law:
>$$F = \dot{m} \, \Delta v.$$
>This connects the conservation of mass $(\rho A v = \text{constant})$ with the conservation of momentum, forming the foundation for pressure generation and thrust in moving fluids.

# Examples
>[!example] (Hard) Reflecting Jet Stream
>A jet stream of fluid has a cross-sectional area $A$ and strikes a surface with speed $v$ and angle $\theta$ with respect to the normal direction of the surface. We assume the stream has the same speed and cross-section after the reflection, and the reflection angle is the same as the incident angle. The jet stream has $n$ particles per volume, and the mass of each fluid particle is $m$. Gravitational effect is not considered in this problem. 
>

>>[!example]- Find the pressure experienced by the surface struck by the jet stream. 
>>Since the surface struck by the stream is the projection of the cross-sectional area $A$ onto the ground, we express $A'=\frac{A}{\cos \theta}$. Similarly, the velocity at which the jet stream hits the ground is $v'=v\cos \theta$. Since the stream reverses direction with the same speed at the same angle after hitting the ground, we have that
>>$$\Delta v' = v'_{\text{in}}-v'_{\text{out}}=2v\cos \theta.$$
>>
>>Since $F=\frac{dp}{dt}$, we regard the momentum as $p=m \Delta v$, and therefore,
>>$$F_{\perp}=\frac{dm}{dt}\Delta v'=(\rho A v) (2v\cos \theta)=2\rho A v^2 \cos \theta,$$
>>where $\frac{dm}{dt}=\rho Av$ is the mass flow rate of the stream.
>>
>>As such,
>>$$P=\frac{F_{\perp}}{A'}=\frac{2\rho A v^2 \cos \theta}{\frac{A}{\cos \theta}}=2\rho v^2 \cos^2 \theta.$$
