>[!note] Production and Costs
>This section builds the theory of the competitive firm, the supply side of a market. [[6.7.1 Production Functions]] describes technology as $y = f(L, K)$, with positive and diminishing marginal products. An isoquant collects the bundles that make the same output, and its slope is the marginal rate of technical substitution, $\text{MRTS} = dK/dL = -f'_L/f'_K$. Isoquants play the role of indifference curves, but output is measured in units, so the labels on isoquants matter. [[6.7.2 Returns to Scale]] asks what happens when all inputs grow by a factor $t > 1$. Output grows by less than, exactly, or more than $t$ under decreasing, constant, or increasing returns. For Cobb-Douglas $AK^{\alpha}L^{\beta}$ the answer depends on $\alpha + \beta$, and only decreasing returns give a well-defined competitive supply.
>
>The firm's problem is solved in two steps. In step one, [[6.7.3 Cost Minimization]] finds the cheapest inputs for a given output by minimizing $wL + rK$ subject to $f(L, K) \ge y$. At the optimum the isoquant is tangent to an isocost line, $f'_L/f'_K = w/r$. The solutions are the conditional input demands $L^*(w, r, y)$ and $K^*(w, r, y)$, and the minimal cost is the cost function $c(w, r, y)$. This is the firm's version of [[6.2.5 Expenditure Minimization]]. [[6.7.4 Cost Curves]] reads $c$ as a function of output, with marginal cost $\partial c/\partial y$ and average cost $c/y$. A firm makes a profit exactly when price exceeds average cost, and marginal cost crosses average cost at its minimum.
>
>In step two, [[6.7.5 Profit Maximization and Supply]] chooses $y$ to maximize $py - c(w, r, y)$. The first-order condition $p = c'_y$ says that price equals marginal cost, and the second-order condition $c''_{yy} > 0$ needs rising marginal cost. Supply is the marginal cost curve above average cost, and the implicit function theorem gives $\partial y^*/\partial p = 1/c''_{yy} > 0$. [[6.7.6 One-Step Profit Maximization]] reaches the same choice in one problem, $\max_{L,K}\ pf(L, K) - wL - rK$, with first-order conditions $pf'_L = w$ and $pf'_K = r$ and a Hessian condition for a maximum.

# Prerequisites

- [[6.1.4 Indifference Curves and MRS]], since isoquants and the MRTS mirror indifference curves and the MRS.
- [[6.2.5 Expenditure Minimization]], which has the same structure as cost minimization.
- [[7.2 Lagrange Multipliers]], for the cost minimization problem.
- [[7.1 Unconstrained Optimality Conditions]], for profit maximization and its Hessian condition.
- [[2.5. Implicit Function Theorem]], for the slope of an isoquant and the slope of supply.

# Contents

- [[6.7.1 Production Functions]]: marginal products, isoquants, the MRTS, and Cobb-Douglas technology, with a 3D view of the production surface. **Not taught:** perfect-substitute and Leontief production functions, and why output is cardinal.
- [[6.7.2 Returns to Scale]]: decreasing, constant, and increasing returns, the Cobb-Douglas test $\alpha + \beta$, the link to the shape of costs, and why increasing returns break price taking. **Not taught:** homogeneous technologies in general, the scaling of their costs, and technologies that fit none of the three classes.
- [[6.7.3 Cost Minimization]]: step one of the two-step approach, the tangency $\text{MRTS} = -w/r$, conditional input demands, the Cobb-Douglas cost function, and the parallel with expenditure minimization. **Not taught:** the multiplier as marginal cost, Shephard's lemma $\partial c/\partial w = L^*$, and the sign of $\partial L^*/\partial w$ beyond Cobb-Douglas.
- [[6.7.4 Cost Curves]]: marginal and average cost, the break-even condition $p > \text{AC}$, and the one-input cases $y = L^{\alpha}$ with a non-convex technology and a setup cost. **Not taught:** variable cost, AVC, and AFC as named concepts, the order of the three minima, and short-run cost curves with capital held fixed.
- [[6.7.5 Profit Maximization and Supply]]: step two, price equals marginal cost, the second-order condition, and the upward-sloping supply function. **Not taught:** the supply rule when some costs are sunk.
- [[6.7.6 One-Step Profit Maximization]]: profit as a function of inputs, the first-order conditions $pf'_L = w$ and $pf'_K = r$, the Hessian condition, and the effect of input prices on input demand. **Not taught:** the Cobb-Douglas closed form, the proof that the multiplier equals marginal cost, and the split of the cross effect into substitution and scale effects.

# Recommended Path

Read the notes in order. [[6.7.1 Production Functions]] and [[6.7.2 Returns to Scale]] describe technology only. [[6.7.3 Cost Minimization]] and [[6.7.5 Profit Maximization and Supply]] form the two-step method, with [[6.7.4 Cost Curves]] between them to build the pictures of marginal and average cost. Read [[6.7.6 One-Step Profit Maximization]] last as a check on the two-step method. If short on time, read the callouts of 6.7.3 and 6.7.5 and work the Easy example of 6.7.2, which classifies $y = L^{\alpha}$ and its supply for $\alpha < 1$ and $\alpha > 1$. The Hard example of 6.7.3 shows why a better technology can raise employment once the firm chooses its output.
