>[!note] Stable Matching
>Consider the problem of pairing $n$ jobs and $n$ candidates, each with a complete preference list, so that no pair (candidate, job) would prefer each other over their current partners. Such a pairing is called a **stable matching**, and requires that **no rogue couples** (i.e., job-candidate pairs where both parties prefer other agents).
>
>The process of finding it is often done using the **Gale–Shapley Propose-and-Reject algorithm**, where in each iteration:
>1. Each job proposes to the most preferred candidate who hasn’t rejected it yet.
>2. Each candidate keeps the best offer so far (_on a string_) and rejects all others.
>3. Rejected jobs cross off those candidates and prepare to propose to the next in line.
>
>The propose-and-reject algorithm **always halts** once no rejections occur in an iteration.

# Properties


### If a matching is employer/job optimal, then it is also candidate pessimal.
