>[!note] Workspace and Configuration Space
>The **configuration space** $\mathcal{Q}$ of a robot is the set of all admissible joint vectors $\Theta$, accounting for joint limits and constraints. For a serial manipulator with $n$ independent joints, $\mathcal{Q}\subset \mathbb{R}^n$.
>The **workspace** is the image of the forward kinematic map:
>$$W = \{ g(\Theta) \in SE(3) \mid \Theta \in \mathcal{Q} \}.$$
>It consists of all end-effector poses that the robot can achieve.
>
>Two important subsets are distinguished:
>- **Reachable workspace**: all points in $\mathbb{R}^3$ that the end-effector can physically reach, regardless of orientation.
>- **Dexterous workspace**: the subset of the reachable workspace where every orientation in $SO(3)$ is attainable.
>
>For many manipulators with two main links of lengths $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$, the reachable workspace of the wrist center forms a spherical shell with outer radius $\ell_1+\ell_2$ and inner radius $|\ell_1-\ell_2|$, ignoring joint limits and self-collisions. The dexterous workspace is generally smaller and depends on whether full orientation freedom is available at each point.
>
>The dimension of the workspace is at most six (three translational and three rotational degrees of freedom), even if the robot has more than six joints. When $n>6$, the manipulator is redundant, and multiple configurations correspond to the same end-effector pose.

# Intuition
The configuration space $\mathcal{Q}$ and workspace $W$ are related by the [[2.1. Forward Kinematics|forward kinematic map]] $g: \mathcal{Q} \to SE(3)$. The key insight is that $\mathcal{Q}$ is always $n$-dimensional (one dimension per joint), while $W$ maxes out at 6 dimensions regardless of $n$. When $n > 6$, the forward map is many-to-one, and the preimage of a single pose is a continuous manifold of dimension $n - 6$—these are the **self-motions** exploited by [[5.4. Jacobian Inverse Kinematics#Redundant Manipulators and Null-Space Optimization|redundant manipulators]].

The boundary of the reachable workspace is where the [[5.2. Robot Jacobians|Jacobian]] loses rank: at these configurations, the manipulator is at a [[5.3. Singularities and Manipulability|singularity]] and cannot move outward. This connects workspace geometry to differential kinematics.

# Properties

#### (*Workspace Boundary*) The boundary of the reachable workspace corresponds to singular configurations of the manipulator. A point on the workspace boundary can only be reached with the arm fully extended (or in some other singular pose).

#### (*Redundancy and Self-Motion*) For $n > 6$, the null space of the Jacobian $\mathcal{N}(J)$ has dimension $n - 6$ at generic configurations. Self-motions change the joint configuration without moving the end-effector—the arm "reconfigures" internally while the tool stays fixed.
