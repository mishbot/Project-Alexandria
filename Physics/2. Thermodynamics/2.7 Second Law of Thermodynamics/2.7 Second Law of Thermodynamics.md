>[!note] Second Law of Thermodynamics
>The first law says which processes conserve energy. The second law says which of those processes occur. The section opens with the machine the law is about. A [[2.7.1 Heat Engine|heat engine]] takes $Q_H$ from a hot reservoir, delivers work $W = Q_H - Q_L$, and rejects $Q_L$ to a cold one. Its efficiency is $e = 1 - Q_L/Q_H$. The [[2.7.2 Second Law of Thermodynamics|second law]] then says that heat flows from hot to cold on its own and never the reverse. It also says that no cyclic device turns heat entirely into work. A [[2.7.3 Reversible Process|reversible process]] is one that can be run backward with no trace left.
>
>Two folders apply these ideas to specific machines. [[2.7.4 Heat Engines]] works two cycles in full, the Otto cycle of a car engine and the Carnot cycle. The Carnot efficiency $e_C = 1 - T_L/T_H$ is the ceiling for every engine between two temperatures. [[2.7.5 Refrigerators]] runs an engine backward to make a refrigerator or a heat pump. The last two notes introduce [[2.7.6 Entropy|entropy]], a state function with $dS = dQ/T$ on reversible paths. The [[2.7.7 Entropy Statement of the Second Law|entropy statement]] of the law says that the total entropy of a system and its surroundings never decreases. It contains every other form and is the one used in calculations.

# Contents

- [[2.7.1 Heat Engine]]: thermal cycles, $Q_H$ and $Q_L$ as positive numbers, efficiency, the reservoir diagram, and how to analyze any cycle.
- [[2.7.2 Second Law of Thermodynamics]]: the Clausius and Kelvin-Planck statements, why they are equivalent, and what they forbid. **Not taught:** the two statement names and the equivalence proof.
- [[2.7.3 Reversible Process]]: quasi-static and dissipation-free, and what makes real processes irreversible. **Not taught:** the quasi-static and no-friction conditions, maximal work, and which paths can be drawn.
- [[2.7.4 Heat Engines]]: two engine cycles worked in full.
  - [[2.7.4a Otto Cycle]]: the gasoline engine cycle and $e = 1 - r^{1 - \gamma}$. **Not taught:** the diesel comparison.
  - [[2.7.4b Carnot Engine]]: the cycle, the derivation of $e_C = 1 - T_L/T_H$, and Carnot's theorem. **Not taught:** the proof of Carnot's theorem and why the Carnot engine is slow.
- [[2.7.5 Refrigerators]]: engines run backward and the coefficient of performance.
  - [[2.7.5a Refrigerator]]: the reversed engine, the coefficient of performance, and its ideal value. **Not taught:** how the refrigerant fluid works.
  - [[2.7.5b Heat Pump]]: the same machine used for heating, with $\text{COP}_{\text{hp}} = \text{COP}_{\text{ref}} + 1$. **Not taught:** the fluid, coils, and reversing valve.
- [[2.7.6 Entropy]]: $dS = dQ/T$, the ideal gas formula, entropy on each standard process, and the reversible stand-in for mixing hot and cold water. **Not taught:** the free-expansion example.
- [[2.7.7 Entropy Statement of the Second Law]]: $\Delta S_{\text{total}} \geq 0$, the entropy of the environment, the entropy test for reversibility, lost work, and the Carnot limit. **Not taught:** lost work, the direction of time, and the entropy proof of the Carnot limit.

# Recommended Path

Read the notes in order. [[2.7.1 Heat Engine]] gives the method for any cycle. [[2.7.2 Second Law of Thermodynamics]] and [[2.7.3 Reversible Process]] supply what the Carnot argument needs. The two folders then apply the method. [[2.7.4 Heat Engines]] runs engines forward, and [[2.7.5 Refrigerators]] runs them backward. [[2.7.6 Entropy]] and [[2.7.7 Entropy Statement of the Second Law]] restate everything before them as one inequality.
