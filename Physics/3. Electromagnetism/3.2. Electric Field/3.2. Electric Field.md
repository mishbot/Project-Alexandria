>[!note] Electric Field
>This section replaces the force between charges with the field around them. The [[3.2.1. Electric Field|electric field]] is the force per unit charge on a small test charge. A point charge makes the field $kQ\hat r/r^2$. A charge $q$ in a field feels $\vec F = q\vec E$, and fields from several charges add as vectors. [[3.2.2. Electric Field Lines|Field lines]] draw the field: they run from positive to negative charges and crowd together where the field is strong. [[3.2.3. Symmetry Arguments|Symmetry]] fixes the direction of the field before any calculation, provided both the shape and the charge have the symmetry.
>
>The [[3.2.4. Electric Field of a Continuous Charge Distribution|slice-and-dice method]] finds the field of charge spread over a line, surface, or volume. It cuts the charge into point-like pieces and integrates. Its two standard results are the [[3.2.4a Infinite Line of Charge|infinite line]], $E = 2k\lambda/R$, and the [[3.2.4b Ring of Charge|ring]] on its axis, $E = kQz/(a^2 + z^2)^{3/2}$. A charge in a field accelerates at $q\vec E/m$, and between two fixed charges it can [[3.2.5. Motion of a Charged Particle in an Electric Field|oscillate]]. An [[3.2.6. Electric Dipole|electric dipole]] has a field that falls as $1/r^3$. In a uniform field it feels a torque $\vec p \times \vec E$ but no net force.

# Contents

- [[3.2.1. Electric Field]]: field as force per test charge, the point-charge field, $\vec F = q\vec E$, and why fields fix the problem of cause and effect. **Not taught:** nothing.
- [[3.2.2. Electric Field Lines]]: direction and density of lines, standard pictures, and sketching before calculating. **Not taught:** lines never cross, and line counts proportional to charge.
- [[3.2.3. Symmetry Arguments]]: spherical, cylindrical, and mirror symmetry, and why the charge must share the symmetry. **Not taught:** nothing.
- [[3.2.4. Electric Field of a Continuous Charge Distribution]]: charge densities $\lambda$, $\sigma$, $\rho$ and the slice-and-dice steps. **Not taught:** the field beyond the end of a rod, and the semicircle.
    - [[3.2.4a Infinite Line of Charge]]: $E = 2k\lambda/R$ by the substitution $y = R\tan u$. **Not taught:** the finite rod.
    - [[3.2.4b Ring of Charge]]: the field on the axis of a ring, and a sphere built from rings. **Not taught:** the peak at $z = a/\sqrt 2$, and the last steps of the sphere integral.
- [[3.2.5. Motion of a Charged Particle in an Electric Field]]: $\vec a = q\vec E/m$ and the small oscillations of a charge between two fixed charges. **Not taught:** motion in a uniform field.
- [[3.2.6. Electric Dipole]]: dipole moment, the bisector field, $1/r^3$ falloff, torque, and potential energy. **Not taught:** the on-axis field, the proof of $U = -\vec p\cdot\vec E$, and the direction of the net force in a nonuniform field.

# Recommended Path

Read [[3.2.1. Electric Field]] to [[3.2.3. Symmetry Arguments]] in order, since every later note uses them. The three notes under [[3.2.4. Electric Field of a Continuous Charge Distribution]] are the core of the section and need the most practice. [[3.2.5. Motion of a Charged Particle in an Electric Field]] and [[3.2.6. Electric Dipole]] apply the field to moving and rotating charges.
