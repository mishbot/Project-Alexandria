>[!note] SVM Dual
>The **SVM dual** is the Lagrangian dual of the soft-margin [[4.4. Support Vector Machines|SVM]] problem. It has one multiplier $\alpha_i$ per training point, with labels $y_i \in \{-1, +1\}$. In the notation of [[4.2. Lagrangian Duality]], the margin constraints $1 - \xi_i - y_i(\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}_i + b) \le 0$ get multipliers $\alpha_i \ge 0$. The constraints $-\xi_i \le 0$ get multipliers $r_i \ge 0$. The Lagrangian is
>$$\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w}, b, \boldsymbol\xi, \boldsymbol\alpha, \mathbf{r}) = \tfrac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|^2 + C\sum_{i} \xi_i + \sum_{i} \alpha_i\big(1 - \xi_i - y_i(\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}_i + b)\big) - \sum_{i} r_i \xi_i,$$
>where $C$ is the slack penalty and $\xi_i$ are the slacks of the primal. Minimizing over $\mathbf{w}$, $b$ and $\boldsymbol\xi$ and substituting back gives the **dual problem**:
>$$\max_{\boldsymbol\alpha}\ \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i - \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^n \alpha_i\alpha_j y_i y_j\, \mathbf{x}_i^T\mathbf{x}_j \quad \text{s.t.} \quad 0 \le \alpha_i \le C, \quad \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i y_i = 0.$$
>The data enter only through the inner products $\mathbf{x}_i^T\mathbf{x}_j$. The hard-margin dual drops the upper bound $C$. The dual maximizes a concave quadratic, so it is a convex [[3.5. Quadratic Programming|quadratic program]]. Strong duality holds, so its optimal value equals the primal optimum.
>
>The optimal multipliers give back the primal solution:
>$$\mathbf{w} = \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i y_i \mathbf{x}_i, \qquad b = y_s - \mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}_s,$$
>where $s$ is any point with $0 < \alpha_s < C$. Solvers average $b$ over all such points. Prediction needs only the points with $\alpha_i > 0$:
>$$f(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{i:\,\alpha_i > 0} \alpha_i y_i\, \mathbf{x}_i^T\mathbf{x} + b.$$
>
>The KKT conditions tie each multiplier to the position of its point relative to the margin:
>
>- **$\alpha_i = 0$**: $y_i f(\mathbf{x}_i) \ge 1$. The point is on or outside the margin, and it has no effect on the solution.
>- **$0 < \alpha_i < C$**: $y_i f(\mathbf{x}_i) = 1$ and $\xi_i = 0$. The point is a **free support vector** on the margin.
>- **$\alpha_i = C$**: $y_i f(\mathbf{x}_i) \le 1$ and $\xi_i = 1 - y_i f(\mathbf{x}_i)$. The point is a **bounded support vector**, on the margin, inside it, or misclassified.
>
>The **support vectors** are the points with $\alpha_i > 0$. Conversely, $y_i f(\mathbf{x}_i) > 1$ forces $\alpha_i = 0$, and $y_i f(\mathbf{x}_i) < 1$ forces $\alpha_i = C$. At the optimum $\sum_i \alpha_i = \|\mathbf{w}\|^2 + C\sum_i \xi_i$. For the hard margin this gives the margin width $2/\sqrt{\sum_i \alpha_i}$.

# Intuition

Every constrained optimization problem has a dual problem whose variables are its Lagrange multipliers. The primal searches over the weights $\mathbf{w}$, one per feature. The dual searches over the multipliers $\alpha_i$, one per training point. Each $\alpha_i$ is the weight that point $i$ receives in $\mathbf{w} = \sum_i \alpha_i y_i \mathbf{x}_i$. The weight vector is therefore a weighted sum of training points, and points beyond the margin get weight zero. The cap $\alpha_i \le C$ limits the weight of any one point.

The dual has two uses. It shows that the solution depends only on the support vectors. It also uses the data only through inner products $\mathbf{x}_i^T\mathbf{x}_j$. Replacing each inner product by a kernel gives the [[4.4b. Kernel SVM|kernel SVM]], with the same $n$ variables.

# Properties

#### *(Primal or Dual)* The dual has one variable per sample and the primal one per feature, so each suits a different data shape.

The dual works with the $n \times n$ Gram matrix $\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^T$. Forming it costs $O(n^2 d)$ time and storing it costs $O(n^2)$ memory. With many features and few samples, such as $10{,}000$ gene expressions for $200$ patients, the dual is the smaller problem. With many samples and few features, linear solvers work on the primal. They can also update the dual one coordinate at a time without storing the Gram matrix. A kernel SVM with the RBF kernel has an infinite-dimensional $\mathbf{w}$, so solvers work with the dual.

#### *(Box Constraint Caps Influence)* The bound $\alpha_i \le C$ limits how much any single point can change $\mathbf{w}$.

In the hard-margin dual the multipliers have no upper bound. A point close to the other class can receive a large $\alpha_i$ and dominate $\mathbf{w} = \sum_i \alpha_i y_i \mathbf{x}_i$. The soft margin caps every multiplier at $C$, so a gross outlier contributes at most $C\,y_i\mathbf{x}_i$ to $\mathbf{w}$. Every point inside the margin or misclassified has $\alpha_i = C$, however large its slack. This is the dual form of the fact that the hinge loss has slope at most $1$ in magnitude. On separable data the soft and hard solutions coincide once $C \ge \max_i \alpha_i^{\text{hard}}$. The hard-margin multipliers are then feasible for the soft dual and remain optimal.

#### *(Leave-One-Out Bound)* The leave-one-out error of an SVM is at most the fraction of training points that are support vectors.

Remove a point with $\alpha_i = 0$ and retrain. The old solution still satisfies every KKT condition of the smaller problem, so it stays optimal. The removed point has $y_i f(\mathbf{x}_i) \ge 1$, so the unchanged classifier labels it correctly. Leave-one-out errors can therefore occur only at support vectors (Vapnik 1995):
$$\text{LOO error} \le \frac{\#\{i : \alpha_i > 0\}}{n}.$$
A model with $80$ support vectors among $1{,}000$ points has a leave-one-out error of at most $8\%$. Few support vectors thus guarantee a low [[2.3.2. Cross-Validation|leave-one-out]] error without retraining. The bound is loose on noisy data, where many points sit inside the margin.

#### *(Non-Unique Bias)* When no multiplier lies strictly between $0$ and $C$, the KKT conditions confine $b$ only to an interval.

Without a free support vector, the formula $b = y_s - \mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}_s$ has no point to use. Each remaining KKT condition gives a one-sided bound on $b$. Points with $\alpha_i = 0$ need $y_i f(\mathbf{x}_i) \ge 1$, and points with $\alpha_i = C$ need $y_i f(\mathbf{x}_i) \le 1$. Every $b$ in the resulting interval is optimal, and LIBSVM returns its midpoint (Chang and Lin 2011). Take healthy readings at $-2$ and $0$, faulty readings at $1$ and $2$, and $C = 1$. The solution has $w = 1$ and $\boldsymbol\alpha = (0, 1, 1, 0)$. Every $b \in [-1, 0]$ gives the same objective $1.5$, and LIBSVM reports $b = -0.5$. The weight vector stays unique because $\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|^2$ is strictly convex.

#### *(Sequential Minimal Optimization)* Solvers update two multipliers at a time, because the equality constraint forbids changing one alone.

Changing a single $\alpha_i$ would violate $\sum_i \alpha_i y_i = 0$. Sequential minimal optimization (SMO, Platt 1998) picks two multipliers $\alpha_i$ and $\alpha_j$. It moves them along the line $\alpha_i y_i + \alpha_j y_j = \text{const}$. On that line the dual is a quadratic in one variable. SMO maximizes it in closed form and clips the result to the box $[0, C]$. Each step needs only two columns of the Gram matrix, so the full matrix is never stored. LIBSVM picks a pair that badly violates the KKT conditions, using second-order information (Fan, Chen and Lin 2005). It stops when every violation is below a tolerance. SMO is a form of [[6.3. Coordinate Descent|coordinate descent]] on blocks of two coordinates.

# Derivation

Start from the soft-margin primal, a convex quadratic program with affine constraints. In the form of [[4.2. Lagrangian Duality]], the objective is $f_0 = \frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|^2 + C\sum_i\xi_i$. The $2n$ inequality constraints are $1 - \xi_i - y_i(\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}_i + b) \le 0$ and $-\xi_i \le 0$. Their multipliers are $\alpha_i \ge 0$ and $r_i \ge 0$, where the duality note writes $\lambda_i$.

The dual function $g(\boldsymbol\alpha, \mathbf{r})$ minimizes $\mathcal{L}$ over $\mathbf{w}$, $b$ and $\boldsymbol\xi$. The Lagrangian is a convex quadratic in $\mathbf{w}$ and linear in $b$ and $\boldsymbol\xi$. Setting its partial derivatives to zero gives the stationarity conditions:
$$\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\mathbf{w}} = \mathbf{w} - \sum_i \alpha_i y_i\mathbf{x}_i = \mathbf{0}, \qquad \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial b} = -\sum_i \alpha_i y_i = 0, \qquad \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \xi_i} = C - \alpha_i - r_i = 0.$$
If the second or third condition fails, $\mathcal{L}$ is linear with nonzero slope in $b$ or $\xi_i$, and $g = -\infty$. Such multipliers give no useful lower bound, so the dual keeps only multipliers that satisfy both conditions. Since $r_i = C - \alpha_i$, the requirement $r_i \ge 0$ becomes $\alpha_i \le C$. With these conditions the terms in $b$ and $\boldsymbol\xi$ cancel. Substituting $\mathbf{w} = \sum_i \alpha_i y_i \mathbf{x}_i$ then gives
$$g(\boldsymbol\alpha) = \tfrac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|^2 + \sum_i\alpha_i - \sum_i \alpha_i y_i\,\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}_i = \sum_i \alpha_i - \frac{1}{2}\sum_i\sum_j \alpha_i\alpha_j y_i y_j\,\mathbf{x}_i^T\mathbf{x}_j,$$
because $\sum_i \alpha_i y_i\,\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}_i = \mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{w}$. Maximizing $g$ over the feasible multipliers is the dual problem. Let $\mathbf{Q}$ have entries $Q_{ij} = y_iy_j\,\mathbf{x}_i^T\mathbf{x}_j$. Then $\mathbf{Q} = \mathbf{D}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^T\mathbf{D}$ with $\mathbf{D} = \operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{y})$, so $\mathbf{Q}$ is [[4.2e Positive Semidefinite Matrix|positive semidefinite]]. The dual is the convex QP of minimizing $\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol\alpha^T\mathbf{Q}\boldsymbol\alpha - \mathbf{1}^T\boldsymbol\alpha$ over the box $[0, C]^n$ and the hyperplane $\mathbf{y}^T\boldsymbol\alpha = 0$.

Strong duality follows from Slater's condition, which asks for a strictly feasible primal point. For the soft margin, any $(\mathbf{w}, b)$ with large enough slacks makes every constraint strict. For the hard margin on separable data, doubling a feasible $(\mathbf{w}, b)$ gives $y_i f(\mathbf{x}_i) \ge 2 > 1$. The KKT conditions then characterize the optimum. Complementary slackness reads
$$\alpha_i\big(y_i f(\mathbf{x}_i) - 1 + \xi_i\big) = 0, \qquad (C - \alpha_i)\,\xi_i = 0.$$
If $\alpha_i = 0$, the second condition gives $\xi_i = 0$, and primal feasibility gives $y_i f(\mathbf{x}_i) \ge 1$. If $0 < \alpha_i < C$, both conditions force their other factor to zero. Then $\xi_i = 0$ and $y_i f(\mathbf{x}_i) = 1$. If $\alpha_i = C$, the first bracket vanishes and $\xi_i \ge 0$, so $y_i f(\mathbf{x}_i) = 1 - \xi_i \le 1$. A free support vector has $y_s(\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}_s + b) = 1$. Multiplying by $y_s$ and using $y_s^2 = 1$ gives $b = y_s - \mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}_s$.

# Examples

>[!example] (Easy) Two Emails in Closed Form
>A spam filter has one spam email at $\mathbf{x}_1 = (3, 1)$ with $y_1 = +1$ and one legitimate email at $\mathbf{x}_2 = (1, 2)$ with $y_2 = -1$.
>
>>[!example]- Solve the hard-margin dual and recover $\mathbf{w}$, $b$ and the margin width.
>>The constraint $\alpha_1 y_1 + \alpha_2 y_2 = 0$ forces $\alpha_1 = \alpha_2 = a$. Then $\mathbf{w} = a(\mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_2) = a(2, -1)$. The dual objective is $2a - \frac{1}{2}a^2\|\mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_2\|^2 = 2a - 2.5a^2$. Setting its derivative $2 - 5a$ to zero gives $a = 0.4$. Hence $\mathbf{w} = (0.8, -0.4)$ and $b = y_1 - \mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}_1 = 1 - 2 = -1$. The dual value is $0.8 - 0.4 = 0.4$, equal to the primal value $\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|^2 = 0.4$. The margin width is $2/\|\mathbf{w}\| = \sqrt{5} \approx 2.236$, the distance between the two emails. With two points the boundary is their perpendicular bisector, and in general $a = 2/\|\mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_2\|^2$.

>[!example] (Medium) Multipliers for Four Sensor Readings
>Faulty machines ($y = +1$) read $\mathbf{x}_1 = (2, 0)$ and $\mathbf{x}_2 = (0, 2)$. Healthy machines ($y = -1$) read $\mathbf{x}_3 = (0, 0)$ and $\mathbf{x}_4 = (-1, 0)$. The hard-margin solution from [[4.4. Support Vector Machines]] is $\mathbf{w} = (1, 1)$ and $b = -1$.
>
>>[!example]- Find the multipliers from the KKT conditions and check that the duality gap is zero.
>>Point 4 has $y_4 f(\mathbf{x}_4) = 2 > 1$, so complementary slackness forces $\alpha_4 = 0$. Stationarity in $\mathbf{w}$ gives $\mathbf{w} = \alpha_1(2, 0) + \alpha_2(0, 2) - \alpha_3(0, 0) = (2\alpha_1, 2\alpha_2)$. Matching $\mathbf{w} = (1, 1)$ gives $\alpha_1 = \alpha_2 = \frac{1}{2}$. Stationarity in $b$ gives $\alpha_1 + \alpha_2 - \alpha_3 - \alpha_4 = 0$, so $\alpha_3 = 1$. All multipliers are nonnegative, so dual feasibility holds. The dual value is $\sum_i\alpha_i - \frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|^2 = 2 - 1 = 1$, equal to the primal value $\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|^2 = 1$. The margin width is $2/\sqrt{\sum_i \alpha_i} = 2/\sqrt{2} \approx 1.414$. The healthy machine at the origin is the only healthy support vector. Its multiplier must therefore equal the sum of the two faulty multipliers.

>[!example] (Hard) Overlapping Spam Counts
>A filter uses one feature, the number of links in an email. Legitimate emails ($y = -1$) have $0$ and $3$ links. Spam emails ($y = +1$) have $2$ and $4$ links. The soft-margin SVM uses $C = 1$.
>
>>[!example]- Guess the active set, solve for $w$, $b$ and $\boldsymbol\alpha$, and verify the KKT conditions.
>>No threshold separates the classes, since a legitimate email at $3$ lies above a spam email at $2$. Guess that these two are bounded support vectors with $\alpha = C = 1$. Guess also that the outer emails at $0$ and $4$ are free support vectors on the margin. The margin equations $f(0) = -1$ and $f(4) = 1$ give $b = -1$ and $w = 0.5$. Order the emails as $0, 3, 2, 4$ with multipliers $(\alpha_1, 1, 1, \alpha_4)$. Stationarity in $b$ gives $-\alpha_1 - 1 + 1 + \alpha_4 = 0$, so $\alpha_1 = \alpha_4$. Stationarity in $w$ gives $w = -\alpha_1 \cdot 0 - 3 + 2 + 4\alpha_4 = 0.5$, so $\alpha_4 = 0.375$. Then $\boldsymbol\alpha = (0.375, 1, 1, 0.375)$.
>>Now check each condition. The free multipliers lie strictly inside $(0, 1)$, and their points satisfy $y_i f(\mathbf{x}_i) = 1$. The email at $3$ has $f(3) = 0.5$, so $y f = -0.5 \le 1$. It is misclassified, with slack $1.5$. The email at $2$ has $f(2) = 0$, so $y f = 0 \le 1$. It lies on the boundary, with slack $1$. The primal value is $\frac{1}{2}(0.25) + 1 \cdot (1.5 + 1) = 2.625$. The dual value is $2.75 - 0.125 = 2.625$, so the gap is zero. Both free points give the same $b = -1$. For general $C$ the same steps give $\alpha_4 = (C + 0.5)/4$, which stays below $C$ for every $C > 1/6$.
