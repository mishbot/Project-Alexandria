>[!note] DC Circuits
>This section solves circuits built from sources, resistors, and capacitors. A source moves charge against the electric field, and its [[3.7.1. Electromotive Force|electromotive force]] $\mathcal{E}$, or emf, is the work it does per unit charge. In a **DC circuit**, short for direct current, every source has a constant emf. The currents are steady, except while a capacitor charges or discharges, as in an [[3.7.6. RC Circuit|RC circuit]]. Resistors combine into an [[3.7.2. Equivalent Resistance|equivalent resistance]]. Resistances add in series and reciprocals add in parallel, the reverse of the capacitor rules. A real source has an internal resistance $r$. Its [[3.7.3. Terminal Voltage|terminal voltage]] $\mathcal{E} - Ir$ falls as the current grows, and its current can never exceed $\mathcal{E}/r$.
>
>Two rules solve every circuit in this section. The [[3.7.4. Kirchhoff's Junction Rule|junction rule]] says that the currents at a junction add to zero, by conservation of charge. The [[3.7.5. Kirchhoff's Loop Rule|loop rule]] says that the potential changes around a loop add to zero, by conservation of energy. Labeling a current in every segment and writing both rules gives one equation per unknown current. An RC circuit charges a capacitor through a resistor from a DC source. Its charge approaches $C\mathcal{E}$ on the timescale $\tau = RC$, and its current decays from $\mathcal{E}/R$ toward zero.

# Contents

- [[3.7.1. Electromotive Force]]: the emf as work per unit charge, the symbol $\mathcal{E}$, the source power $\mathcal{E}I$, why a closed circuit needs a source, and the energy count around a loop. **Not taught:** the non-electric work inside a source and the field's work there, battery chemistry, and sources that absorb energy.
- [[3.7.2. Equivalent Resistance]]: series and parallel rules from charge conservation and added potential differences, the comparison with capacitors, short and open circuits, sanity checks, and zip and unzip. **Not taught:** nothing.
- [[3.7.3. Terminal Voltage]]: current-limited sources, the internal-resistance model, the short-circuit current $\mathcal{E}/r$, and finding $\mathcal{E}$ and $r$ from two loads. **Not taught:** the formula $V_{ab} = \mathcal{E} - Ir$, and how $r$ changes with age and temperature.
- [[3.7.4. Kirchhoff's Junction Rule]]: the rule from conservation of charge, its sign convention, free current directions, one current per segment, the redundant junction, and capacitor plates. **Not taught:** nothing.
- [[3.7.5. Kirchhoff's Loop Rule]]: the rule from conservation of energy, the signs for sources, resistors, and capacitors, the full procedure for solving a circuit, elimination, and checks on the answer. **Not taught:** the EE methods (node voltages, mesh currents, Thevenin, Norton), and the failure of the rule when a magnetic flux changes.
- [[3.7.6. RC Circuit]]: the charging equation and its solution, $Q(t)$ and $I(t)$, the time constant $\tau = RC$, the capacitor as a wire and as a gap, and the unphysical current jump. **Not taught:** discharging, and the energy budget of charging.

# Recommended Path

Read [[3.7.1. Electromotive Force]] first, since every later note uses $\mathcal{E}$. [[3.7.2. Equivalent Resistance]] reuses the method of [[3.5.2. Equivalent Capacitance]] and reads quickly. [[3.7.3. Terminal Voltage]] is short. Read [[3.7.4. Kirchhoff's Junction Rule]] and [[3.7.5. Kirchhoff's Loop Rule]] together. The loop rule note holds the procedure that solves every circuit in this section, and it needs the most practice. [[3.7.6. RC Circuit]] applies the loop rule to a capacitor and comes last.
