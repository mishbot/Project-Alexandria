>[!note] QR Decomposition
>The QR decomposition of a general matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ is its factorization into the product of a matrix with [[2.9c Matrix Orthonormality|orthonormal columns]] $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$, and an upper triangular matrix $R \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$, for which
>$$A = QR,$$
>
>where $Q=\begin{bmatrix}\vec{q}_1 & \vec{q}_2 & \dots & \vec{q}_\mathscr{l}\end{bmatrix}$, and $R=\begin{bmatrix}\left<\vec{a}_1, \vec{q}_1\right> & \left<\vec{a}_2, \vec{q}_1\right> & \dots & \left<\vec{a}_\mathscr{l}, \vec{q}_1\right> \\ 0 & \left<\vec{a}_2, \vec{q}_2\right> & \dots & \left<\vec{a}_\mathscr{l}, \vec{q}_2\right> \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \ldots & \left<\vec{a}_\mathscr{l}, \vec{q}_\mathscr{l}\right>\end{bmatrix}.$

# Intuition
We can interpret the QR decomposition of $A$ as a change of basis, where an upper-triangular matrix can be transformed into any matrix using an orthonormal basis. More particularly, 
- $A$ is originally expressed in some arbitrary coordinate system (its column vectors may not be orthogonal).
- In QR decomposition, we **convert the basis** of $A$ into an **orthonormal basis** to make computations easier:
	- $Q$ contains the **orthonormal basis**, computed using Gram-Schmidt.
	- $R$ describes how the original coordinates of $A$ are represented in this new basis.

For example, we can perform a real change of basis on $A$ to find $R$, such that $Q^{T}A=R$
