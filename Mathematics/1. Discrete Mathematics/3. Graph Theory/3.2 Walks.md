>[!note] Types of Graph Walks
>A **walk** of length $k-1$ in an undirected graph is a sequence of edges
>$$S = \{v_1, v_2\}, \{v_2, v_3\}, \dots, \{v_{k-1}, v_k\},$$
>where each $v_i$ is a (not necessarily distinct) vertex in the graph, and each pair $\{v_i, v_{i+1}\}$ represents an edge between consecutive vertices.
>
>Several important concepts in graph theory are special types of walks defined by additional constraints:
>- A **closed walk** is a walk that **starts and ends at the same vertex** (i.e.,$v_1 = v_k$).
>- A **trail** is a walk in which **no edge is repeated**, although vertices may be revisited.
>	- A **path** is a trail in which **no vertex is repeated**, meaning all vertices and edges are distinct.
>		- A **cycle** is a **closed path**, where the walk starts and ends at the same vertex, and **no other vertex or edge is repeated**.
>	- A **circuit** (or **tour**) is a **closed trail**, meaning it starts and ends at the same vertex **without repeating any edges**.
>
>These definitions form a hierarchy: every **path** is a **trail**, every **trail** is a **walk**, and every **cycle** is both a **path** and a **circuit**:
>
> Furthermore, an **Eulerian walk** (or trail, circuit, etc.) is a walk that traverses **every edge** of the graph **exactly once**, whereas a **Hamiltonian walk** (or path, cycle, etc.) is a walk that visits **every vertex** of the graph **exactly once**. These are both more strictly constrained cases of the more general concepts defined above.

#### (*Eulerian Walk*) An undirected graph has an Eulerian walk if and only if the graph is connected, and there are exactly 0 or 2 odd-degree vertices.
