>[!note] Compound Annual Growth Rate
>**Compound Annual Growth Rate** (also called compound return) is the cumulative growth of an investment over multiple periods, where the net simple return $R_{t}$ from each period $t$ is reinvested and earns returns in subsequent periods. Formally, the CAGR between time $t_0$ and $t$, given $n$-period returns, is given as
>$$1+R_{t_{0}, t}=\left[\prod_{i=1}^{t}(1+R_{t_{0}+i})\right]^{\frac{1}{nt}}.$$
>
>The compounded return over $T$ years, paid $n$ times per year and given an APR $r$ can generally be calculated as
>$$1+R_{T} = \left(1+\frac{r}{n}\right)^{nT}.$$
>
>Letting $n \to \infty$ yields continuous returns, such that
>$$1+R_{T} = \lim_{ n \to \infty }\left[\left(1+\frac{r}{n}\right)^{nt}\right]=e^{rT},$$
>which yields the **log return** $\ln(1+R_{T})=rT$.
>
>>[!warning] Compound Return Excludes the Initial Period $t$
>>Period $t$ isn’t included in the compound return formula because it’s the the subsequent returns that measure the growth or changes in value over the previous period, not the initial period itself.
