>[!note] Cross-Correlation Function (CCF)
>The **cross-correlation function** between two jointly stationary [[2.1. Time Series|time series]] $\{X_t\}$ and $\{Y_t\}$ is the normalized [[2.2.4 Cross-Covariance Function|cross-covariance]], measuring the linear dependence between $X_{t+h}$ and $Y_t$ on a unitless scale:
>$$\rho_{XY}(h) = \frac{\gamma_{XY}(h)}{\sqrt{\gamma_X(0)\,\gamma_Y(0)}} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X_{t+h}, Y_t)}{\sqrt{\text{Var}(X_t)\,\text{Var}(Y_t)}},$$
>where $\gamma_{XY}(h)$ is the [[2.2.4 Cross-Covariance Function|cross-covariance]] at lag $h$, and $\gamma_X(0)$, $\gamma_Y(0)$ are the [[2.2.2 Autocovariance Function|variances]] of each series. This extends the [[2.2.3 Autocorrelation Function|autocorrelation function]] to a pair of series, and is the time series analogue of the [[2.5b Second-Order Moments|correlation coefficient]] $\rho_{X,Y}$.
>
>Like cross-covariance, the CCF is **not symmetric**: $\rho_{XY}(h) \neq \rho_{XY}(-h)$ in general. The relationship $\rho_{XY}(h) = \rho_{YX}(-h)$ holds, so reading the CCF from one direction gives the lead-lag structure from $X$'s perspective, and reversing it gives $Y$'s.
>
>>[!warning] Spurious Cross-Correlation
>>If both $\{X_t\}$ and $\{Y_t\}$ share a common trend or seasonal component, the CCF can show large values at many lags even when the series have no genuine dynamic relationship. It is standard practice to **pre-whiten** both series (remove autocorrelation and trend) before interpreting the CCF causally.

# Intuition
The CCF answers: **on a scale from $-1$ to $1$, how strongly is the value of series $X$ at time $t + h$ linearly associated with the value of series $Y$ at time $t$?** It is the primary tool for detecting **lead-lag relationships** between two series.

A peak in $\rho_{XY}(h)$ at $h > 0$ means that $X$ leads $Y$ by $h$ periods (future $X$ covaries with current $Y$, equivalently current $X$ covaries with future $Y$). A peak at $h < 0$ means $Y$ leads $X$. A peak at $h = 0$ indicates contemporaneous association. In practice, the **sample CCF** is plotted and compared against confidence bands at $\pm 1.96/\sqrt{T}$ to identify statistically significant lead-lag structure.

# Properties
#### (*Boundedness*) $-1 \leq \rho_{XY}(h) \leq 1$ for all $h$, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.
#### (*Asymmetry*) $\rho_{XY}(h) = \rho_{YX}(-h)$, but $\rho_{XY}(h) \neq \rho_{XY}(-h)$ in general.
#### (*Autocorrelation as Special Case*) When $Y_t = X_t$, the CCF reduces to the [[2.2.3 Autocorrelation Function|ACF]]: $\rho_{XX}(h) = \rho_X(h)$.
#### (*Normalization*) $\rho_{XY}(h) = \gamma_{XY}(h) / \sigma_X\sigma_Y$, where $\sigma_X = \sqrt{\gamma_X(0)}$ and $\sigma_Y = \sqrt{\gamma_Y(0)}$.
#### (*Independence*) If $\{X_t\}$ and $\{Y_t\}$ are independent, then $\rho_{XY}(h) = 0$ for all $h$.
#### (*Invariance to Location-Scale*) If $\tilde X_t = aX_t + b$ and $\tilde Y_t = cY_t + d$ with $a, c > 0$, then $\rho_{\tilde X \tilde Y}(h) = \rho_{XY}(h)$. The CCF is unaffected by shifts and positive rescalings.

# Examples
>[!example] Delayed Response
>An economist models monthly advertising spend $\{X_t\}$ and monthly revenue $\{Y_t\}$ for a company. Both series are stationary after differencing. The sample CCF shows a significant peak at $h = 2$.
>>[!example]- Interpret the peak.
>>A peak at $h = 2$ in $\rho_{XY}(h)$ means that $X_{t+2}$ is correlated with $Y_t$, or equivalently, $X_t$ is correlated with $Y_{t+2}$. This indicates that advertising spend **leads** revenue by roughly $2$ months: an increase in ad spending today is associated with a revenue increase two months later. The magnitude of $\rho_{XY}(2)$ quantifies the strength of this association on a $[-1, 1]$ scale.

>[!example] CCF of Lagged Signal
>Let $Y_t = \beta X_{t-d} + W_t$, where $\{X_t\}$ is stationary white noise with variance $\sigma_X^2$, $\{W_t\} \sim \text{WN}(0, \sigma_W^2)$ is independent of $\{X_t\}$, and $d > 0$.
>>[!example]- Derive the CCF.
>>From the [[2.2.4 Cross-Covariance Function|cross-covariance]]:
>>$$\gamma_{XY}(h) = \text{Cov}(X_{t+h}, \beta X_{t-d} + W_t) = \beta\,\text{Cov}(X_{t+h}, X_{t-d}) = \begin{cases} \beta\sigma_X^2 & h = -d, \\ 0 & h \neq -d, \end{cases}$$
>>since $\{X_t\}$ is white noise and $\{W_t\}$ is independent. The variances are $\gamma_X(0) = \sigma_X^2$ and $\gamma_Y(0) = \beta^2\sigma_X^2 + \sigma_W^2$. So
>>$$\rho_{XY}(h) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{\beta\sigma_X^2}{\sigma_X\sqrt{\beta^2\sigma_X^2 + \sigma_W^2}} = \dfrac{\beta\sigma_X}{\sqrt{\beta^2\sigma_X^2 + \sigma_W^2}} & h = -d, \\ 0 & h \neq -d. \end{cases}$$
>>The CCF is a single spike at $h = -d$, correctly identifying that $Y$ lags $X$ by $d$ periods. The height of the spike depends on the signal-to-noise ratio $\beta^2\sigma_X^2/\sigma_W^2$.
