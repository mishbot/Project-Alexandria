>[!note] Perpetual and Binary Options
>Perpetual and binary options are two of the simplest [[2.3.6 Exotic Options|exotic options]]. Both are priced in the Black-Scholes-Merton setting with a constant rate $r$, dividend yield $q$ and volatility $\sigma$.
>- [[2.3.6.1a Perpetual American Options]] covers calls and puts that never expire. Their value solves the BSM equation without its time term, and smooth pasting sets the exercise level $S^*$. The note also covers perpetual warrants and perpetual convertible preferred stock.
>- [[2.3.6.1b Binary Options]] covers contracts that pay a fixed amount or nothing. A cash call paying $H$ is worth $He^{-r\tau}N(d_2)$ and is the limit of a narrow call spread. The note also covers one-touch and no-touch options, range options, and the effect of the skew.
>
>Both notes come from IEOR 221 Module 9 and its discussion section.

# Contents

- [[2.3.6.1a Perpetual American Options]]: the time-free BSM equation, the choice of root, value matching and smooth pasting, the exercise levels of the call and the put, delta and vega, perpetual warrants, and perpetual convertible preferred stock. **Not taught:** the perpetual put's closed form and delta and the vega of perpetual options (homework only); Hull's form of the values.
- [[2.3.6.1b Binary Options]]: cash and asset calls and puts, the split of a call into binaries, replication by call spreads, one-touch and no-touch options, range options and range accruals, the vega sign and the skew correction, and hedging near the trigger. **Not taught:** the closed forms for a binary's vega and gamma.

The structured products in [[2.3.6.3 Basket Options and Structured Products]] use the binaries of 2.3.6.1b. Readers short on time should read the perpetual put derivation in 2.3.6.1a and the callout of 2.3.6.1b.
