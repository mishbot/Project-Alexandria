>[!note] Probability Mass Function (PMF)
>The probability [[2.2.1. Probability Distribution|distribution]] of a discrete random variable $X$ is fully described by a non-negative **probability mass function (PMF)** $p_{X}: \mathcal{X} \to [0,1]$, formally defined as
>$$p_{X}(x)=\mathbb{P}[X=x],$$
>where $\set{ X=x } \in \mathcal{F}$ is the event consisting of all outcomes where the random variable $X$ equals the specific value $x \in \mathcal{X}$.
>
>The cumulative probability that $X$ takes on a value less than or equal to $x$ in the PMF is given by the monotonically non-decreasing **cumulative distribution function (CDF)** $F_X: \mathbb{R} \to [0,1]$, formally defined as
>$$F_{X}(x)=\mathbb{P}[X \leq x]=\sum^{}_{k \leq x}p_{X}(k),$$
>implying that the PDF is the rate of change of the CDF, $p_{X}(x)=F_{X}(x)-F_{X}(x-1)$.
>>[!warning] PMFs Sum to One
>>For a PMF to meet the sum-to-one criterion of any probability function,
>>$$\sum^{}_{x \in \mathcal{X}}p_{X}(x)=1.$$

# Properties
#### (*Joint Distribution*) $p_{X,Y}(x,y)=\mathbb{P}[X=x \space \cap \space Y=y]$.
#### (*Conditionality*) $p_{X \mid Y}(x \mid y)=\cfrac{p_{X,Y}(x,y)}{p_{Y}(y)}$.
#### (*Marginality*) $p_{X}(x)=\sum\limits_{y \in \mathcal{Y}}p_{X,Y}(x,y)$.
#### (*Independence*) If $X,Y$ are independent, then $p_{X,Y}(x,y)=p_{X}(x) \cdot p_{Y}(y)$ and $p_{X \mid Y}(x \mid y)=p_{X}(x)$.
