>[!note] Joints
>Mechanical joints constrain motion to one-parameter subgroups of $SE(3)$, meaning each joint corresponds to a fixed twist $\xi$ with a scalar displacement $\theta$: 
>- The simplest case is the **revolute joint**, which produces pure rotation about a fixed axis. Its twist has $\omega\neq0$ and zero pitch; if the axis passes through a point $q$, the linear component satisfies $v=-\omega\times q=\omega \times (p - q)$, per the laws of rotational kinematics. 
>- A **prismatic joint** produces pure translation along a fixed direction, characterized by $\omega=0$ and $v$ equal to the direction of motion. 
>- More generally, a **helical (screw) joint** combines rotation about an axis with translation along the same axis, corresponding to a twist with finite pitch.
>
> These joint types represent the fundamental building blocks of mechanisms. Because each joint generates motion through an exponential map $\exp(\hat{\xi}\theta)$, complex mechanisms can be modeled as products of exponentials of individual twists. This perspective unifies rigid body motion, infinitesimal generators, and mechanical design within the structure of $SE(3)$.
