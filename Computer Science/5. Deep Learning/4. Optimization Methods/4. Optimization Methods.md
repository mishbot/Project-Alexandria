>[!note] Optimization Methods
>This section covers how deep networks are set up and trained, in the order CS 182 teaches it. The optimizers themselves work on any objective, so their notes live in [[6.4. Gradient Descent|Mathematical Optimization]]. This folder holds the parts that only make sense for networks:
>
>- **Starting point**: [[4.1. Weight Initialization|weight initialization]] sets the scale of the weights so that signals neither explode nor vanish.
>- **Optimizers for weight matrices**: [[4.2. Muon|Muon]] orthogonalizes each update with Newton-Schulz iterations.
>- **Scaling with width**: [[4.3. Maximal Update Parameterization|μP]] sets initialization and learning rates per layer so that tuned hyperparameters carry over to wider networks.
>- **Regularization**: [[4.4. Neural Network Regularization|regularizers]] reduce overfitting, with separate notes on [[4.4.1. Dropout|dropout]] and [[4.4.2. Data Augmentation|data augmentation]].
>- **Normalization**: [[4.5. Neural Network Normalization|normalization layers]] keep activations at a stable scale, with [[4.5.1. Batch Normalization|batch]], [[4.5.2. Layer Normalization|layer]] and [[4.5.3. Group Normalization|group normalization]].

# Prerequisites

- [[2. Neural Networks]], for layers, activations and backpropagation.
- [[6.1. Least Squares]], the model problem the course uses to analyze optimizers.
- [[3.2.1. Ridge Regression]], for ridge regression.
- [[13.1 Singular Value Decomposition]], for the spectral view of updates in Muon and μP.

# Contents

Optimizers, in the Mathematical Optimization folder:

- [[6.4. Gradient Descent]]: the basic update and its behavior on least squares.
- [[6.4.1. Stochastic Gradient Descent]]: minibatch gradients and their noise.
- [[6.4.2. Momentum]]: averaging past gradients.
- [[6.4.3. Adam]]: per-coordinate step sizes, AdamW and sign descent.
- [[6.4.4. Steepest Descent Under a Norm]]: the norm choice behind normalized GD, sign descent and spectral steps. **Not taught:** the $\ell_1$ ball and the Shampoo update rule.

This folder:

- [[4.1. Weight Initialization]]: variance propagation, zero initialization and symmetry, Xavier, He and Glorot, bias initialization, ReLU elbow placement. **Not taught:** uniform variants and PyTorch defaults, the backward-pass argument, zero-initialized output and residual layers.
- [[4.2. Muon]]: the Muon update, Newton-Schulz iteration, the cubic and quintic polynomials. **Not taught:** cost counts and which parameters use Muon.
- [[4.3. Maximal Update Parameterization]]: RMS norm, the RMS→RMS norm, feature-learning conditions, learning-rate scaling with width, hyperparameter transfer. **Not taught:** random-matrix spectral norms, the spectral initialization rule, the lazy regime.
- [[4.4. Neural Network Regularization]]: weight decay, model averaging and an overview of the other regularizers.
- [[4.4.1. Dropout]]: random masking of hidden units, test-time scaling, and the equivalence with ridge regression.
- [[4.4.2. Data Augmentation]]: label-preserving transformations, mixing methods, and test-time augmentation.
- [[4.5. Neural Network Normalization]]: comparison hub for normalization layers, with a decision rule and a comparison table.
- [[4.5.1. Batch Normalization]]: statistics across the batch, test-time statistics, scale invariance, batch noise, and the backward pass.
- [[4.5.2. Layer Normalization]]: statistics within each example, used in transformers and RNNs.
- [[4.5.3. Group Normalization]]: statistics over groups of channels, with instance normalization as a special case.

# Recommended Path

Read [[4.1. Weight Initialization]] first. Then read [[6.4.4. Steepest Descent Under a Norm]], [[4.2. Muon]] and [[4.3. Maximal Update Parameterization]] together, since they share the RMS→RMS norm. Read [[4.5.1. Batch Normalization]] and [[4.4.1. Dropout]] with the CNN material.
