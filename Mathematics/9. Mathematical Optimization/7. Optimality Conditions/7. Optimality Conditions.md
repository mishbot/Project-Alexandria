>[!note] Optimality Conditions
>This section covers the calculus tests that locate and classify the optimum of a smooth function, and the tools that describe how the optimum moves when a parameter changes. [[7.1 Unconstrained Optimality Conditions]] treats problems on an open set. The first-order condition $\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^*) = \mathbf{0}$ finds the candidates, and the Sylvester criterion on the leading principal minors of the Hessian classifies them. For a concave function every critical point is a global maximum.
>
>[[7.2 Lagrange Multipliers]] adds equality constraints $h_j(\mathbf{x}) = 0$. The candidates are the critical points of the Lagrangian $\mathcal{L} = f - \sum_j \lambda_j h_j$. With two variables and one constraint, the sign of the bordered Hessian determinant classifies them. [[7.3 Envelope Theorem]] differentiates the optimal value $F(\mathbf{p})$. Only the direct effect of a parameter counts, so $dF/dp_i = \partial \mathcal{L}/\partial p_i$ at the optimum, and $dF/dc = \lambda$ for a constraint $h(\mathbf{x}) = c$. The response of the optimal choice itself comes from the [[2.5. Implicit Function Theorem]] applied to the first-order conditions.
>
>All constraints in this section are equalities. Inequality constraints, the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions, and convex duality are in [[4.2. Lagrangian Duality]]. The micro notes in [[6.2 Consumer Choice]] and [[6.3 Comparative Statics]] use these results throughout.

# Prerequisites

- [[3.6. Gradients]], for the first-order condition $\nabla f = \mathbf{0}$.
- [[3.7. Hessians]], for the second-order conditions.
- [[2.2 Convex Functions]], for convexity, concavity, and the tangent-plane inequality.
- [[2.5. Implicit Function Theorem]], for the derivation of the Lagrange conditions and for how optimal choices respond to parameters.

# Contents

- [[7.1 Unconstrained Optimality Conditions]]: first- and second-order conditions in one and several variables, corner solutions, the Sylvester criterion, saddle points, and concavity through the Hessian. **Not taught:** the tangent-plane proof that a critical point of a concave function is a global maximum.
- [[7.2 Lagrange Multipliers]]: equality constraints, the Lagrangian and its sign convention, the tangency condition, the bordered Hessian, and the meaning of $\lambda$.
- [[7.3 Envelope Theorem]]: the derivative of the optimal value, unconstrained and constrained, with $dF/dc = \lambda$ and its uses for indirect utility and the expenditure function.

# Recommended Path

Read the notes in order, since each one uses the one before it. With little time, focus on three skills. The first is the FOC and Sylvester check in 7.1. The second is setting up the Lagrangian and solving its first-order conditions in 7.2. The third is differentiating the Lagrangian with respect to a parameter in 7.3. The concavity test with permutations and the bordered Hessian have the most signs to track, so work through those examples.
