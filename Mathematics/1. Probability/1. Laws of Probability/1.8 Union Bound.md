>[!note] Union Bound
>For any events $A_1,\ldots,A_N \subseteq \Omega$ in the same probability space, the **union bound** states that the probability of the union of the events is at most the sum of their individual probabilities. Formally,
>$$\mathbb{P}\Big[\bigcup^{N}_{i=1}A_i\Big] \leq \sum^{N}_{i=1}\mathbb{P}[A_i].$$
# Intuition
Let's consider why the union bound is true:
1. In the case of [[1.2 Mutually Exclusive Events|mutually exclusive events]], their union is exactly equal to the sum of their marginal probabilities.
2. In the case of all other events, per the [[1.7 Inclusion-Exclusion Principle|inclusion-exclusion principle]], their union is less than their marginal probabilities.

There are no scenarios where the probability of the union exceeds the sum of the individual probabilities. This would imply the inclusion of probability mass outside the defined events, which violates the basic principles of both the union operation and probability as a whole. If you are still unsure, draw a Venn diagram and think about it!
