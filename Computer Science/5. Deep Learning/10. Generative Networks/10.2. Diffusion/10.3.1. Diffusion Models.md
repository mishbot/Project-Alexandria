>[!note] Denoising Diffusion
>A **denoising diffusion model** is a generative model that learns to reverse a gradual noising process. It defines a **forward diffusion** that transforms data $x_0$ into pure noise through a sequence of Gaussian transitions, and a **reverse denoising process** that reconstructs samples by learning the **score** (i.e., the gradient of the log-density of noisy data). The generative model is thus a learned approximation of the time-reversal of diffusion.
>
>The **forward process** $\{x_t\}_{t=0}^T$ is a Markov chain that incrementally adds Gaussian noise:
>$$q(x_t \mid x_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}\left(\sqrt{1-\beta_t}\, x_{t-1}, \; \beta_t I \right),$$
>where the **variance schedule** $\beta_1,\dots,\beta_T$ controls how rapidly signal is destroyed. Each step slightly shrinks the signal via $\sqrt{1-\beta_t}$ and adds isotropic noise $\beta_t I$, where $I$ is the identity matrix. Because each transition is linear-Gaussian, the marginal distribution **conditioned on the original clean data** $x_{0}$ has a closed form:
>$$q(x_t \mid x_0) = \mathcal{N}\left(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\, x_0,\; \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t }I \right),$$
>where $\bar\alpha_t = \prod_{s=1}^t (1-\beta_s)$. This formula expresses a **mixture of preserved signal** $(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t} x_0)$ and **accumulated noise** $(1-\bar\alpha_t)I$. As $t \to T$, nearly all signal disappears and $x_T \approx \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, yielding a **tractable prior distribution** for generative sampling.
>
>The generative model then defines a learned approximation to each reverse transition:
>$$p_\theta(x_{t-1} \mid x_t) = \mathcal{N}\left(\mu_\theta(x_t,t), \; \Sigma_t \right),$$
>where a neural network (often a U-Net or Transformer) predicts the mean $\mu_\theta(x_t, t)$ or equivalently the added noise $\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)$. 
>
>A **sampling procedure** generates new data by running this learned reverse process starting from pure Gaussian noise. Since the model cannot reconstruct a clean sample in one step, it denoises gradually: begin with pure noise $x_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)$ and iteratively apply the reverse transitions
>$$x_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I), \qquad x_{t-1} \sim p_\theta(x_{t-1} \mid x_t),\; t = T,\ldots,1,$$
>yielding $x_0$ as the generated sample.
>
>Diffusion models are **trained by predicting the noise** used in the forward process:
>$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{DM}} = \mathbb{E}_{x_0, \epsilon, t} \left[ \|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon,\, t)\|^2 \right],$$
>a simple regression objective that approximates $\nabla_{x_t} \log q(x_t)$, the **score** of the noisy data distribution.
