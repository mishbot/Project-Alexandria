>[!note] Hidden Markov Model
>A **Hidden Markov Model (HMM)** is a probabilistic model that can be viewed as a [[6.4.1. Markov Models|Markov Model]] whose states are **not directly observable**. The underlying chain of hidden variables $X_t$ evolves according to the same Markov property,
>$$P(X_t | X_{0}, X_{1}, \ldots, X_{t-1}) = P(X_t \mid X_{t-1}),$$
>but instead of observing each state $X_t$ itself, we only observe an **evidence variable** $E_t$ that depends probabilistically on that hidden state.
>
>The **hidden layer** forms a Markov Chain, while the **observation layer** adds an **emission process** that provides the update step
>$$P(E_t | X_{0:t}, E_{0:t-1}) = P(E_t \mid X_t).$$
>
>Each timestep thus contains two components:
>1. A transition model $P(X_{t+1} \mid X_t)$, governing how hidden states evolve, just like a Markov Chain.
>2. An **emission model** $P(E_t \mid X_t)$, governing how each hidden state generates observable evidence.
>
>Both models are assumed **stationary**, so their conditional tables do not change with $t$.
>
>The full process is a Markov Chain "wrapped" by an observation model. The resulting **joint distribution** over both hidden states and evidence variables factorizes as
>$$P(X_{0:n}, E_{0:n}) = P(X_0) P(E_0 \mid X_0) \prod_{i=0}^{n-1} P(X_{i+1} \mid X_i) P(E_{i+1} \mid X_{i+1}).$$
>
>This structure allows inference over hidden states using algorithms such as **forward-backward**, **Viterbi**, or **particle filtering**, all of which operate on the same transition dynamics as a Markov Chain but must also account for the uncertainty introduced by the hidden layer.
