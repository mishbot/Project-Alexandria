>[!note] Feature Selection
>**Feature selection** is the stage between [[4.1. Feature Detection|detection]] and [[4.3. Feature Description|description]] that culls the raw set of keypoints down to a smaller, better-distributed subset. Detectors like [[4.1.1. Harris Corner Detection|Harris]] or DoG often return many keypoints concentrated in high-texture regions (buildings, foliage) while leaving other regions empty. A well-distributed set of keypoints gives geometric estimators (homography, fundamental matrix) more leverage than a clustered set of the same size.

# Intuition
Raw detection responses are noisy — thousands of keypoints cluster on a brick wall while the plain sky beside it has none. Downstream [[4.4.1. RANSAC|RANSAC]] cares not just about how many inliers exist but where they are: four inliers well-spread across the image are better than forty inliers packed into one corner. Feature selection is the practical step that enforces spatial diversity and keeps the keypoint count manageable for matching.
