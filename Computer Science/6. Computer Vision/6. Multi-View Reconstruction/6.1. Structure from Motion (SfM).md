>[!note] Structure from Motion
>**Structure from Motion (SfM)** jointly recovers a 3D point cloud (**structure**) and the poses of the cameras that observed it (**motion**) from an unordered collection of $n \geq 2$ images with overlapping views. It is the general multi-view extension of [[5.3. Epipolar Geometry|two-view reconstruction]], and produces a **sparse** reconstruction (one 3D point per matched feature).
>
>Given images $I_1, \dots, I_n$ with known intrinsics $K$, SfM estimates:
>- Camera extrinsics $(R_i, T_i) \in SE(3)$ for each view $i$,
>- A set of 3D points $\{X_j\} \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ visible across multiple views.
>
>The standard **incremental SfM pipeline** proceeds as:
>1. **Feature detection and description** ([[4.3.2. SIFT|SIFT]] / ORB) across all images.
>2. **Pairwise feature matching** with [[4.4.1. RANSAC|RANSAC]] filtering of the [[5.3. Epipolar Geometry|epipolar constraint]] to reject outliers.
>3. **Initial pair selection**: pick two images with many matches and a wide baseline. Recover their relative pose from the essential matrix, then [[5.2. Stereo Geometry|triangulate]] matched points to seed the point cloud.
>4. **Incremental registration**: for each new image, find 2D-3D correspondences with existing points, then solve [[6.1. Structure from Motion (SfM)#Perspective-n-Point|Perspective-n-Point]] (PnP) to recover its pose.
>5. **Triangulate new points** visible in the registered image but not yet reconstructed.
>6. **Bundle adjustment** (global nonlinear refinement) to jointly optimize all poses and 3D points by minimizing **reprojection error**:
>$$E = \sum_{i,j} v_{ij}\,\| x_{ij} - \pi(K[R_i | T_i] X_j) \|^2,$$
>where $v_{ij} = 1$ if point $j$ is visible in camera $i$ and $\pi(\cdot)$ is the pinhole projection.
>
>Reconstruction is recovered **up to a global similarity transform** (rotation, translation, and scale) unless metric priors (e.g., known baseline or calibration object) anchor the absolute scale.

# Intuition
Two-view geometry gives the relative pose of one camera with respect to another — but it says nothing about a *third* view. SfM solves the problem of stitching many pairwise views into a single globally consistent reconstruction, much like how a panorama stitches overlapping images into a common frame.

The central idea is **incremental anchoring**: start with two views whose geometry is well-constrained (wide baseline, many matches), triangulate a seed point cloud, and then add each new image by finding 2D features that correspond to existing 3D points. Once a new image has enough 2D-3D correspondences, its pose is recovered by solving a minimal geometric problem (PnP), and the growing point cloud both supports and is extended by each new view.

The pipeline is a sequence of **locally linear** problems (SVD-based initial pair, PnP, DLT triangulation) interleaved with **global nonlinear** refinement (bundle adjustment) that corrects the drift accumulated by greedy incremental registration.

# Properties

## Pipeline Structure

#### *(Feature-based front end)* SfM begins with detector-descriptor features ([[4.3.2. SIFT|SIFT]], ORB, SuperPoint) matched pairwise across images.

The front end produces *tentative* correspondences. Descriptor ambiguity, repeating texture, and moving objects introduce outliers, so [[4.4.1. RANSAC|RANSAC]] is applied to each pair using the epipolar constraint as the geometric model: a correspondence $(x, x')$ is an inlier iff $(x')^T F x \approx 0$.

#### *(Initial pair selection)* The two seed images are chosen to maximize reconstructability: many inlier matches, but a sufficiently wide baseline.

A narrow-baseline pair has poorly conditioned triangulation (rays nearly parallel), while a very wide baseline has few matches. A common heuristic: pick the pair with the most inliers *subject to* a minimum median triangulation angle (e.g., 3°).

#### *(Incremental registration)* Each new image is registered by solving Perspective-n-Point for its pose, then triangulating new points.

Once a partial reconstruction exists, each new image $I_k$ is aligned by looking up 2D features in $I_k$ whose descriptors match 3D points already in the cloud. This gives 2D-3D pairs $(x, X)$, and the camera pose $[R_k | T_k]$ that projects each $X$ to its observed $x$ is recovered via PnP (see below).

## Perspective-n-Point

#### *(P3P — minimal solver)* Three 2D-3D correspondences give a closed-form solution with up to four geometric candidates.

Given three points $X_1, X_2, X_3$ seen at normalized image directions $\hat{x}_i = K^{-1}x_i$, the distances $d_i = \|X_i - C\|$ from the camera center $C$ satisfy three law-of-cosines equations in the known angles $\cos\theta_{ij} = \hat{x}_i \cdot \hat{x}_j$ and known 3D distances $\|X_i - X_j\|$. The resulting polynomial system has up to four real roots; a fourth correspondence disambiguates.

#### *(PnP with $n > 3$)* More correspondences give an overdetermined system solved by least squares, often refined nonlinearly.

With $n \geq 4$ points, a direct linear transform (DLT) estimates $[R|T]$ linearly by solving $A\,\text{vec}([R|T]) = 0$, then projects the result onto $SE(3)$ (e.g., via SVD of the rotation block). EPnP is a faster $O(n)$ variant. The DLT solution is typically refined by minimizing reprojection error with Levenberg-Marquardt.

#### *(RANSAC wrapping)* PnP runs inside RANSAC because 2D-3D matches still contain outliers.

Repeated minimal fits (P3P samples of three correspondences) followed by inlier counting reject descriptor-based mismatches and erroneous triangulations.

## Triangulation and Bundle Adjustment

#### *(Linear triangulation)* Given two or more cameras $P_i = K[R_i | T_i]$ observing the same 3D point, its homogeneous coordinates are recovered by stacking the cross-product constraints $x_i \times (P_i X) = 0$ into $AX = 0$.

Each view contributes two linearly independent rows (the third is dependent). The solution $X$ is the right singular vector of $A$ corresponding to the smallest singular value. Because noise makes rays non-intersecting, this gives a *least-squares* midpoint rather than a true intersection.

#### *(Reprojection error)* The gold-standard cost for jointly refining structure and motion is the sum of squared pixel distances between observed and reprojected features.

$E = \sum_{i,j} v_{ij}\|x_{ij} - \pi(P_i X_j)\|^2$, where $v_{ij}$ is the visibility indicator. This is the negative log-likelihood under i.i.d. Gaussian pixel noise and is the right objective for Gauss-Newton / Levenberg-Marquardt descent.

#### *(Bundle adjustment)* Joint nonlinear least squares over all camera poses $\{(R_i, T_i)\}$ and all 3D points $\{X_j\}$ minimizes reprojection error across the entire reconstruction.

The Jacobian has a characteristic **sparse block structure** (each residual depends on only one camera and one point) that the Schur complement exploits to solve the normal equations in $O(\text{views} \cdot \text{points})$ rather than cubically. Bundle adjustment runs periodically during incremental registration and once at the end.

## Reconstruction Ambiguities

#### *(Similarity gauge)* Without metric priors, the reconstruction is determined only up to a 7-DoF similarity: overall rotation, translation, and scale.

The reprojection error is invariant under $X_j \to sR X_j + T$ and $(R_i, T_i) \to (R_i R^T, T_i - sR_i R^T T / s)$. Fixing the world frame (e.g., world = first camera, unit baseline) removes this gauge freedom and is required for numerical stability.

#### *(Degenerate configurations)* Pure rotation, planar scenes, and collinear points all produce degenerate SfM problems.

Under pure camera rotation there is no parallax, triangulation fails, and only a [[3.4. Homographies|homography]] relates the views. For a planar scene, the essential matrix is not uniquely determined from matches — the correct model is again a homography. These cases must be detected and handled with alternative estimators.

## Practical Considerations

#### *(Drift accumulation)* Purely incremental registration accumulates error, especially along long sequences.

The solution is periodic bundle adjustment and, for video or loop-closing sequences, explicit loop-closure detection (a new image that matches an *old* part of the reconstruction), which provides a large-baseline correction.

#### *(Modern implementations)* COLMAP, OpenMVG, and Meshroom package the full pipeline with robust heuristics for pair selection, keyframing, and BA scheduling.

These systems operate on unordered internet photo collections and handle reconstructions with $10^4$–$10^5$ images and $10^7$ points.
