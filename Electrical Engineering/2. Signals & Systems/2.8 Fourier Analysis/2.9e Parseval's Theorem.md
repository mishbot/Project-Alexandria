>[!note] Parseval's Theorem
>Parseval's Theorem states that the total energy of any $p$-periodic discrete signal $x(n)$ in the *time domain* can be expressed as a linear combination of its period with its Fourier coefficients, such that
>$$\sum^{p-1}_{n=0}|x(n)|^2 = p\sum^{p-1}_{n=0}|X_n|^2,$$
>where the right hand side represents the total energy of $x(n)$ in the *frequency domain*.

# Intuition
For discrete-time, we can take the vector equivalent of $x(n)$ to be $\vec{x} \in \mathbb{C}^p$. We can then express $|x(n)|^2$ as the squared norm of vector $\vec{x}$, such that
$$\begin{align}
||\vec{x}||^2 &= \langle \vec{x}, \vec{x} \rangle \\
&= \vec{x}^T\vec{x}\textasteriskcentered \\
\end{align}$$
Applying the equation $\vec{x} = \Psi X$, we get
$$||\vec{x}||^2=(X^T\Psi^T)(\Psi\textasteriskcentered\\X\textasteriskcentered).$$
Since we already know that $\Psi^T\Psi\textasteriskcentered=pI$,
$$\begin{align}
||\vec{x}||^2 &= X^T(pI)X\textasteriskcentered \\
&= pX^TX\textasteriskcentered \\
&=p\langle X, X \rangle \\
&= p||X||^2.
\end{align}$$
Which is equivalent to $\sum^{p-1}_{n=0}|x(n)|^2 = p\sum^{p-1}_{n=0}|X_n|^2$ in the signal domain.
