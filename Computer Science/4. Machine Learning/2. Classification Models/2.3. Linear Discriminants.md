>[!note] Linear Discriminants
>Linear discriminant methods classify data by defining **linear decision boundaries** in the feature space. For two-class problems, the model assumes a linear function of the input vector, 
>$$y(x) = w^Tx + w_0,$$ where the sign or magnitude of $y(x)$ determines the class. The decision rule assigns $C_{1}$ if $y(x) \ge 0.5$, and $C_{2}$ otherwise.
>
>The boundary itself occurs when $y(\mathbf{x}) = 0.5$, forming a ($D - 1)$-dimensional surface that splits the input space into two regions.
>
>For any two points $x_{A}$ and $x_{B}$ lying on the decision surface,
>$$y(\mathbf{x}_A) = y(\mathbf{x}_B) = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \mathbf{w}^\top (\mathbf{x}_A - \mathbf{x}_B) = 0.$$
>This means that $w$ is **orthogonal** to every vector lying within the decision boundary. The vector $\mathbf{w}$ therefore gives the **orientation** of the surface, while the bias term $w_0$ gives its **location** relative to the origin. The perpendicular (normal) distance from the origin to the surface is
>$$\frac{|\mathbf{w}^\top \mathbf{x} + w_0|}{\|\mathbf{w}\|} = \frac{|w_0|}{\|\mathbf{w}\|}.$$
