>[!note] Harris Corner Detection
>**Harris corner detection** is a corner detection algorithm used to identify **corner points** in an image where the intensity changes significantly in **both directions** (i.e., where edges intersect or where there is strong local variation). These points are often **good features to track** or match between images.
>
>For each pixel $(x, y)$, consider a small window $W$ and compute the sum of squared differences (SSD) between the window and its shifted version:
>$$E(u,v) = \sum_{(x,y) \in W} w(x,y)\,[I(x+u, y+v) - I(x, y)]^2,$$
>where $w(x,y)$ is a Gaussian weighting function.
>
>Approximating with a Taylor expansion leads to
>$$E(u,v) \approx \begin{bmatrix} u & v \end{bmatrix} M \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \end{bmatrix},$$
>where $M = \sum_{(x,y) \in W} w(x,y) \begin{bmatrix} I_x^2 & I_x I_y \\ I_x I_y & I_y^2 \end{bmatrix}$ is the **second-moment (structure) matrix**.
>
>The eigenvalues $\lambda_{1}, \lambda_{2}$ of $M$ describe intensity variation, such that a point can be a **corner** (both $\lambda$ large), an **edge** (one $\lambda$ large), or a **flat region** (both $\lambda$ small).
>
>To avoid explicit eigenvalue computation, Harris proposed a **corner response function** $R$,
>$$R = \det(M) - k\,(\text{trace}(M))^2,$$
>such that pixels with large $R$ (positive and above threshold) are identified as corners. Here, $k$ is typically between **0.04–0.06**.
